## Original authoritative sources inspected (source scope matters)
1. **HSE MW1, CNC machining, COSHH Essentials, published March 2021**, original five-page PDF, pp. 1–3: https://www.hse.gov.uk/pubns/guidance/mw1.pdf . **DOC-CONFIRMED:** mist can escape after machining stops; enclosure + LEV, flow indicator and *breathing-zone* clearance before opening are distinct from stopping the spindle. HSE advises a clearance delay determined from the actual enclosure/extraction, not a universal fixed number. HSE says the delay may be implemented by machine program or timer **for occupational mist control**. This does **not** establish safety-rated guard unlocking, mechanical standstill, or permission to reach a hazard. MW1 p. 3 gives a UK-specific LEV thorough-examination interval of at least every 14 months; not a universal US requirement.
2. **HSE MW2, Control of skin risks during machining, March 2021**, original four-page PDF pp. 1–3: https://www.hse.gov.uk/pubns/guidance/mw2.pdf . **DOC-CONFIRMED:** MWF and contaminants/biocides can cause dermatitis; gloves may protect against chemical contact during suitable tasks but introduce **entanglement hazards at moving machinery**. Guarded tooling, non-hand swarf removal, correct PPE for *stopped/isolated* cleaning, and skin-care measures have distinct roles. Barrier cream is not a liquid glove.
3. **HSE MW5, Managing fluid quality, March 2021**, original five-page PDF pp. 1–2: https://www.hse.gov.uk/pubns/guidance/mw5.pdf . **DOC-CONFIRMED:** water-mix MWF can become bacterially contaminated; concentration, pH, tramp oil, temperature, flow/dead legs and microbiological dip slides are separate quality indicators. MW5 recommends weekly dip slides unless documented management justifies another interval; results ≥10^4 CFU/ml indicate growth; ≥10^6 CFU/ml indicate poor control requiring prompt action, normally drain/clean or equivalent. These are **HSE water-mix management guidance**, not a universal medical infection threshold, a US PEL, or a valid automatic safety-stop threshold. Do not add biocide before good-practice correction; use supplier-approved doses.
4. **HSE Bacterial contamination**, official page updated 2024-12-04: https://www.hse.gov.uk/metalworking/bacterial.htm . Confirms water-containing systems need direct contamination checks together with concentration and pH; a clear-looking sump or a single pH reading cannot prove microbiological control.
5. **NIOSH 2008 Science Bulletin, Preventing Health Hazards from Metal Working Fluids**, directly inspected: https://www.cdc.gov/niosh/bulletin/2008/metal-working-fluids.html . **DOC-CONFIRMED:** the **1998 NIOSH recommended** MWF aerosol limits are 0.4 mg/m³ *thoracic particulate* or 0.5 mg/m³ *total particulate* TWA up to 10 h/day in a 40 h week. They are **recommendations, not a newly invented OSHA MWF PEL**. NIOSH notes that adverse effects have occurred below RELs and that skin exposure has no analogous numerical REL. Its historical 1998–2006 HHE observations cannot be projected to a selected machine.
6. **OSHA Metalworking Fluids—Evaluating Exposure**, official indexed page (direct page retrieval 403): https://www.osha.gov/metalworking-fluids/exposure-evaluation . **DOC-CONFIRMED, indexed-only:** SDS/product selection, mist-minimizing delivery, splash guards, LEV, fluid management, worker symptoms, and sampling methods are recommended. No claim that this best-practices page alone creates a new binding MWF-specific PEL.
7. **HSE engineering FAQ**, directly inspected: https://www.hse.gov.uk/engineering/faqs.htm . **DOC-CONFIRMED:** uncontrolled compressed-air blowdown adds swarf/eye, skin, mist and noise hazards; HSE recommends vacuum/absorbent/low-pressure alternatives, or extracted enclosure and reduced pressure if no alternative. HSE's 30 psi example is a *cleaning guide*, **not** a universal safety pressure or machine limit.

## Failure-path analysis and authority separation
| Initiating condition | Causal consequence | Misleading green indication | Independent evidence or design response |
|---|---|---|---|
| Spindle stops; operator opens enclosure immediately | Residual aerosol reaches breathing zone | CNC M5 complete / door unlocked | Actual enclosure clearance/LEV performance, occupational-hygiene review; *separately* safe physical spindle stop and guard/access protection |
| Extraction motor commanded ON, duct blocked/filter loaded | Inadequate capture despite command | LinuxCNC M-code, fan contactor, filter element rating | Verified capture/flow, maintenance and appropriate exposure measurement; a sensor alone is not exposure proof |
| Water-mix sump stagnates or develops dead leg | Biofilm/microbial growth and exposure | Clear appearance or nominal pH | Fluid-quality trend + appropriate microbiological testing and qualified action |
| Technician adds extra biocide to mask odor | Potential skin/chemical exposure, underlying flow issue persists | Odor temporarily improves | Supplier-compatible concentration, process correction, trained handling; no ad hoc dose |
| Operator uses gloves while reaching near rotating work | Entanglement despite reduced skin contact | PPE checkbox green | Eliminate reach during motion; separate stationary cleaning PPE from running-machine procedures |
| Compressed-air blowdown outside enclosure | Aerosol, flying chips, noise, skin contact | Part looks clean | Safer alternative and effective capture; independent eye/skin and noise controls |

**AUTH:** LinuxCNC/HAL/ordinary FPGA may sequence MWF pump, extractor, door-delay notification and maintenance reminders, but these are ordinary process/exposure controls unless the actual safety function has been independently designed and validated. A mist-clearance timer is **not** a protective stop or guard-lock authorization by default. **PHY:** motion/guard/energy safety, airborne exposure control and fluid/skin protection require **different witnesses**. Do not use the aerosol REL to set a guard-unlock delay, or a coolant dip-slide threshold to claim motion safety.

## Practical human factors and verification boundaries
Make a reliable extractor easy to check, a sump sample port reachable without reaching rotating parts, swarf removal feasible without compressed air, and guards simple to reinstall. A worker who must bypass the enclosure to clean every part exposes a **design defect**, not a reason to waive controls. Do not invent a universal mist-clearance time, extraction rate, microbe threshold for individual health, guard performance level, or air concentration for a selected machine. Any actual exposure sampling requires a qualified occupational hygienist and representative operating conditions. UK COSHH/14-month TExT provisions do not automatically apply to a US installation.

# 4400-IMD-01 — Ungrounded control circuits: first insulation fault, second fault and wrong monitor variant
**2026-10-26 | distinct 4400 safety branch | original Bender manufacturer/OSHA research | NON-GRADUATED**

## Why this is a new branch
RCD-01/02 studied *residual-current interruption* in particular grounded/protected supply architectures. An **unearthed/IT control circuit** has a different fault-management strategy: detect deteriorating insulation, preserve defined operation where appropriate, and repair before a second fault can create a hazardous current path. This branch does not assert that OpenPressBrake has an IT circuit or that an IMD is a personnel-safety final element. The safety index had no prior first/second insulation-fault or IMD-specific module. PANEL-01 and BOND-01 remain prerequisites.

## Mechanism / causal sequence (conditional INFERENCE)
1. In an appropriately designed IT circuit, a *first* insulation fault to earth may not create the same high-current clearing path as in a grounded system; the machine may continue running **only where that is part of the qualified design**. An IMD is intended to detect deterioration and alert for action.
2. If the first fault remains uncorrected, a second fault from another conductor to earth can create a more dangerous conductive path or control-state ambiguity. Actual current, protective operation and possible unintended motion are **UNKNOWN** without the circuit topology and component failure behavior; do not invent a fault truth table.
3. An alarm ignored for weeks, wrong IMD variant, failed measurement connection, muted alarm relay, wrong threshold or stale HMI can leave the operator believing the circuit is healthy. Bender documents configurable alarms/self-test and connection monitoring for the named devices; **INFERENCE** explains why those features must be commissioned and maintained rather than assumed.
4. **Critical variant trap:** iso415R product page expressly limits its listed main-circuit application to **without VFDs**, while iso685 explicitly covers power conversion. A shared “ISOMETER” brand name does not qualify substitution.

## Four independent safety propositions
- **Electrical design:** exact grounded/ungrounded supply and return topology, applicable rules, conductor and protective device design, IMD compatibility and response/alarm behavior.
- **Normal monitoring:** LinuxCNC/HAL may display IMD values and faults with timestamps, but neither Modbus freshness nor a green lamp is personnel-safety authority.
- **Safety-related control:** any required protective response to first/second fault must be explicitly derived from the machine SRS and independently implemented/validated. Do not automatically stop a gravity-loaded ram on every alarm without examining the consequence of losing holding energy.
- **Physical isolation:** service requires proper isolation of all energy sources, stored-energy control and qualified verification; an IMD measurement or first-fault alarm does not replace lockout.

## Safer-by-default human factors
Provide a clear “INSULATION FAULT — REPAIR REQUIRED” indication that identifies the affected circuit and qualified repair route. Make alarm acknowledgement different from clearing the underlying fault, prevent silent persistent overrides, provide easily accessible approved replacement monitors, and recheck alarm wiring after panel changes. For critical circuits, make it easier to locate and repair the first fault than to live indefinitely with a muted alarm.

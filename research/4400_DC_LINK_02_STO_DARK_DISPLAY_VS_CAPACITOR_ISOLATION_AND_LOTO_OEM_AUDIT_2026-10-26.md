# 4400 DC-LINK-02 — STO, dark display, and capacitor energy are different propositions
**2026-10-26 | Original manufacturer/OSHA documentary audit | No energized work authorized**

## Novelty and scope
The safety-course index contains drive STO and maintenance isolation as separate foundations but no dedicated **DC-link capacitor / dark display / de-energization** field branch. This audit targets the unsafe shortcut: **drive reports STO, power is off and the HMI is dark; therefore the cabinet is electrically safe to touch**. That conclusion is false even when STO itself operates as intended. Relevant to VFD-driven mills/lathes/plasma/robot cells and possible press-brake auxiliary drives; it does **not** establish a VFD on OpenPressBrake.

## Original sources and exact access status
1. **Rockwell Automation PowerFlex 400 Frames D–H, Main Control Board Replacement Service Bulletin**, **22C-SB003A-EN-P, August 2008**, 12 pages, **directly inspected original PDF**, pp. 1 and 4: https://literature.rockwellautomation.com/idc/groups/literature/documents/sb/22c-sb003_-en-p.pdf . The bulletin requires lockout of incoming power, **five-minute wait for that exact drive family**, and **measured zero DC bus voltage at the documented DC-/DC+ or DC-/P2 terminals**. It explicitly says a dark LCD or LEDs **do not prove capacitor discharge**. **DOC-CONFIRMED exact product**, not a universal wait time or measurement point.
2. **Rockwell PowerFlex 520-series User Manual 520-UM001O-EN-E**, manufacturer-indexed original 2025 PDF: https://literature.rockwellautomation.com/idc/groups/literature/documents/um/520-um001_-en-e.pdf . Search-indexed warning says **three-minute wait**, then qualified verification of line AC, DC bus and DC-to-ground paths; display LEDs are not a safe-voltage witness. **DOC-CONFIRMED manufacturer-indexed excerpt only**; direct original 34 MB PDF rejected by retrieval tool. Do not transplant 520-series instructions to PowerFlex 400 or vice versa.
3. **ABB ACS880-31 Hardware Manual**, manufacturer-indexed original PDF: https://library.e.abb.com/public/b6f37b648d06455ebc0f7a1699ce15f5/EN_ACS880-31_HW_H.pdf . Indexed excerpt specifies disconnect **all possible sources**, lockout, wait **five minutes**, verify tester before/after, check input/output/DC voltages and warns permanent-magnet motors can be an energy source. It also warns that probing unconnected DC terminal screws can give misleading results. **DOC-CONFIRMED indexed excerpt only**; direct 32 MB PDF rejected. Exact model/installation manual required.
4. **Siemens SINAMICS Safety Integrated**, original manufacturer product explanation: https://www.siemens.com/de-de/products/sinamics/safety-integrated/ (manufacturer-indexed original page; direct fetch returned unsupported content type). It states **STO removes torque, not active braking**, and the **DC link remains charged** for rapid restart. **DOC-CONFIRMED manufacturer-indexed excerpt only**, not a universal stop/brake result.
5. **OSHA 29 CFR 1910.147**, direct official regulation: https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.147 , especially (b) energy isolating device definition, (d)(5) stored energy, (d)(6) verification and (e) release. An ordinary control button is not an energy-isolating device. **DOC-CONFIRMED official regulation** within its applicable US workplace scope.
6. **OSHA December 12, 2012 interpretation** on LED indicators and energy isolation: https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/standardinterpretations/2012-12-12 . OSHA says **LED-only indication does not satisfy affirmative isolation/de-energization verification** under 1910.147(d)(6). **DOC-CONFIRMED official interpretation**, not a device-specific voltage test procedure.

## Four separate propositions (do not merge)
| Claim | What it can establish | What it cannot establish |
|---|---|---|
| LinuxCNC motion disabled / E-stop software state | Ordinary controller request or reported state | Independently interrupted motor energy, physical stop, electrical isolation |
| Drive STO active | Selected drive's validated torque-generation prevention function | DC bus discharged, no electrical shock, motor standstill, gravity holding |
| Mains disconnect locked | Isolation of the **identified** supply path, when properly implemented | All auxiliary/backfeed sources absent, bus discharged, trapped mechanical/hydraulic energy removed |
| Verified electrical de-energization by qualified person | Actual measured electrical proposition at **identified** hazardous nodes with suitable tester and exact OEM instructions | Permanent proof against reaccumulation, gravity load safety or all untested circuits |

**DOC-CONFIRMED:** The Rockwell 400 original requires measured zero at its specified DC bus terminals after waiting; display darkness is explicitly rejected. The Rockwell 520 indexed excerpt differs (**3 min** vs **5 min**). This variation alone defeats a universal 'wait N minutes' curriculum rule. ABB indexed material adds all-source isolation and live-dead-live tester checking, with exact test-point caveats.

**INFERENCE:** Regenerative energy, permanent-magnet rotation, external DC buses, shared power modules, auxiliary supplies and a failed bleed resistor can complicate an actual machine's energy inventory; each must be evaluated only where installed. A timer or voltage display is useful information but must not replace the qualified verified-safe-work procedure. Do not infer an actual regenerative topology, discharge constant or zero-voltage time for OpenPressBrake.

## Failure-path / practical architecture
- Normal operation: ordinary LinuxCNC/FPGA sends run/stop; drive's selected safety input and independent safety system govern required torque safety; mechanical holding/guarding remains separately justified.
- Maintenance: identified energy-isolating disconnects and lockout → documented stored-energy waiting **for exact model** → qualified electrical worker's verified absence-of-voltage at appropriate OEM-specified points with proper equipment/procedures → separately restrain elevated loads and other stored energy. No public field card instructs unqualified users to open live enclosures or probe DC buses.
- Human factors: a clearly labeled disconnect, lockable access, accessible OEM manual, readable hazard labels and non-bypassable service process are cheaper than confusing 'green STO' with 'safe to touch'. A dark HMI should **not** be the shop's only energy-status cue.
- Change triggers: drive replacement, power-supply wiring change, common DC bus, permanent-magnet motor, UPS, regen resistor or enclosure modification invalidate prior energy-isolation assumptions pending qualified review.

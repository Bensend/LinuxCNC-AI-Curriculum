# 4400-SOLVENT-CLEAN-01 — cleaning solvent, residual vapor and independent machine-safe state
2026-10-27 | Original source audit | NON-GRADUATED; documentary evidence only.

## Novelty / prior-art check
Direct canonical safety-course index and SAFETY_COURSE_RESEARCH.md full-text semantic scans found **no dedicated solvent/degreasing/flammable cleaning vapor module**. Existing MWF-MIST, LASER-AIR, FLUID-FIRE, DUST and AIR-CLEAN-01 address different hazards or transfer surfaces. This module adds a **new flammable-solvent cleaning/ignition/exposure proposition**, not a claim that OpenPressBrake uses such solvents. No 1000–3000 reopening.

## Official source record and precise scope
1. **DOC-CONFIRMED — OSHA 29 CFR 1910.106(e)(2)(iv), (e)(6), (e)(9)**, https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.106 (read 2026-10-27). For the categories and covered industrial operations described, the standard addresses covered containers when not in use, prompt control of spills, ignition sources along possible vapor paths, safe transfer/dispensing, static/electrical/mechanical ignition sources, housekeeping and combustible waste in covered metal receptacles. **Do not** transfer a ventilation rate, electrical classification, or category-specific rule from one paragraph to all machine cleaning jobs; the actual chemical/operation and applicability must be determined.
2. **DOC-CONFIRMED — HSE 'Cleaning or degreasing substances including valeting'**, https://www.hse.gov.uk/mvr/topics/degreaser.htm (read 2026-10-27): cleaners may harm skin, eyes or lungs, cause narcotic effects, and some release easily ignited vapors. Compare SDSs, select least harmful product, prefer sealed proprietary cleaning equipment rather than open solvent buckets, and provide adequate ventilation. UK HSE guidance is engineering evidence, not universal US law.
3. **DOC-CONFIRMED — HSE 'About dangerous substances'**, https://www.hse.gov.uk/fireandexplosion/about.htm (read 2026-10-27): flammable cleaning solvents can create vapor-air ignition/explosion hazards. A described incident involved cellulose thinners spilled onto a worker's clothing; vapors ignited when the worker entered a nearby oven room, causing serious burns. This is a **published HSE illustrative incident**, not an OpenPressBrake event.
4. **DOC-CONFIRMED — HSE HSG140 (2015 second edition) publisher page**, https://www.hse.gov.uk/pUbns/books/hsg140.htm (read 2026-10-27): covers safe use/handling of flammable liquids and fire/explosion risk in general work. Only publisher summary examined; no uninspected normative clause asserted.
5. **DOC-CONFIRMED — Massachusetts government 'Water-Based Parts Cleaning'**, https://www.mass.gov/info-details/water-based-parts-cleaning (read 2026-10-27): recommends aqueous/microbial alternatives to volatile solvent parts washing and explains possible exposure, waste and cost benefits. **INFERENCE:** substitute when compatible with material/corrosion/process requirements. Do not claim every aqueous formulation is nonflammable or safe for energized electronics.
6. **DOC-CONFIRMED — OSHA 1910.147**, https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.147 (read 2026-10-27): unexpected machine energization/stored energy during exposed servicing requires appropriate hazardous-energy control within its scope. Chemical vapor/ignition exposure requires separate controls; E-stop and CNC state alone do not satisfy them.
7. **Scope exclusion example — OSHA shipyard solvent cleaning eTool**, https://www.osha.gov/etools/shipyard/ship-repair/surface-preparation/chemical-removers : shipyard-specific hot-work and confined-space rules must **not** be quoted as universal shop thresholds. This audit deliberately does not prescribe a universal LEL percentage, vapor-clearance time, electrical-area classification or ventilation CFM for a retrofit press brake.

## Causal hazard and authority separation
| Task state | Physical hazard | False reassurance | Needed physical/organizational witness |
|---|---|---|---|
| Solvent wipe after CNC motion stops | Vapor travels to hot motor/braking resistor/heater/relay | LinuxCNC M5 or E-stop indicator | Correct solvent/SDS, ignition inventory, actual deenergization/cooldown/ventilation as warranted |
| Open solvent bucket in enclosure | Inhalation, skin/eye exposure, vapor accumulation | Door open or fan request ON | Substitute closed/aqueous method where feasible; selected ventilation/exposure assessment |
| Spilled solvent on rag/clothing | Transfer of fuel to another room/ignition zone | Spill removed from machine surface | Spill control, contaminated-clothing procedure, appropriate waste container and emergency response |
| Solvent on machine before welding/cutting | Residual vapor, contaminated cavities | Workpiece looks dry | Qualified hot-work authorization and assessment of hidden spaces/ignition path |
| Exhaust fan turns OFF on E-stop | Potential vapor buildup versus ignition risks of fan | Normal output state | Hazard-specific fan/electrical/ignition architecture, no universal always-ON/OFF rule |
| Service of cleaner/washer | Trapped electrical/chemical/mechanical energy | Power button OFF | Actual isolation, draining/ventilation/verification under qualified procedure |

**INFERENCE:** independent physical chemical/ignition/exposure witnesses are required alongside motion/guard safety. The correct safe-state action for a fan depends on its suitability for the atmosphere and selected process; do not mandate running a non-rated fan in a potentially flammable environment. Do not equate absence of visible vapor with acceptable concentration.

## Practical anti-defeat pattern
Make a suitable low-hazard cleaner and sealed/contained cleaning station the **default within reach**; keep solvents labeled, controlled, and away from ignition; provide easy spill cleanup, compatible PPE, accessible ventilation, and a clear service-to-restart procedure. A difficult parts-washing workflow encourages workers to use a fast open bucket or aerosol spray inside a machine; treat that inconvenience as a design issue. Verify chemical compatibility with manufacturer and SDS before substitution.

# 4400-MWF-01 — CNC coolant-mist clearance is not a motion-safe door unlock (2026-10-29)

**Status:** direct original regulator/health guidance audit, NON-GRADUATED, no machine/exposure testing. **Machine scope:** mills, lathes, grinding and other CNC machines using metalworking fluids (MWF); not evidence that OpenPressBrake has such a coolant system. Canonical safety index/research/progress direct scan found no existing dedicated metalworking-fluid/mist-clearance module. Existing DUST/LEV, enclosure and interlock lessons are predecessors; this is a distinct occupational exposure/door-access hazard, not renewed 3000 specialization.

## Original source provenance

1. **UK Health and Safety Executive (HSE), COSHH Essentials MW1 CNC Machining, original 5-page PDF**, copyright imprint **Published 03/21 (March 2021)**, directly opened 2026-10-29; original PDF page 2 (zero-index 1) image inspected: https://www.hse.gov.uk/pubns/guidance/mw1.pdf . **DOC-CONFIRMED** MW1 printed pp.1–3: mist can escape through enclosure gaps and when doors open shortly after machining stops; use enclosure and local exhaust ventilation (LEV), a practical airflow indicator, and a delay based on actual mist clearance before opening. HSE expressly allows the mist delay to be implemented in the machine program **or** a timer, and suggests smoke/dust-lamp clearance checks. Its control advice is UK COSHH guidance, not a universal legally required numerical delay or a personnel-motion safety certification. PDF search-result crawl date is not publication date.
2. **HSE MW1 continued:** it distinguishes stopping coolant delivery while not machining from continuing circulation to prevent stagnation; extraction airflow/duct/filter condition must be checked, and compressed-air cleaning generates mist. HSE guidance calls for competent periodic LEV examination/testing in its UK context, and for fluid maintenance and exposure controls. No exact safe clearance time is supplied for a selected machine.
3. **NIOSH Criteria for a Recommended Standard: Occupational Exposure to Metalworking Fluids**, DHHS/NIOSH Publication 98-102, January 1998, official CDC archive directly opened: https://stacks.cdc.gov/view/cdc/11283 . **DOC-CONFIRMED:** recommended MWF aerosol exposure limits **0.4 mg/m³ thoracic mass** or **0.5 mg/m³ total particulate**, TWA up to 10 hours/day, 40 hours/week. This is a **NIOSH REL recommendation**, not an OSHA PEL, not a real-time mist sensor setpoint and not a machine-door release threshold. Skin and respiratory risk persists even with 'machine stopped'.
4. **OSHA Metalworking Fluids Best Practices Manual**, original regulator guidance directly searchable: https://www.osha.gov/metalworking-fluids/manual . Describes enclosure, mist collection, fluid contamination and respiratory/skin exposure; its mention of an advisory committee's proposed limit does not make that proposal an enacted general-industry PEL.
5. **HSE public enforcement notices, 2021/2022, indexed-only**: https://resources.hse.gov.uk/notices/notices/notice_details.asp?SF=CN&SV=311684090 and https://resources.hse.gov.uk/notices/notices/notice_details.asp?SF=CN&SV=312322615 . Indexed records describe broken CNC enclosure interlocks, absent LEV and door opening immediately after machining, but direct page fetch timed out. **COMMUNITY/REGULATOR-INDEXED ONLY**; no immutable full record verified and no claim about the user's machines.

## Safety-function decomposition: two independent hazard clocks

| Physical proposition | Why a green CNC state is insufficient | Authority and proof |
|---|---|---|
| **MOTION-SAFE ACCESS** | spindle/feed stop command or LinuxCNC idle status is not measured safe standstill or verified guard locking | machine-specific independent protective stop/guard interlock and physical stop evidence, per applicable risk assessment |
| **MIST-CLEAR ACCESS** | axes may be stationary while airborne aerosol remains | qualified LEV/enclosure, measured or justified clearance under real operating conditions, accessible airflow indicator and maintenance evidence |
| **EXTRACTION EFFECTIVE** | fan command bit is not airflow or captured mist | actual airflow/pressure/LEV performance and maintenance; no invented threshold |
| **FLUID SAFE TO HANDLE** | coolant pump OFF is not clean fluid or safe skin contact | fluid condition, contamination controls, cleaning, PPE and exposure review |

**Important nuance:** HSE permits a *normal program timer* as one possible implementation of **mist-clearance hygiene control** after adequate validation. That does **not** move the separate **personnel motion-stop/guard authority** into LinuxCNC or the ordinary FPGA. If a single door latch must satisfy both functions, the independent machine safety guard/stop must remain effective; any mist timer cannot shorten or bypass it. Conversely, an independently stopped spindle does not justify opening the door into uncleared mist. This is a function-by-function design choice, not a blanket statement that all environmental exposure timers must be safety-rated.

## Adversarial and human-factor failure paths

- **Cycle complete → door opens immediately:** operator is exposed to residual mist despite stationary axes; delayed access and verified extraction can be necessary. Do not invent a universal fixed number of seconds.
- **Fan command ON but filter plugged/duct detached:** HMI status is misleading; provide easy-to-read airflow indication, maintenance and competent LEV checks. A defective extraction system should lead to an exposure-control HOLD until corrected, without falsely asserting an automatically safety-rated shutdown.
- **Fast cycle encourages guard defeat:** design a reliable low-delay clearance workflow and convenient non-mist-generating part-cleaning alternative; repetitive nuisance waits invite defeat. Avoid defeating the separate motion interlock to solve an extraction problem.
- **Compressed-air blowoff with open door:** creates additional aerosol and ejects swarf; prefer enclosed cleaning/extraction or appropriate alternative. Do not use compressed air on skin/clothing.
- **Power loss / restart:** ventilation may stop while mist remains; ordinary CNC reset must not prove either airborne clearance or motion-safe access. Restore controls and assess actual conditions before access.
- **NIOSH REL misused as instantaneous threshold:** a TWA recommendation cannot be inserted into a HAL comparator and called a safety-rated interlock or real-time clearance proof.

## No-energy engineering worksheet

Record exact fluid/SDS and machining process; enclosure seams and door mode; LEV type, discharge/recirculation and airflow witness; filter and duct maintenance; coolant flow and blowoff; actual aerosol clearance observation method and qualified result; operator access timing; independent motion-safe guard path; reset/restart; role responsible for exposure review; applicable jurisdiction. **UNKNOWN** without selected-machine evidence: mist concentration, clearance seconds, LEV airflow, interlock integrity, worker exposure, and physical motion stopping. No physical smoke/mist experiments are authorized by this source audit.

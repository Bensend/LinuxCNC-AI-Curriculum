# 4400-APC-01 — Automatic pallet changer: premature position indication, reversed hydraulics, and option-dependent E-stop continuity
**2026-10-27 | original Haas OEM source study | NON-GRADUATED | NO physical test, selected machine, safety rating or runtime**

## Safety question and exact new mechanism
A CNC automatic pallet changer reports that its receiver is clamped or that its H-frame has reached position. Does that prove the pallet is mechanically seated, its hydraulic motion is in the intended direction, the operator load station is protected, and the optional station E-stop is integrated? **No.** Original Haas troubleshooting documentation describes a pallet **lowering before it reaches clamp position because a switch trips too early**, reversed clamp/unclamp behavior from reversed hydraulic lines or reversed sensors, and a seal failure that routes hydraulic fluid into the air-blast system. A separate **2026** installation manual documents an OEM service shorting block when the pallet pool is *absent*, which must not be mistaken for evidence that the pallet pool's installed E-stop/guard chain is present.

**Predecessor check:** searched canonical safety-course index, research plan, progress, design package and source filenames for pallet changer, H-frame, pallet clamp/receiver/pool; no dedicated module found. Existing CLAMP, CHUCK, ATC, HYD-INJ, CELL, GUARD, EDM/RESTART, RIG and TSC/TAB address generic retention, fluid power, access and service. **New delta** is the exact Haas **early switch due to rack geometry**, separate clamp and H-frame motion/position sensors, reversed hydraulic direction, two-media seal path and OEM *option-absent versus option-installed* E-stop continuity. Do not create generic clamp or E-stop exercises again.

## Manufacturer-specific sequence and its physical limitations
**DOC-CONFIRMED TG0108** describes a normal pallet change as checking clamped-to-receiver status; moving Z/B to zero; unclamping; hydraulically lifting, rotating and lowering the H-frame while air blast is on; then clamping again. The HPU monitors pressure and inhibits sequence advance after insufficient pressure. The manufacturer separately documents:
- **Pallet lowers before reaching clamp position**: a switch/bracket may be mispositioned. Haas revised an EC-400 gear rack with a notch and shortened the switch mounting block to avoid early sensor activation. **An input transition can occur before actual geometry is safe**; merely changing an I/O parameter cannot repair physical trip geometry.
- **Pallet clamp/unclamp operates in reverse**: reversed hydraulic hoses are one documented cause. Reversed clamp/unclamp input wiring is another. Both require selected topology inspection; the same symptom does not identify which.
- **Air blast carries hydraulic fluid**: damaged/incorrect seals in the receiver assembly can route hydraulic oil into the air-blast path. The manufacturer distinguishes an older receiver construction on some machines built before 2022-03-01 from later styles and offers a replacement assembly. This is **hydraulic-to-air cross-media contamination**, distinct from TSC/TAB coolant/air check-valve faults.
- **H-frame and clamp sensors are not the same physical witness**: up/down, full CW/CCW, pallet clamp/unclamp and HPU pressure are different observations. The OEM I/O table includes a context in which *no pallet is present* and both clamp/unclamp inputs read inactive. Consequently, treating a generic 0/0 input pair as universally invalid is **wrong**. A status combination must be interpreted with pallet presence, motion phase and exact OEM model.
- **Alarms/timeouts** (e.g. clamp state, up/down and rotation faults) diagnose ordinary sequencing. They are not independent proof of a mechanically retained pallet or safe human access.

**DOC-CONFIRMED 2026 Revision E installation:** The UMC pallet-pool service procedure includes an OEM shorting block during a defined **machine-without-pool** leveling/service configuration and later instructs removal and connection of the pallet station interface when installing the pool. It also describes different hydraulic quick-disconnect sexes for clamp versus unclamp to reduce hose reversal and labeled hose identification. The OEM notes service jumpers should be retained for future service. **Engineering distinction:** a correctly specified jumper in a deliberately absent-option service configuration is not automatically a rogue bypass; leaving an option-absent configuration while claiming an **installed** pallet-pool station E-stop/guard is an unresolved, potentially hazardous integration mismatch. No pinout, bypass recipe or instructions to energize with protective devices absent are supplied here.

## Four-layer safety architecture
1. **Ordinary CNC:** commands pallet change, clamps, lift, rotate, air blast and recovery. CNC/LinuxCNC/FPGA commands and I/O are not independently validated personnel safety.
2. **Diagnostics:** position switches, HPU pressure, phase timeout, pallet scheduler and operator indicators. Diagnostic truth is not physical retention.
3. **Safety-related controls:** selected, independently justified load-door protection, E-stop, motion inhibit and rearm path; exact required PL/SIL and installed devices **UNKNOWN**. Do not move this authority into ordinary FPGA for convenience.
4. **Physical mechanisms:** pallet locator teeth/receiver, clamps, hydraulic cylinders, mechanically supported loads, guarded pinch zones, lockable energy isolation, residual-pressure relief and physical restraint. No invented hydraulic truth table or stopping distance.

## Practical anti-defeat and minimum threshold
The simplest robust design makes it easy to see a **physical clamp engagement witness** separately from the control's “clamped” bit, prevents swapping clamp/unclamp hoses by keyed fittings, and provides accessible inspection/replacement of the actual rack/sensor/receiver variant. Keep the load-station door and E-stop wiring intact and easy to service. Use a documented *option-absent versus option-installed* wiring/configuration sign-off so a commissioning jumper cannot silently become the production architecture. A recovery screen should clearly show **UNKNOWN pallet position / NOT READY** until a qualified verification establishes the mechanical state and deliberate restart conditions.

**Do not operate with personnel in an unprotected pallet-transfer envelope or under an unverified suspended/partially clamped pallet.** Experimental operation requires a qualified isolated/remote plan, personnel outside the danger zone, and explicit residual-risk statement. Never perform live sensor or hydraulic line reversals as a public teaching exercise.

# 4400-RST-UVR-01 — Undervoltage-release delay, breaker anti-pumping and remote reset are not personnel-safe restart
**2026-10-28 | original manufacturer/publisher documentation | source-depth ADDENDUM to RST-01; NOT GRADUATED / NOT TESTED**

## Concrete safety question
A machine loses some control power, then supply returns while a normal cycle demand or remote breaker-closing command is retained. Does an MN undervoltage release guarantee immediate interruption? Does mechanical *anti-pumping* guarantee no hazardous restart? Can automatic fault reset or remote reset restore electrical closing eligibility without a qualified area-clear/fresh machine-start check? The answer depends on the **exact selected device, optional delay, breaker/closing circuit, safety architecture and final energy paths**. Nothing here identifies the installed OpenPressBrake electrical equipment.

## Exact source findings
**DOC-CONFIRMED — NSXm MN versus MX, DOCA0185EN-01:** MN trips below **0.35 Un**; in the **0.35–0.70 Un** band it *may* trip, not deterministically; above **0.70 Un** it does not trip from undervoltage; reclosure becomes possible at **0.85 Un**. The optional MN delay module can suppress transient-dip tripping for **up to three seconds**. MX is a separately powered **shunt trip** commanded by applied voltage, not equivalent to a loss-of-voltage-operated MN. The OEM recommends periodic accessory testing (example every six months) for that product. **Do not transplant these values, delay or inspection interval to another breaker, safety function or installed machine.**

**DOC-CONFIRMED — MTZ2/3 MN/MX/XF, DOCA0101EN-09:** MN with absent supply prevents breaker closure; when supply returns, *closing becomes enabled*, not automatically commanded. MX opens on a powered order; maintained MX opening holds OFF in the specified standard accessory. XF is a **powered closing release** when the spring is charged. The **PF ready-to-close** indication concerns the breaker's own conditions (open, spring charged, no maintained opening order), not an independent personnel-safe area or hydraulic state.

**DOC-CONFIRMED — MTZ2/3 mechanical anti-pumping:** with simultaneous maintained open and close orders, the mechanism keeps the main contacts open; after a trip/open, the close order must be removed and reapplied to close. This prevents repeated open/close cycling, **not** unexpected energization from a later fresh *electrical* closing command generated by a controller reboot, relay bounce or human action. The latter scenarios are **INFERENCE / selected-machine UNKNOWN** and require proof, not an assumed outcome.

**DOC-CONFIRMED — MTZ RES/RAR and MCH:** RES can reset fault-trip indication and make the breaker **eligible** to close; RAR permits closing after trip without the blue reset-button action. The optional MCH can recharge the closing spring; XF and a separate closing order are needed for remote closing in the documented configurations. Reset eligibility, spring charge, PF ready-to-close, actual main-contact closure, normal motor/valve actuation and physical hazard control are **different observations**.

**DOC-CONFIRMED — standard scope only:** ISO 14118 covers unexpected startup from electrical/hydraulic/pneumatic power, gravity, springs and external influences; it does not assign machine-specific PL/SIL. IEC 60204-1:2016+A1:2021 covers machine electrical equipment including control-circuit protection; paid normative clauses were not inspected. Neither publisher scope proves an OEM example meets OpenPressBrake requirements.

## Failure-path architecture and negative claims
| Trigger / misleading indicator | Mechanism / source status | Required independent discriminator |
|---|---|---|
| Brief control-voltage dip, MN time delay installed | OEM documents a **deliberate ride-through option**; duration/selected accessory unknown | Identify actual accessory and wiring, protective stop-time requirement, voltage rails and qualified stop/hold witness. |
| Voltage falls in NSXm 0.35–0.70 Un band | OEM explicitly says trip uncertain in this range | Do not infer deterministic trip from a threshold guessed from nominal 24 V or HMI undervoltage bit. |
| MX trip command supply itself lost | MX is **powered-to-trip**; consequence depends on all actual supplies/circuit topology (**INFERENCE**) | Trace the exact loss-of-power final-element path; no assertion that a selected breaker remains closed. |
| Anti-pumping mechanism and remote close both present | OEM anti-pumping prevents repeated pumping while opening and closing demands coexist | Prove separate **fresh safety rearm** and **new normal start**, and no retained/recreated XF close demand after fault. |
| RES/RAR reset; PF shows ready | OEM says reset/ready makes breaker closable; does not prove physical safe state | Observe actual breaker main contacts and independent final elements, Y1/Y2 stop/hold, guard/occupancy and energy isolation. |
| Breaker open; hydraulic ram gravity-loaded | Electrical open state alone is not gravity restraint (**INFERENCE**) | Qualified independent hydraulic/mechanical holding and pressure-zone witness; service LOTO remains separate. |

## Safer-by-default field architecture (proposal, NOT selected design)
Make power/guard/fault restoration **ineligible for automatic machine-cycle start**. Keep qualified independent safety reset, contactor/valve safety outputs, physical holding, and ordinary LinuxCNC cycle-start separate. Make the breaker trip/close status and accessory model obvious to the technician; do not bury an MN delay or RAR option behind a generic “power OK” HMI lamp. Do not wire a convenient remote reset so it also issues an XF close and a retained motion command. For service, use qualified energy isolation and positive restraint, not MN/MX trip, breaker remote reset or a software OFF indication.

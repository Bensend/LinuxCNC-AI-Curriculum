# 4400-CHIP-03 — Haas conveyor door-open unexpected start: exact firmware and control-family evidence
**2026-10-27 | Documentary source delta to CHIP-01/02 | NON-GRADUATED | NO MACHINE TEST**

## Engineering question and novelty check
Can a technician assume a CNC chip auger/conveyor is prevented from restarting merely because the machining enclosure door is open, or because a software update has reportedly corrected an unintended restart? **No general assumption is justified.** This is a *version-specific original-OEM defect and control-family discriminator*, not a newly discovered generic conveyor hazard. Before selection, read [CHIP-01 source audit](4400_HAAS_CHIP_CONVEYOR_PERIODIC_RESTART_REVERSAL_AND_CLEANOUT_OEM_AUDIT_2026-10-25.md), [CHIP-01 technician card](../safety-course/4400_CHIP_01_TECHNICIAN_DECISION_CARD_AND_BLANK_WITNESS_2026-10-25.md) and CHIP-02 LinuxCNC M31/M33 portability audit linked from the [canonical index](../safety-course/SAFETY_COURSE_INDEX.md). CHIP-01 already teaches Settings 114/115 periodic restart, jam reversal, exposed chute/nip and auxiliary isolation; this addendum supplies **exact controller-version/door-rule fault evidence** previously missing.

## Direct original manufacturer evidence (DOC-CONFIRMED)
1. Haas, *Chip Auger — Service Manual*, online troubleshooting table, accessed 2026-10-27: https://haascnc.com/service/online-manuals/chip-and-coolant---service-manual/chip-auger . Symptom: chip auger/conveyor **may start without operator action even with doors open**. The table associates this with **software 100.17.000.1016** and recommends **100.17.000.2030 or greater**. The same row distinguishes **Classic Haas Control** (operator may restart conveyor with door open) from **NGC machines updated after 2018-10-10**, which the OEM says have stricter door rules. This is the OEM's product-family claim, **not** an independently witnessed machine test or universal door interlock.
2. Haas, *Conveyor System — Troubleshooting Guide TG0053*, original manufacturer page, accessed 2026-10-27: https://haascnc.com/service/troubleshooting-and-how-to/troubleshooting/conveyor-system---troubleshooting-guide.alarm%3Dngc_000-0000 . The OEM separately documents a pre-100.17.000.2030 behavior where **M31 re-starts the conveyor timer when the conveyor is already on**. It says corrected software does not restart an already-running conveyor's *off-interval* merely upon encountering M31; the current interval continues. This is a distinct state-machine/command defect from the door-open spontaneous start.
3. Haas, *Chip Conveyor* technical product page, accessed 2026-10-27: https://haascnc.com/reference-container/Technical-Details/Options/Chip-and-Coolant_Management/conveyor.html . **Settings 114/115** still describe intentionally scheduled on/off cycling following an M31-enabled machining cycle. **M33/CHIP STOP** are ordinary control operations, not electrical isolation or safety certification.
4. Haas, *VR Series Operator's Manual Supplement*, **June 2001**, original manufacturer-hosted PDF: https://www.haascnc.com/content/dam/haascnc/en/service/manual/supplement/english---vr-series-operator%27s-manual-supplement---2001.pdf . §4.12 documents automatic jam reversal, M31/M32/M33 and door-open stop on a legacy machine with safety circuit. This is a **different generation**, not proof that all Classic Haas/NGC controls share a door rule.
5. OSHA 29 CFR 1910.147, original regulation: https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.147 . Servicing/maintenance expressly includes cleaning and unjamming when hazardous unexpected energization is possible; §§(c)–(d) require an applicable energy-control program, isolation and verification. Scope/exceptions require case-specific review. A software update or door bit is **not** a lockable energy-isolating device.

The OEM's “after October 10th, 2018” is its stated NGC update applicability boundary, not evidence of this page's publication date. ## Important non-contradiction: three different restart mechanisms
| Mechanism | Manufacturer evidence | Do not infer |
|---|---|---|
| **A — defective door-open uncommanded start** | OEM lists 100.17.000.1016 symptom and recommends 100.17.000.2030+ | That all Haas generations are vulnerable, or that every later machine is validated safe. |
| **B — repeated M31 timer restart during an already active conveyor cycle** | OEM says pre-100.17.000.2030 behavior was corrected | That M31 is a protective stop, or that updated control never cycles a conveyor. |
| **C — intentional periodic start after M31** | Settings 114/115 explicitly schedule cycling during machining | That the phrase “never restarts automatically” in the defect table abolishes normal scheduled operation. |

The phrase “never restarts automatically” in the troubleshooting correction is **context-bound to the defect row**. Read as a universal statement it conflicts with the manufacturer's own current scheduled-cycle product description. Do not silently choose one and erase the other. Obtain exact machine/controller/option/software context and qualified evidence for the actual physical protection claim.

## Safety authority and failure-path analysis
- **Normal CNC control:** M31, M33, pendant FWD/REV/STOP, settings and software revision govern *operational* conveyor sequencing. An ordinary software fix may remove one unsafe command path, but does not itself establish the machine-level required safety performance.
- **Diagnostics:** firmware inventory, door-state inputs, alarm/overcurrent traces and conveyor command history help identify exposure; none independently prove the motor cannot be energized, the belt is at rest, or an external nip is inaccessible.
- **Safety-related control:** only a qualified design may credit a specified guard/interlock and independently evaluated final-element chain, with fault/restart and control-family assumptions verified. Do not grant LinuxCNC/Haas ordinary firmware independent personnel-safety authority.
- **Physical safe state:** isolate **all** relevant conveyor/auger/lift supplies, account for stored motion, secure accessible chute/nip and prove absence of hazardous energy before hands-on cleaning/unjamming under a qualified procedure. A main spindle stop, closed/open main door, or M33 alone is insufficient.
- **Human factors:** jam-clearing is frequent and tempting. Provide a captive/hinged guarded cleanout, accessible labelled lockable isolator, reachable external chip-bin service and simple fault display. An awkward guard that is routinely discarded is an architecture defect.

1. **Identity/version:** dated as-built machine and control family (Classic/NGC/other), chip option and installed firmware version/build, plus applicable manufacturer service bulletin/manual revision.
2. **Door-to-conveyor authority:** qualified drawing/review of whether and how the *selected* guard state inhibits **all** relevant conveyor motion, including independent external chute access, manual reverse, timer restart, overload reverse and after power restoration. Record separate normal-control and safety paths.
3. **Change/revalidation:** version-specific OEM update record and approved revalidation plan addressing A/B/C mechanisms; if upgrade unavailable, do not assume a software setting or door position supplies the missing safeguard.
4. **Physical proof:** only after G0/G1-style qualified readiness, an isolated, no-person-exposure verification plan with actual final-element state and safe restart evidence. **NOT RUN**; no live jam, intentional door-open motion, guard bypass, or exposed personnel test is proposed here.

## Independent regulator source-depth — interconnected isolation and actual cleaning exposure
- **DOC-CONFIRMED OSHA interpretation (2006-05-09):** https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/standardinterpretations/2006-05-09-1 states that a conveyor feeding a baler requiring a **second lock** does not meet the **single-lock** condition for the narrow documented-procedure exception in §1910.147(c)(4)(i). This is an example of *multiple energy-isolation points*, not a universal rule that every CNC conveyor always needs exactly two locks. It is not a new obligation beyond the regulation; scope and actual feeds must be reviewed.
- **DOC-CONFIRMED OSHA citation record, not an independently adjudicated root-cause finding:** https://www.osha.gov/sites/default/files/citations/BasicGrainProducts_98342_0215_12.pdf , inspection 98342, issued **2012-02-15**, PDF page **15/23**. OSHA recorded serious conveyor-related injury during cleaning on/around 2011-08-31 and alleged auger-cleaning amputation exposure on/around 2011-11-02 without lockout. This is **food-processing equipment, not a Haas CNC incident**. It corroborates the general cleaning/unguarded auxiliary-motion hazard, not the Haas software defect or any OpenPressBrake failure.

**Practical implication (INFERENCE):** include *interconnected auxiliary equipment* in the qualified energy-source inventory, and make the lockout path simple enough to use for routine chip clearing. The two regulator sources do not validate any particular firmware, guard, circuit or physical stop.

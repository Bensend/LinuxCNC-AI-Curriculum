# 4400-SETUP-01 — Ten adversarial PAPER cases

**PRACTICE ONLY / UNSCORED / NOT PHYSICAL TESTS.** For each: identify whether normal production, hands-on setup, or temporary test; which hazards remain; what evidence could qualify a narrow exception; what would prevent exposing a person; and which machine facts remain UNKNOWN.

1. Two-hand INCH works correctly, but a technician has fingers between a loosened upper die and the slide.
2. A key switch forces 5% speed. The hydraulic cylinder drifts with all control outputs OFF.
3. A die setter needs a 3 mm alignment stroke. A second worker remains inside the point of operation.
4. A technician calls a weekly complete die swap 'routine and repetitive' and assumes it is exempt from LOTO.
5. A production operator repositions an external coolant nozzle using a dedicated safe remote tool, without entering the hazard zone. Which exception questions matter?
6. A CNC controller asserts 'safety ready'; the upper die retaining clamp has been released.
7. Temporary test ends; a supervisor says re-locking is unnecessary because the HMI is in IDLE.
8. Two operators claim a two-hand control protects against a hydraulic hose rupture during setup.
9. A machine has a light curtain and a key-selected inch mode; its setup mode disables part of the curtain. What must be traced?
10. An engineer cites OSHA's 2005 hydraulic power press die-setting letter as direct proof of a press brake's required mechanical-block strength.

**Independent-evaluator rubric (not executed):** 0–2 each for exposure classification, exact narrow exception, residual gravity/hydraulic hazard, temporary-test isolation sequence, and source-scope/uncertainty discipline (/10). Novel held-out scenario and independent scoring remain OPEN.

Sources: OSHA 1910.147(a)(2)(ii), (f)(1), 2005-04-22, 2006-12-28, 2008-01-25 letters; original source audit linked in the module. Never treat these cases as a permit to run a machine.

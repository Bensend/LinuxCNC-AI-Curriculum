# 4400-HYD-01 extension — Twelve paper line-rupture/holding scenarios
**2026-10-24 | LEARNER-VISIBLE / NOT BLIND / NO LIVE FAILURE INJECTION**

For each case identify the physical hazard, whether the HAWE LB original manual actually supports the claim, the remaining UNKNOWN, the independent safety owner, a safe qualified verification method and a no-exposure restriction. Do not prescribe live hose rupture tests or copy product thresholds.

1. A mechanic says 'hose-burst valve installed, therefore a slow cylinder leak cannot move the ram.' Identify the flow-threshold assumption.
2. A tripped line-rupture valve has an OEM-listed orifice bore. Explain why 'tripped' does not prove zero flow.
3. A replacement LB has a similar body but a different response-flow code. What design and change-control review is needed?
4. A valve has the '-0' ordering code. Is it necessarily already set to the needed trip flow?
5. A line-rupture valve is installed near the pump, while the protected load and several hoses are downstream. What exact line segment must be identified?
6. A decompression transition trips protection during normal cycling. Why is removing the valve an unacceptable convenience fix?
7. A proposed added throttle is installed inside the very hose segment it is intended to protect. Which OEM design boundary is being violated?
8. A pressure sensor says zero upstream of a tripped valve, but a cylinder port and accumulator remain charged. Which physical evidence is missing?
9. The CNC's Y1/Y2 position graphs remain flat for 10 seconds after a trip. Does this establish long-term gravity hold?
10. A controller reports both safety outputs OFF, but an operator observes one-sided ram drift. Which propositions take precedence?
11. A service worker cannot read the valve code without removing a guard. Propose a safer maintenance/access arrangement.
12. The OpenPressBrake team proposes copying a HAWE example response-flow ratio. What selected-machine inputs and qualified review must precede any setting?

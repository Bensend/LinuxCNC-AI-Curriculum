## Causal architecture: at least three different physical systems
```text
ordinary bend planner / LinuxCNC or OEM HMI
  |--> Y1/Y2 commanded ram motion (normal control; physical gravity hold still separate)
  |--> V / crowning target -> motor drive -> wedge / bed shape (Haas/WILA motorized examples)
  |--> tool clamp command -> hydraulic clamping actuator (WILA exact examples)
  |--> diagnostic display: deflection / end-limit / motor phase / interlock
independent machine safety: guard / E-stop / mode / final elements -> verified hazardous-motion inhibit
physical protection: guards + mechanical retention + isolation + support + safe access
```
A press can also have **hydraulic Auto Crown** instead of motorized wedges (Cincinnati). The *name* “crowning” does not identify the energy type, final element, safety function or isolation point. The WILA catalog states hydraulic **clamping** even though crowning itself is motorized; “motor stopped” cannot prove die clamp retained or depressurized. A normal V-axis display cannot prove actual wedge position, motor direction, limit-chain integrity, bed geometry or guarding.

## Manufacturer-guidance tension and explicit limits
Haas §7.3 documents a high-risk **exceptional service intervention** while its §3.2 states never bypass safety devices and restricts cabinet access. The existence of both passages is **DOC-CONFIRMED**; the specific safe authorized procedure, wiring, cabinet electrical exposure, motor energy path, contactor function, safety-channel defeat (if any), personnel position and machine revision are **UNKNOWN**. Therefore neither “the OEM has certified manual contactor override as safe” nor “the OEM definitely wired a bypass” is supported. A qualified OEM/service safety review is required before any analogous real action. This is not an operator troubleshooting card.

The term “Hard-Stop Travel Safety Switch” is the **OEM's label**, not independent proof of ISO 13849 PL, redundancy, diagnostic coverage, category, or stopping time. The product web pages provide **no** selected-machine stop-distance or safety-integrity rating. The normal 2026 Haas HPB V-axis is not asserted to exist on OpenPressBrake. The exact WILA assemblies are illustrative variants only; their motor voltage, hydraulic clamping pressure and ton/m labels are **not** OpenPressBrake values.

## Proposed documentary acceptance / human factors
Under the existing restricted Stage A **A/B/C/D/E/F/G**, qualified reviewer first records whether crowning exists at all and its *actual* mechanism: motorized wedge, hydraulic cylinder, manual adjuster or none (**N/A only with dated evidence**). If present, link `BND/ENE/HAZ` for ram, crown, clamp and workpiece separately; `AUTH` for ordinary controller, independent safety final elements and service authorization; `PHY` for actual travel, tool retention, guard and gravity support; `VAL/PT` for separately justified future isolated witness; `CHG` for motor phase, switch, sensor, clamp, guard, tooling or service-procedure changes. Reuse existing Stage A and conditional Stage B cards; **no new owner questionnaire**.

Prefer a **guarded, externally accessible, positively identified recovery method** approved by the actual OEM, with clear “CROWNING POSITION UNKNOWN — SERVICE HOLD” state and no surprise restart. Do not normalize removing covers or manually forcing motor contactors to restore production. A hard-to-recover limit switch or omitted guard is a design/maintenance usability issue, not a reason to defeat the safeguard. This is an **INFERENCE / design recommendation**, not a demonstrated product feature.

## Frozen question and novelty

## Intended behavior (DOC-CONFIRMED)
- [Tagged G-code manual, G93/G94/G95](https://github.com/LinuxCNC/linuxcnc/blob/v2.9.10/docs/src/gcode/g-code.adoc): G95 interprets F as units per spindle revolution, requires `spindle.N.speed-in`, uses `$` to select the spindle, and is **not** threading; use G33/G76 for threading. Switching to G94/G95 requires a new F.
- [Tagged motion HAL man page](https://github.com/LinuxCNC/linuxcnc/blob/v2.9.10/docs/man/man9/motion.9#L652-L661): `spindle.M.speed-in` is **actual speed feedback in revolutions per second (RPS)**, whereas `spindle.M.speed-out` is desired RPM (lines 663–665) and `speed-cmd-rps` is commanded RPS (656–657). These names/units are not interchangeable.
- Same motion man page lines 592–596 describes the ordinary `spindle.M.at-speed` wait. It is not an independent speed monitor or a guard.

## Exact source chain (SOURCE-CONFIRMED)
1. **Selection / inherited-state trap:** `src/emc/rs274ngc/interp_execute.cc:256–264` sets `settings->active_spindle = 0` when processing a G93/G94/G95 feed-mode word, then overrides it only when that **same block** has a `$` word (range checked against `num_spindles`). Thus `G95 F...` without `$1` selects spindle **0**, even after a prior `G96 ... $1` or `M3 $1`. The code is a parser/setting claim, **not a runtime claim that an axis moved**.
2. **Feed mode / programmed-speed guard:** `interp_convert.cc:2246–2270` sets `UNITS_PER_REVOLUTION`, enqueues `SET_FEED_MODE(active_spindle,1)`, clears previous feed rate, and enqueues zero feed. `interp_convert.cc:4822–4828` checks nonzero F and `settings->speed[active_spindle] != 0` for G1. The latter is **interpreter programmed spindle-speed state**, not sampled HAL `speed-in`; a nonzero S alone cannot establish physical rotation.
3. **Canonical command:** `src/emc/task/emccanon.cc:518–530` uses `START_SPEED_FEED_SYNCH(canon.spindle_num, rate, 1)` in G95 mode. `emccanon.cc:1247–1255` emits `EMC_TRAJ_SET_SPINDLESYNC` with `velocity_mode=true`; `src/emc/task/emctaskmain.cc:1882–1885` passes this to `emcTrajSetSpindleSync`, and `src/emc/motion/command.c:989–991` passes the motion command to `tpSetSpindleSync`.
4. **Planner selection and segment inheritance:** `src/emc/tp/tp.c:3516–3528` chooses `TC_SYNC_VELOCITY`, records `uu_per_rev` and selected spindle; `tp.c:2047–2070` copies TP state into a new line segment via `tcSetupState`; `src/emc/tp/tc.c:674–680` copies `tp->synchronized` and `tp->uu_per_rev` to the segment.
5. **HAL-to-planner speed path:** `src/emc/motion/motion.c:733–736` declares `spindle.N.revs`, `speed-in`, `at-speed` HAL inputs and initializes `at-speed` TRUE; `src/emc/motion/control.c:346–360` samples the HAL `speed-in` directly into `spindleSpeedIn`. `src/emc/tp/tp.c:3459–3468` dispatches `TC_SYNC_VELOCITY` to `tpSyncVelocityMode`. `tp.c:2946–2963` sets `tc->target_vel = fabs(spindleSpeedIn) * tc->uu_per_rev` (adjusting for next-segment progress when blending). It uses **magnitude** of the supplied RPS, not an independently verified spindle encoder/standstill proposition.
6. **Ordinary at-speed is separate:** `tp.c:2890–2901` checks a segment's `atspeed` flag or first **position** sync, and `tp.c:2819–2828` can wait on `at_speed`. The G95 velocity calculation does not itself verify that `speed-in` came from a real sensor, has a plausible timestamp, matches spindle selection, or that `at-speed` is safety-rated. Do **not** claim that all G95 moves skip every at-speed wait: M3 and queued segment flags can cause an ordinary wait.

**Discriminator:** G95 is **velocity-based feed** from `speed-in`; G33/G76 position-synchronized operations use a different mechanism involving `revs`/index. Neither is a personnel-safety function. A valid-looking G95 feed can therefore be based on wrong-unit, stale, command-derived or wrong-spindle feedback unless the machine integration independently detects those faults.

## Version-sensitive historical source and community evidence
- [LinuxCNC issue #2530](https://github.com/LinuxCNC/linuxcnc/issues/2530) documents a 2023 two-spindle G95 failure on 2.8.3/2.8.4; the issue was closed **2023-07-08**. The [exact fix commit `c15ac50957f341a0ed35d8aa2b2757f108816104`](https://github.com/LinuxCNC/linuxcnc/commit/c15ac50957f341a0ed35d8aa2b2757f108816104) changes `interp_check.cc` to recognize `G95` in `block->g_modes[GM_FEED_MODE]`, rather than treating it as a motion code. The fixed logic is present at v2.9.10 `interp_check.cc:263–271`. **SOURCE-CONFIRMED fix**; no runtime retest or claim about all 2.8 builds.
- [LinuxCNC forum, “Feed per rev G95 way too fast”, October 20, 2025](https://forum.linuxcnc.org/49-basic-configuration/57514-feed-per-rev-g95-way-too-fast): a builder reported a severe fast-feed anomaly and later traced `spindle.0.speed-in` to `encoder.0.velocity-rpm` rather than `encoder.0.velocity`; correcting the connection reportedly resolved it. **COMMUNITY-REPORTED** outcome, not a laboratory or machine witness by this curriculum. The source/man-page RPS contract independently explains why an RPM value could produce a 60× numeric scale mismatch; actual physical feed is subject to trajectory/axis limits and cannot be asserted 60× without measurement.
- [LinuxCNC forum, June–July 2023 multi-spindle report](https://forum.linuxcnc.org/49-basic-configuration/49340-g95-feed-not-working-with-2nd-spindle) corroborates the issue history but does not establish current-tag behavior by itself.

## Practical safety allocation / human factors
- **Normal LinuxCNC/HAL:** select spindle, convert units, generate G95 feed and display valid/invalid *normal diagnostic* feedback. Machine integration should expose source, units, actual-versus-command provenance, sample age, signal loss, cross-spindle selection, plausibility and alarms. These are engineering recommendations, **not implemented safety logic**.
- **Independent safety:** guard/interlock/E-stop and safe-speed/stop functions (where required by risk assessment) must be assigned to selected, qualified safety hardware and final elements, not G95, `at-speed`, a PLC/HAL status bit, or the FPGA normal control. Physical spindle coast, chuck/workpiece containment, and tool approach require machine-specific proof.
- **Make correct wiring easy:** use distinct `actual_spindle_rps` versus `command_spindle_rpm` names; display RPM and RPS with units, provide an offline one-page signal-to-spindle table and a conspicuous UNKNOWN state for missing feedback. Never silently connect a commanded speed merely to make G95 “work”; any development-only surrogate must be isolated and visibly not for production.
- **Machine differences:** lathe G95 affects tool/feed/chuck hazards; mill/tap uses different spindle-synchronization requirements; press brake Y1/Y2 hydraulics are not made safe by G95; robot/plasma safety functions remain independent.

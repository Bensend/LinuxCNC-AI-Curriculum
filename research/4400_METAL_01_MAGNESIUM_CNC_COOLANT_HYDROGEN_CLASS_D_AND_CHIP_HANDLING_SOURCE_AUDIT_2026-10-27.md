# 4400-METAL-01 — Magnesium CNC machining: coolant chemistry, fines, hydrogen, fire agents and change control

**Study:** 2026-10-27 UTC. **Status:** source/documentary investigation, NON-GRADUATED; no selected machine or physical proof. **Scope:** conditional CNC mills, lathes, grinders, chip handling and associated collectors. **OpenPressBrake applicability UNKNOWN.** 1000/2000/F02/3000 remain GRADUATED/CLOSED; this does not reopen machine-specific 3000 work.

## Question and predeclared hypothesis

When a shop switches a normally nonreactive CNC job to magnesium alloy, can an ordinary LinuxCNC coolant ON signal, a running dust collector, or a general-purpose extinguisher prove that the chip/coolant/fire system is safe? **Prediction (INFERENCE): no.** Material-specific chip size, ignition behavior, coolant additives, hydrogen management, collector protection, firefighting agent and waste handling require separate qualified decisions. **Important falsifier:** evidence of a qualified, documented magnesium-specific wet-machining system would disprove a blanket claim that all water-mixed coolant is forbidden; this investigation therefore does **not** make that blanket claim.

## Causal/failure-path matrix (INFERENCE, not an installed fault)

| Change or fault | Attractive but false shortcut | Independent discriminator / safe architecture |
|---|---|---|
| Recipe changed from aluminum/steel to magnesium, M8 still works | “Coolant ON means compatible” | Approved alloy + process/coolant formulation/inhibitor + circulation/filtration/ventilation and chip-route review; ordinary CNC output is not proof. |
| Dry cutting creates fines, collector motor is ON | “Extraction commanded means dust is controlled” | Qualified magnesium-compatible capture/collection, duct isolation, housekeeping and ignition/fire response; no improvised shared wood/metal collector. |
| Wet chip conveyor/sump becomes stagnant after machine stop | “Cycle complete means hydrogen gone” | Hazard assessment includes idle, off-shift and power-loss gas/ventilation states in sump, chip transport and storage; physical evidence, not a timer guess. |
| Hot magnesium swarf ignites | “Any nearby water/CO2/ABC extinguisher is suitable” | Approved agent matched to metal/process, accessible from safe position, trained response and evacuation; **do not improvise water application**. Industrial engineered deluge exception is not hand-hose permission. |
| Small collector fire is repeatedly cleared and operation resumes | “It was only a nuisance trip” | Treat as abnormal hazard warning; investigate deposits, ignition source, collection design and revalidate before restarting. CSB Hayes Lemmerz documents consequence of normalization. |
| Fire/E-stop or mains loss changes fan/coolant state | “Stop spindle and turn everything OFF” | Separate protective motion stop, extraction/gas management, fire isolation, electrical/hydraulic isolation, and deliberate restart based on qualified physical system design. |

**Authority boundaries:** normal LinuxCNC/FPGA = recipe, M8 request, HMI and non-safety diagnostics; safety-related = independently qualified process-specific interlocks/ignition or fire system as risk assessment requires; physical = chip/dust collector, coolant chemistry, gas paths, extinguishing media, guards and isolation. No new safety authority is allocated to LinuxCNC.

## Practical human-factors implementation

Make the correct workflow easiest: visible **material-class change gate** before the first magnesium job; preapproved process/coolant/collector combinations with revision identity; clearly labeled dedicated chip containers; easy cleaning access without guard defeat; separate verified fire media located along a safe egress route; and clear “STOP / NO RESTART” response for unexplained hot chips, smoke, gas alarms, extraction loss or previous minor fires. Do not depend on an operator memorizing coolant chemistry or looking up a powder type during an emergency. A material code in G-code is an ordinary administrative cue, not a certified safety input. Maintenance and shift handoff must preserve the hazardous-material state even when the controller is rebooted.

# 4400-DSEDM-01 original OEM source audit — die-sinking electrical discharge machining
**2026-10-25 | source/documentary only | NOT a machine safety validation**

## Question frozen before source review
Can a generic CNC `M8` fluid request, an HMI fluid-level indicator, a power-on state, or an E-stop indication independently establish that an oil-dielectric die-sinking EDM machine is safe from fire, hazardous motion, or service energy? What exact OEM evidence changes the design?

3. **Makino UPV-3 wire EDM product page**, manufacturer-hosted: https://www.makino.com/en-us/machine-technology/machines/wire-edm/upv-3 . Explicitly identifies an **oil** dielectric wire EDM and integrated fire suppression. Thus **wire EDM != necessarily water dielectric**; machine process type alone is not a safe fluid assumption.
4. **OSHA 29 CFR 1910.147**, official: https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.147 . Maintenance hazardous-energy control context; do not treat the EDM tank as automatically governed by OSHA's *dipping/coating* 1910.125 without a separate scope determination.

This mirror was not used as a selected-machine authority.

## Critical safety architecture lesson
**INFERENCE, not a tested architecture:** Allocate separate functions to (a) normal axis/EDM pulse generator, tank-fill/flushing, process alarm and HMI; (b) selected and independently justified fire/level/temperature detection, discharge-power inhibit, guarded access and physical final elements; (c) independently justified fire suppression, egress/notification and emergency response; (d) lockable electrical/other energy isolation for servicing. A stop request from LinuxCNC/FPGA may be a useful *additional* ordinary inhibition, never the only authority for a hazardous spark, fire, moving tank, or held vertical head. Loss of fluid circulation, false-high level, floating sensor, mispositioned nozzle, failed suppression, retained spark-enable, stuck ordinary cycle request, power restoration, and service override each need separate fault/rearm paths.

A fire can remain hazardous after spark power is removed. Conversely, an extinguishing-agent discharge does not prove axes, electrical cabinets, or stored energy safe for access. Do not use the same ordinary process sensor and UI tag as both the cause of a stop and its purported independent physical witness. An OEM's **temperature threshold** is a process/model-specific instruction, not a universal design setting or an independently validated safe stopping limit.

## Adversarial causal paths (desk analysis only)
1. **Low local fluid with plausible tank float:** spark zone partly uncovered -> ordinary level remains green -> combustible vapor/ignition possible -> selected independent detection and discharge inhibit must be designed and physically validated; suppression is last-layer mitigation.
2. **False temperature normal:** sensor drift/disconnect -> high dielectric temperature unobserved -> demand safe response cannot be inferred from the HMI -> require exact sensor diagnostics and fault response.
3. **Nozzle moved to accommodate fixture:** nominal suppression device installed -> agent no longer reaches hazard -> inspection and change/revalidation required before operation.
4. **E-stop while hot spark/incipient fire:** pulse generator commanded off, pump off -> thermal/fire hazard may persist -> separate detection, alarm, extinguishing and safe evacuation still required.
5. **POWER ON after maintenance:** documented pump start and Z “break” release -> a supposedly idle machine may change mechanical state before CNC cycle start -> hold/occupancy/clearance and selected brake mechanism require qualification.
6. **Different OEM/model substitution:** EDGE2 mirrored 53°C vs EDGE3i original 45±3°C -> copied setpoint invalid -> use exact model/serial/revision and current manual; no generic threshold.
7. **Oil-based wire EDM assumed water:** misclassified fire risk -> wrong protection chosen -> verify dielectric chemistry/SDS, OEM approvals and fire response.

## Transfer to OpenPressBrake without false equivalence
EDM and press-brake physics differ; **do not assert an EDM is installed on OpenPressBrake**. The transferable engineering mechanism is the distinction between a normal *request*, an independently effective *final element*, an independent *physical safe-state witness*, and deliberate *fresh rearm*—especially where POWER ON may change an actuator state. Actual OpenPressBrake Y1/Y2 stop/hold, hydraulic isolation, guard, rescue and restart remain UNKNOWN; G0/G1 HOLD.

## Failure-path analysis
1. **Documentary accumulation:** every useful exact-OEM finding is appended to the “one-page” owner handoff, regardless of whether the named device exists on the selected machine.
2. **Human-factors pressure:** the technician sees long conditional material, cannot distinguish essential A–G records from examples for uninstalled components, and may respond with unsupported N/A, skip the packet, or seek an informal bypass to get the machine running. This behavioral consequence is **INFERENCE**, not observed OpenPressBrake conduct.
3. **Safety-proof confusion:** a longer checklist can appear comprehensive while still containing no installed Pilz/AKAS program, hydraulic final-element identity, gravity-load proof, actual guard geometry, or independent physical stop/hold witness.
4. **Countermeasure:** keep A–G short, preserve exact source provenance in a dated reviewer archive, expand only after a qualified component-presence/hazard map, and keep every missing/contradictory essential safety proposition on HOLD. No “checkbox green” or appendix length can substitute for physical validation.

## Non-regression rule for future sessions
- **Do not append long manufacturer source audits to the owner-facing A–G request.** Put exact conditional evidence in a linked dated reviewer/source audit, then update only the affected existing packet ID and a one-sentence pointer when the distinction changes the owner's immediate request.
- If a real selected-machine document reveals a newly installed energy source or hazard, the qualified reviewer updates the **private** hazard/component map and opens only relevant Stage B material. N/A requires dated as-built proof; absence of a catalog item never proves absence of a hazard.

# 4000-LC-OVERRIDE-95 — M51 spindle selector is discarded at Task and reported as a global bit

**2026-11-01 | NON-GRADUATED | corrective source-depth finding; no runtime tests.** This **corrects a too-strong claim** in [OVERRIDE-SCOPE-01](4400_OVERRIDE_SCOPE_01_LINUXCNC_2910_M48_M49_M51_DOLLAR_SELECTION_SOURCE_DOC_CONFLICT_2026-10-29.md): `M51 P0 $1` is accepted and emitted for spindle 1 by the interpreter, but the inspected lower layers do **not** preserve that selectivity.

## Consequences and correction

The interpreter maintains per-spindle `settings->speed_override[s]` and emits an indexed canonical message, but Task converts the message to a **global override enable**. For a two-spindle system, `M51 P0 $1` can therefore disable speed override for both spindles in motion/status rather than only spindle 1. The exact timing depends on the queued versus new enable mask and command ordering; **no real runtime behavior has been reproduced**.

This global bit is also the least-significant bit accidentally used by the feed and spindle inhibit expressions. Thus a supposedly spindle-specific `M51` operation can affect the effectiveness of `motion.feed-inhibit` and `spindle.N.inhibit`. See [OVERRIDE-93](4400_LINUXCNC_OVERRIDE_93_FEED_INHIBIT_SS_BIT_COUPLING_SOURCE_AUDIT_2026-11-01.md) and [OVERRIDE-94](4400_LINUXCNC_OVERRIDE_94_SPINDLE_INHIBIT_SS_BIT_SPEED_ON_SOURCE_AUDIT_2026-11-01.md).

**Explicit predecessor correction:** OVERRIDE-SCOPE-01's prediction row “`M51 P0 $1` disables spindle 1 only” is **valid only for interpreter selection and canonical message construction**, **not** for the traced Task/motion behavior. The earlier conclusion must not be taught as end-to-end selectivity.

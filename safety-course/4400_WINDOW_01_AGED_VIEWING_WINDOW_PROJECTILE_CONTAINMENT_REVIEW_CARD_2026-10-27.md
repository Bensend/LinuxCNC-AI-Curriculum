# 4400-WINDOW-01 — “Clear window” is not “qualified projectile containment”
**One-pass technician/engineer review; 2026-10-27. NON-GRADUATED, documentary.** Read [original source audit](../research/4400_WINDOW_01_POLYCARBONATE_GUARD_WINDOW_AGEING_RETENTION_AND_REPLACEMENT_OEM_AUDIT_2026-10-27.md) before making any selected-machine decision.

A CNC door can close, its interlock can report healthy, and its window can still be mechanically unsafe. The critical physical function is **retain a credible ejected workpiece/tool/wheel fragment at the chosen process energy**; optical clarity and electrical door status are different witnesses.

**Practical review:** identify the exact machine and original panel SKU, laminated construction, layer orientation, frame/fasteners, impact rating and install date. Look for edge-seal leakage, coolant between layers, impact bulging, cracks, heavy scratching, chemical/cleaner history and expired OEM service date. **Absence of visible damage does not prove remaining impact strength**; STUDER and UNITED GRINDING explicitly warn that embrittlement may be invisible. Replace immediately when the applicable OEM directs it. Do not infer a universal two-year interval: STUDER/UNITED GRINDING specify two years for their described grinder windows; SICURTEC distinguishes pure polycarbonate and protected laminate service recommendations. Use selected manufacturer instructions.

**Change-control trap:** a new coolant, cleaning agent, spindle speed, chuck, abrasive wheel, tooling, window supplier, laminate or door frame may invalidate the original containment argument even if all LinuxCNC/FPGA signals are unchanged. Require qualified review and correctly specified spare, not a clear panel cut to fit. Put the dated replacement label on the outside, stock the correct kit, use approved cleaning supplies and make reinstallation easier than leaving the guard removed.

**Separate authority:** (1) ordinary CNC process and status; (2) independently qualified access interlock/stop and deliberate restart; (3) passive projectile barrier and mounting; (4) physical maintenance isolation. None proves the others. Never use a person's body or a running machine as an impact test. If barrier adequacy is unknown, keep people outside the hazard zone; no personnel-exposed hazardous operation.

**Evidence handoff:** WIN-1 identity/date, WIN-2 ejection threat, WIN-3 age/chemical/condition, WIN-4 frame/retention, WIN-5 changes/interlock/restart, WIN-6 usability. All selected-machine witnesses UNKNOWN/NOT RUN; independent 2520–25F0 evaluation OPEN; OpenPressBrake G0/G1 HOLD. Cross-machine documentary study only.

**Independent research caveat:** Original DGUV IFA 5169 (status page dated 2022-10-28) reports a past investigation finding serious retention loss in a window after only **one year**; the cause was still being investigated. IFA says historical replacement intervals span **2–12 years** by material protection, maker and coolant. This is **not** a new universal one-year interval. Historical DGUV/BIA 6054 tested material panels with a 100-g projectile; its thickness/speed examples are **not** a ready-made retrofit specification. Acrylic/PMMA cannot be substituted merely because it is transparent. ISO 16090-1:2022 is the current machining-centre edition; its paid technical clauses were not inspected.

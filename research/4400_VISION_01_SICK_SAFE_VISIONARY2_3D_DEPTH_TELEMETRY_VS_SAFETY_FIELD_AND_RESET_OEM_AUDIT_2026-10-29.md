# 4400-VISION-01 — A 3D depth picture is not a personnel-protective safety function
**2026-10-29 | 4000 safety source-depth | NON-GRADUATED / no physical validation**

## Question and novelty check
Can a LinuxCNC/FPGA/AI vision display or a networked 3D point cloud be treated as proof that a person is absent from a robot cell, press-brake approach or AGV path? What does an actual safety-oriented 3D camera add, and where does its responsibility end?

Checked the full `safety-course/SAFETY_COURSE_INDEX.md`, `SAFETY_COURSE_RESEARCH.md`, `PROGRESS.md` and `CURRICULUM.md` for `camera`, `safeVisionary`, `depth map`, `3D vision`, `time-of-flight`, plus scanner/ESPE/optical/restart predecessors. No camera-specific safety-function module found. **Novel mechanism:** a manufacturer's *same camera* supplies ordinary depth/UDP data, safety-oriented data with additional integrity information, and independent configured OSSD protective-field outputs; these are NOT interchangeable. Existing scanner/light-curtain, G0 telemetry, 2520 hazard-to-function and 2590 guarding lessons are prerequisites, not superseded.

## Direct manufacturer findings (DOC-CONFIRMED for the observed passages)
1. **Safety field vs ordinary image:** §3, printed pp. 15–17 describes a configurable protective field with a safety OFF output; the distance-monitor/ROI table separately distinguishes measurement data output (**no** safety switch-off) from a configured **safe distance check** (**yes**). Table 44, printed p.99, lists the device **Type 2 / Category 2 / PL c / SIL 1**. These are device/function claims, **not** a machine PL or an approval for a specific press brake.
2. **UDP depth is not a safe-stop output:** §7.14, printed pp.79–80, says ordinary depth data is for general control/navigation; safety use requires additional error monitoring for transport errors, contamination, missing/repeated/distorted depth, motion, reflectivity and exposure. A distinct `In safe mode` UDP option adds per-pixel safety/diagnostic information, but SICK explicitly assigns further processing and the **entire safety function** to the integrator. An ordinary LinuxCNC HAL, FPGA, PC vision model or Ethernet dashboard cannot inherit PL c merely by consuming these packets.
3. **A 3D view is not complete coverage:** §4.3.1–4.3.2, printed pp.22–24, warns of undetected regions outside the field, obstacles, crawl-under/reach-over routes, and people remaining between sensor and hazard. OEM suggests deflector plates/fences, additional measures and restart interlock where people can remain unseen. The protective-field vertical aperture **68°×42°** differs from **68°×58°** available for other fields/data (§3 and technical data p.102); do not treat every visible 3D pixel as a protective pixel. Manufacturer-specific installation guidance includes testing the actual zone with a test object; do not transfer a geometric dimension to another camera.
4. **Background tolerance can defeat near-background detection:** §7.11, printed p.69, warns that a larger reference-contour tolerance band may fail to detect an object touching the background. A **dark test object of the configured size** is prescribed for checking detection. A too-small band instead causes nuisance shutdowns due to vibration/noise/temperature. This is a **coverage/availability trade-off**, not permission to weaken detection to avoid trips.
5. **Multiple sampling changes protective response:** §7.10, printed pp.62–63, describes sampling as the count of detections needed before response. Table 52, printed p.103, gives **89 ms at n=2** and **293 ms at n=8** (difference **204 ms** at the sensor interface). That difference is not a machine stopping-distance calculation. Controller, final-element and physical run-down times remain additional. Monitoring-case changes have their own timing and a person may already occupy the newly selected field (§4.3.3 p.25 and §7.10 p.63).
6. **Reset is not restart:** §4.4.6, printed pp.44–45, describes a configurable restart interlock: after the field clears, the safety output remains OFF until a reset outside the hazard zone with full visibility, followed by a *separate* machine start. §7.12.3 p.75 also lists immediate and delayed automatic output re-enable options; whether these are acceptable is application-specific. A green camera indicator or OSSD ON alone cannot establish the zone is empty, a ram is held, or the operator has deliberately requested a new hazardous cycle.
7. **OSSD and non-safety outputs:** §4.4.5 p.44 expressly excludes universal diagnostic outputs from safety functions. §12.3–12.4 pp.102–104 defines sensor-interface response and brief OSSD test pulses. Correctly selected safety input evaluation is required; a normal PLC bit is not an independent final element.

## Hazard → physical function → witness (INFERENCE / design synthesis, NOT installed-machine proof)
| Hazard and plausible shortcut | Safer architecture and physical boundary | Minimum independent witness |
|---|---|---|
| An AI 3D visualization looks clear while a person is hidden behind tooling or outside protective aperture. | Fixed guard or verified complementary safety-rated protective device covers blind spots; independent safety controller processes qualified safety signals. LinuxCNC receives **status only**. | As-built field map; test-object coverage at approach/occlusion points; final-element stop/hold verification by qualified personnel. |
| Technician widens contour tolerance to stop nuisance trips, leaving a person near the background undetected. | Fix mounting, vibration, lighting/reflectors and contamination first; restrict parameter access and require revalidation after every field change. | Exact device/firmware/configuration and approved field checks including dark test object at boundary/background. |
| Integrator treats Ethernet depth/AI person classifier as equivalent to OSSD. | Keep raw UDP/AI outputs in normal control/diagnostics; if safety-oriented UDP is proposed, separately engineer and validate **end-to-end** integrity, timing, failure response, receiving subsystem and physical cut-off; prefer certified field/OSSD integration for simple applications. | Safety-function SRS, exact receiver implementation and diagnostic/fault tests, selected machine stop/hold results. |
| Camera field clears, OSSD goes ON and CNC resumes an old queued command. | Deliberate visible reset outside zone, separate new start, stale-command inhibition and independent final-element feedback; presence cannot be inferred from one camera's clear status. | Restart/fault/power-cycle/mode-change qualified tests; no hazardous automatic restart. |
| Installer increases n=2 to n=8 to suppress nuisance stops without updating layout. | Recalculate separation with the selected sensor response, receiving chain and measured machine stopping/holding time; do not reuse an old distance. | Approved configuration, measured stop/run-down, actual geometry and independent review. |

**Human-factors design:** Prefer accessible fixed/hinged guards and clearly marked, easily revalidated fields; put reset where the entire hazard is visible. A camera is a *supplement* where its coverage is demonstrable, not an inexpensive excuse to remove physical guards. Frequent false trips are a defect to fix at the installation/environment level, not a reason to silently bypass protective logic.

## Narrow engineering question and predecessor comparison
Can a handwheel's internal count advance while the HAL jog-count pin remains unchanged in a non-jog mode, then publish the entire difference on the first jog event after switching modes? This is an exact XHC-WHB04B-6 pendant version/fault path, not a new generic feed-inhibit or wheel-jog safety function. ## Conditional failure-path timeline (no simulated numeric machine values)
1. A non-lead axis is selected; pendant operates in MPG feed-override mode. The handwheel internal counter advances while the v2.9.10 `jog-counts` output does not get updated in that MPG branch.
2. Motion's `old_ajog_counts` continues to reflect the last published count, assuming ordinary HAL wiring and motion scheduling.
3. Operator selects CON/STEP; the first nonzero handwheel event publishes the new cumulative counter. Motion sees a **delta that includes earlier MPG turns**, not merely the latest click.
4. If the motion enable, teleop, jog-enable, non-homing, non-keyboard-jog and limit conditions permit, `axis.c` uses that delta multiplied by the current jog scale to request movement. No physical distance or stopping time is inferred.
5. In the inspected development code, MPG publishes intermediate counts while jog scale is zero, reducing this specific stale-count transition mechanism. This does **not** validate the pendant as a safety device or eliminate all jog/mode-change hazards.

## Human factors, review and validation
A user can reasonably expect a feed-override wheel turn not to become an axis move after changing modes. Source-level correctness therefore matters to a safe, low-friction operator interface: make the transition predictable, display the selected mode and active axis clearly, and require independent physical protection where hazardous exposure is possible. A guard or E-stop cannot be replaced by HAL jog-count synchronization.

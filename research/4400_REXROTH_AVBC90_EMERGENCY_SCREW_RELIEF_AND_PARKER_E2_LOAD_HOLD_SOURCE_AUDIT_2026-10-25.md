# 4400-HOLD-01 — Original OEM source audit: cylinder-side check/metering, relief, emergency override and false holding claims
**2026-10-25 | Original research | DOCUMENTARY ONLY | NON-GRADUATED**

## Engineering question and why this is not a duplicate of the earlier velocity-fuse lesson
Can a designer treat a nominally load-holding hydraulic valve as an unconditional gravity lock after power loss, hose rupture, shock pressure, service override, or a changed valve variant? **No such conclusion follows from the inspected documents.** Earlier [HYDAC flow-window audit](4400_HOSE_BURST_VELOCITY_FUSE_FLOW_WINDOW_AND_UNSAFE_TEST_TRANSFER_AUDIT_2026-10-24.md) addresses flow-triggered rupture fuses; [Parker HAS override audit](4400_PARKER_HAS_LOAD_HOLD_OVERRIDE_CODE_AND_MANIFOLD_ORIENTATION_2026-10-23.md) addresses a different product. This audit instead traces the **same original Rexroth check/metering assembly's multiple release paths** and the OEM-mandated restoration of its emergency screw. It does not establish a selected press-brake design.

## Adversarial failure path and authority separation
1. **Normal command layer:** LinuxCNC/HAL/ordinary FPGA requests a proportional valve, pump, ram motion or stop. Its position/pressure readback proves only the defined telemetry path; it cannot establish a personnel-safe holding function.
2. **Independent safety-control layer:** selected safety controller, fault monitoring, independent final elements and safe-stop/hold requirements must be specified per hazard; a relay output is not the actual hydraulic state.
3. **Physical energy layer:** cylinder chambers, gravity, pilot/vent/return paths, relief opening, seals, hoses, manual E override and mechanical ram restraint determine actual movement potential.
4. **Maintenance layer:** service overrides, residual pressure, mechanical restraint, LOTO, qualified recovery and deliberate rearm are independent propositions.
5. **Adversarial scenario:** E screw used for qualified emergency lowering, then omitted from restoration. A later power-off yields a false “valve should hold” assumption. **INFERENCE**, not a measured fault; source confirms restoration instruction, not a tested failure rate or travel.
6. **Common cause:** both Y1/Y2 circuits might share a pilot, vent, pressure supply, common relief or maintenance practice. Do not infer independence from two nominal valves.

## What the source does NOT establish
No actual OpenPressBrake hydraulic drawing, installed A-VBC-90/E2 valve, valve orientation, cylinder-side mounting, independent Y1/Y2 chamber map, pilot truth table, pressure threshold, leakage, PL/SIL, stopping distance, rescue plan, secondary restraint, or test result. DX and SX documentation use separate identifiers; no asserted compatibility. **SOURCE-CONFIRMED:** no new LinuxCNC code inspected. **TEST-CONFIRMED:** none. **COMMUNITY-REPORTED:** none. **UNKNOWN:** all selected-machine details.

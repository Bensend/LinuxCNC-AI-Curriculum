# 4400-WELD-RETURN-02 — welding current return is not protective earth, a crane, or an ordinary CNC stop

**2026-10-27 | SOURCE/DOCUMENTARY RESEARCH ONLY | NON-GRADUATED | NOT RUN | no machine-specific wiring authorization**

## Predecessor and reason to study
Full canonical safety index, research plan and PROGRESS search for `weld current`, `work lead`, `return circuit`, `electrode holder`, `crane hook`, `grounding`, and `bonding` found no dedicated **welding-current return versus protective equipment grounding** safety module. Existing electrical isolation, leakage/sneak-current, tool retention and 3300 plasma material cover adjacent issues, but not this exact high-current physical return-path trap. **New exact regulatory/OEM causal mechanism for robotic weld fixtures and automated cells**; not a new general E-stop concept and not a claim OpenPressBrake welds.

## Direct original sources and scope

No machine current, conductor ampacity, welding polarity or physical installation is established.
2. **DOC-CONFIRMED direct** [Miller, *How Do Safety and Scheduled Welding Machine Maintenance Protect Your Welding Assets?*](https://www.millerwelds.com/resources/knowledge-hub/industrial-welding/safety-and-scheduled-maintenance-protect-your-welding-assets), inspected 2026-10-27. Manufacturer explicitly distinguishes **work lead from grounding lead**, warns that misdirected welding current can damage other equipment conductors of inadequate ampacity, and repeats the prohibition on crane/hoist/chain return paths. This is generic OEM education, not the exact installed welder manual.
3. **DOC-CONFIRMED indexed-only** US [OSHA 29 CFR 1910.333](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.333), official indexed text: control circuit pushbuttons/interlocks are not sole electrical deenergization; qualified verification must account for backfeed. Not an arc-welding-specific universal voltage threshold or a substitute for the selected machine's isolation plan.

## Why this matters physically
**Normal process return:** welding power source electrode output → arc/workpiece → **intentional work-lead return** to welding source. **Protective earth/equipment grounding** has a different fault-protection purpose and should not be casually substituted for the process return. If a work clamp is loose, contaminated, or attached remotely, **INFERENCE:** process current may seek unintended conductive paths through a fixture, structural member, hinge, equipment ground, cable shield or moving hardware. The consequences may include localized heating, arcing, equipment damage and an exposure hazard. These are plausible mechanisms requiring actual machine electrical design; no selected incident or measurements were obtained.

**Source-backed special hazards:** a lifting crane or hoist is **not** a permissible current return; a fixture/structure used as a return needs deliberate bonding/current collection. An earthed frame is not proof of a proper welding work-return path. Conversely a sound work lead is not proof of equipment protective grounding.

**Normal stop trap:** arc-off, robot motion stop or an ordinary LinuxCNC output transition cannot by itself demonstrate electrical supply isolation or absence of live welding outputs; the actual power-source output-enable, open-circuit state, residual energy and disconnect topology remain **UNKNOWN**. A machine may also retain hot workpiece/fire and shielding-gas hazards after welding stops; see [WELD-GAS-01](4400_WELD_GAS_01_SHIELDING_GAS_OXYGEN_DEFICIENCY_SCREEN_VENTILATION_AND_RESCUE_SOURCE_AUDIT_2026-10-27.md). No claim is made that every power source energizes its electrode after an OFF request.

**Adversarial conditional path:** a robotic cell welds a part held by a crane while the work clamp is loose. It is unsafe to reason that the crane frame's protective grounding provides an acceptable welding return. The eCFR expressly prohibits cranes and hoists carrying welding current. The correct next action is **stop the work and obtain qualified electrical review**, not experiment by intentionally passing current through a crane. The case is **PAPER ONLY / NOT RUN**.

## Design and human-factors consequences (INFERENCE)
- Make the correct, rated work-lead connection mechanically obvious and easy to attach, inspect, clean and retain; a difficult clamp invites improvised structural return.
- Separate the operator's ordinary weld-stop from electrical isolation and personnel-access authority. Do not use LinuxCNC/normal FPGA as sole protection against electric shock.
- Design fixtures so intended high-current return is controlled and hoists/guards/hinges/sensors are not accidental conductors. Verify source-specific requirements rather than inventing cable sizes or acceptable current.
- Treat return-path changes, added clamps, changed fixture material/hinges, welding power-source replacement, extra simultaneous welders, and protective-ground changes as safety-evidence invalidation triggers.
- When safe electrical isolation/return/grounding cannot be demonstrated, no personnel-exposed hazardous operation; remote isolated experimentation only under a separately qualified plan.

## Same-session direct-source upgrade — exact output-on conditions and electrical LOTO
The [Miller maintenance article](https://www.millerwelds.com/en-us/resources/knowledge-hub/industrial-welding/safety-and-scheduled-maintenance-protect-your-welding-assets), **published 2024-08-27**, was directly opened in full after initial search. It distinguishes semiautomatic/automatic wire welding (wire, reel, drive roll housing and touching metal can be live when output is on), MIG/TIG sources with output on/off controls, and some SMAW/stick sources **without** such output control (electrode live when the power switch is on). Therefore **"arc stopped" is not a universal electrical-isolation witness**, and the actual installed source model/output topology matters. Do not generalize that all MIG/TIG electrodes remain energized after output OFF, or that all SMAW sources behave identically.

US [eCFR 29 CFR 1910.333](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-29/subtitle-B/chapter-XVII/part-1910/subpart-S/subject-group-ECFRbd7903c591a5eff/section-1910.333) was also **directly opened**, showing Title 29 updated through **2026-10-22**. §(b)(2)(ii)(B) prohibits control-circuit pushbuttons, selector switches and interlocks as the sole electrical deenergization; §(b)(2)(iv)(A)-(B) requires qualified verification of no restart and instrument verification of exposed circuit deenergization including unrelated backfeed. **DOC-CONFIRMED direct** replaces the earlier "official indexed-only" status for §1910.333. This is a regulatory electrical-work practice, not a direction for unqualified users to probe energized welding terminals. No machine test or authorization.

**Source-precedence note:** Miller distinguishes welding work lead from equipment grounding but cautions that workpiece grounding and work-terminal grounding configurations require qualified application-specific review; do not create an unqualified extra earth path by treating generic guidance as a universal wiring schematic. The as-built OEM power-source manual and qualified electrical design must determine the actual permitted topology.

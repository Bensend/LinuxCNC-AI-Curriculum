# 4400-WINDOW-01 — CNC viewing-window ageing, fragment retention and replacement authority
**Date audited:** 2026-10-27 UTC. **Status:** source/documentary only, NON-GRADUATED. **Scope:** mills, lathes, grinding machines and enclosed CNC machines; **not** an assertion that OpenPressBrake has a CNC viewing window. **Physical tests / independent evaluation:** NOT RUN.

## Unresolved engineering question and source selection
A retrofit may retain the original transparent panel while changing spindle speed, wheel, chuck, coolant, cutting process or guard frame. Does a visually clear, interlocked door prove that the window can contain a foreseeable ejected fragment? **No.** Window *retention capacity* and its *service-life validity* are independent of door-interlock electrical status. This is the incremental physical witness beyond the existing 4400-ABRASIVE-01 wheel-burst/speed/guard module, 4400-GUARD-01/02 interlock usability, 4400-AUTODOOR-01 moving-door pinch and 4400-LASER-ENC-01 optical viewing-window hazard. Full canonical index, research plan and historical progress were searched for polycarbonate, acrylic, viewing-window ageing, coolant attack, window replacement, impact rating and related synonyms: no corresponding mechanical window-ageing lesson found. ABRASIVE-01 covers the projectile *source* and general guard compatibility, not material ageing/edge-seal loss/transparent-panel replacement. LASER-ENC-01 covers **optical attenuation**, which cannot be used as fragment-containment proof.

## Hazard and authority decomposition
1. **HAZ / projectile:** wheel burst, chuck/workpiece release, tool fragment or process ejection may impact the transparent enclosure. Existing spindle overspeed/guard and workholding modules address initiators, not panel integrity. The possible ejection energy, direction, geometry and duty are **UNKNOWN** until selected-machine analysis.
2. **PHY / barrier:** transparent panel material, layer orientation, thickness, ageing, edge seals, clamps/frame retention and supporting guard structure must jointly resist the selected ejection scenario. **INFERENCE:** a correctly interlocked door with an aged or chemically compromised panel may still fail mechanically. A generic clear acrylic replacement, arbitrary polycarbonate sheet or “same dimensions” spare is not qualified by appearance.
3. **SF / authority:** door interlock, guard locking and independent machine-stop functions can restrict access while hazards exist. They cannot restore lost impact resistance. Ordinary LinuxCNC/FPGA machine-state or maintenance-counter logic may *display* a window due date, but cannot certify containment or replace independent safety authority.
4. **AGE / chemistry:** coolant, oils, unsuitable cleaning agents, edge-seal leakage, impact and abrasion change the validity of original barrier evidence. Manufacturer says embrittlement can be invisible: **clear/no crack != retained strength**. A single visual inspection is necessary but not sufficient for a known overdue or chemically exposed panel.
5. **CHG:** coolant formulation, cleaning solvent, spindle/wheel speed, tooling/chuck, machine task, door/frame hardware, panel laminate, supplier, edge seal and pane installation all trigger review of affected physical impact evidence. A machine control update alone is not a barrier qualification.
6. **HF / practical design:** make the OEM-correct pane and sealed frame a convenient replacement kit, put a legible install/replace-by label on the *outside*, keep approved cleaner at the machine, stock spare parts, use accessible captive fasteners and a service instruction that does not require defeating the door interlock. If replacement requires cutting away the guard or a long outage, the resulting incentive to keep an expired pane is an engineering usability defect. No recommendation to operate with an unqualified pane.
7. **Residual:** even a correctly selected and maintained window has finite containment energy. UNITED GRINDING warns against putting one's face near the panel during high-energy machining. This is residual-risk communication, not a substitute for the correct pane and process guard.

## Adversarial paper failure path (not a physical observation)
A retrofit keeps the old clear CNC door window; its interlock tests PASS. New coolant/cleaner attacks exposed polycarbonate edges; pane becomes brittle without obvious clouding. Operator sees the interlock “ready” and runs a higher-energy workpiece setup. A chuck/wheel/tool fragment strikes the weakened pane. **INFERENCE**: the electrical interlock may behave correctly while mechanical containment fails. No selected incident or calculated ejection energy is claimed. An unchanged normal LinuxCNC configuration does not prove window safety.

**Discriminating questions:** What exact pane/laminate, maker, install date, replacement interval and edge protection are installed? What coolant/cleaner history and ingress signs exist? Which ejection scenarios and rated pane/frame/fasteners are in the selected design file? What changes stale that file? Can the guard be restored easily with OEM parts? Is the operator's face outside the likely bulge/ejection zone?

## Minimum selected-machine witness (conditional, UNKNOWN / NOT RUN)
- **WIN-1 — Identity:** machine/model/guard/pane SKU, original impact specification, installation orientation, manufacturer, service label and dated replacement record.
- **WIN-2 — Threat:** selected tool/wheel/chuck/workpiece ejection scenarios, operating speeds, geometry and qualified required containment; do not invent impact joules or thickness.
- **WIN-3 — Condition:** approved cleaner and coolant compatibility, visible damage, edge-seal state, layer ingress, age and replacement trigger; known expiration/ingress => remove from hazardous service pending qualified replacement.
- **WIN-4 — Retention:** qualified frame, clamps, fasteners, seals and adjacent guard; correct pane material alone does not prove installed assembly performance.
- **WIN-5 — Change/restart:** record supplier/pane/coolant/process changes, independent guard interlock/final-element tests and deliberate restart separately. Neither test can substitute for mechanical containment.
- **WIN-6 — Human factors:** accessible correct spares, no bypass-required maintenance, legible date/cleaner guidance and operator standoff instructions.
All selected-machine witnesses are **UNKNOWN / NOT RUN**. No destructive impact test or personnel-exposed machine test is authorized by this course. For a machine whose barrier cannot be qualified, do not operate with people exposed to the projectile hazard; any experimental work requires isolated/remote conditions with people outside the danger zone and residual risk plainly documented.

## Independent primary research and current machining-centre edition (same-session source-depth)
**DOC-CONFIRMED direct original public research registry:** [DGUV IFA project 5169, *Ageing of polycarbonate viewing windows under new metalworking fluids*](https://dguv.de/ifa/;/forschung/projektverzeichnis/ifa5169.jsp), **page status dated 2022-10-28**, lists the project as *ongoing* at that date. It reports that earlier IFA research supported replacement intervals ranging **two to twelve years**, depending on manufacturer, protective construction/coating/film/glass and coolant. Critically, during a separate accident investigation, IFA observed significantly reduced retention capacity in windows only **one year** in service. Possible explanations (new coolant chemistry, polymer manufacture or coatings) are **hypotheses**, not confirmed causes. The project proposes immersion exposure and repeated impact/material characterization; no final study outcome is asserted. This strengthens the **do not use a universal interval** and **a clear, younger window can still be compromised** conclusions. It does **not** prove all one-year windows fail or prescribe a new universal one-year replacement schedule.

**DOC-CONFIRMED direct original research registry:** [DGUV/BIA project 6054, completed August 1997](https://www.dguv.de/ifa/forschung/projektverzeichnis/bia_6054-2.jsp) used a **100-g projectile** under the then draft prEN 12417 setup to compare steel, aluminium, polycarbonate and polymethyl methacrylate (PMMA/acrylic). It reported specific sufficient guard constructions under its test conditions and found PMMA less ductile than polycarbonate. **Do not reuse** its 115 m/s / 25 m/s test context, 3/5/8-mm material examples, or projectile mass as a selected machine rating, design limit or accepted retrofit thickness. Its important transferable mechanism is **material ductility and assembly design determine fragment retention; transparent PMMA is not an interchangeable polycarbonate replacement**. Its 1997 test setup and 2002 page update are historical, not a current normative approval.

**DOC-CONFIRMED publisher scope only:** [ISO 16090-1:2022](https://www.iso.org/standard/81558.html) is the current published machining-centre/milling/transfer-machine safety edition (corrected October 2023; 2017 edition withdrawn); its published scope includes powered doors, tool changers, clamping and additional grinding equipment. Full paid impact/ageing clauses were **not** inspected. This corrects the tendency to treat the grinding-machine ISO 16089 as the only relevant Type-C standard for a mill.

**Evidence consequence:** DGUV's independent investigation demonstrates why *calendar age alone* and *visual clarity alone* are insufficient to infer retained impact resistance. The qualified physical witness must include actual coolant/cleaner exposure and pane construction, and the reviewer must not transfer historical coupon impact tests to an assembled pane/frame. These are research-registered observations, not selected-machine **TEST-CONFIRMED** results for this curriculum. No lab, physical proof or new numerical acceptance threshold is justified.

# 4400-COORD-01 — Type 2 short-circuit coordination is not permission to reset and run
**2026-10-28 | 4000 safety course | Original manufacturer-source research | NON-GRADUATED | NO LIVE FAULT TEST**

## Bounded question and direct predecessor novelty check
After a cabinet/motor-starter short circuit, the breaker or fuse has operated. The starter is advertised as **Type 2 coordinated** and its safety controller shows no active fault. May maintenance simply replace the fuse/reset the breaker and resume motion? **No.** The manufacturer expressly allows some contact welding under Type 2 conditions, and the machine's independent stop/hold and safe restart must still be established. The selected **complete protection combination** and manufacturer post-fault instructions determine what may be reused.

This is a **new source-specific post-short-circuit / combination-coordination failure path**, not a rediscovery of contact welding, EDM or panel SCCR. The [2026-10-25 PANEL-01 UL/OSHA audit](4400_UL_OSHA_MACHINE_PANEL_MULTIFEED_SCCR_ARC_FLASH_AND_LOTO_SOURCE_AUDIT_2026-10-25.md) already distinguishes available fault current, panel SCCR, isolation and ram holding. [2026-10-25 MIRROR-01](4400_SCHNEIDER_SIEMENS_MIRROR_NC_VS_GUIDED_AUX_EDM_VARIANT_SOURCE_AUDIT_2026-10-25.md) and [2026-10-28 accessory source-depth](4400_MIRROR_01_ROCKWELL_SUVA_AND_SCHNEIDER_ACCESSORY_SOURCE_DEPTH_ADDENDUM_2026-10-28.md) already teach mirror/EDM and replacement drift. **Increment:** Type 1 versus Type 2 is about **tested short-circuit behavior of the selected starter/protective-device combination**, with explicit post-fault contact-weld/inspection implications, not the safety function's PL/SIL or a guarantee that all energy paths are open.

## Directly inspected original manufacturer sources
1. **Schneider Electric USA**, FAQ **FA105974**, *What is the difference between Type 1 & Type 2 coordination?*, published **2003-10-08**, modified **2026-04-20**, directly opened original HTML https://www.se.com/us/en/faqs/FA105974/. **DOC-CONFIRMED:** Type I may require repair/replacement after a short circuit; Type II permits further use under the coordinated conditions **but explicitly recognizes risk of contact welding** and directs the manufacturer to identify maintenance measures.
2. **Schneider Electric USA**, FAQ **FA99443**, *What is the definition of Type II Coordination?*, published **2002-08-28**, modified **2026-05-12**, directly opened original HTML https://www.se.com/us/en/faqs/FA99443/. **DOC-CONFIRMED:** light welding is allowed only within the manufacturer's conditions (contacts easily separable); Type II is evaluated by **specific contactor, overload relay and short-circuit protective-device combinations** with low/high-level fault, overload-calibration and dielectric checks. The FAQ's example **100,000 A** is a test example, not a selected OpenPressBrake rating or a generic coordination requirement.
3. **Schneider Electric USA**, FAQ **FA238342**, *ATS48 Type 2 coordination / allowed breaker combination*, published **2014-12-29**, modified **2026-05-26**, directly opened original HTML https://www.se.com/us/en/faqs/FA238342/. **DOC-CONFIRMED for the named ATS48 configuration only:** the manufacturer says a different breaker selection, even if its standalone interrupt rating appears adequate for the feeder, does not automatically retain the tested Type II combination. It discusses peak current and I²t let-through and specific product-dependent protection. The example breaker sizes and 150-kA value are **not transferable** to OpenPressBrake.
4. **Siemens**, *Electrical Designer* official product FAQ https://www.siemens.com/el-gr/industries/industrial-machinery/panel-building-electrical-designer/ — search-indexed official manufacturer text corroborates Type 1 repair and Type 2 contact-weld maintenance. **Direct page open timed out**; use **INDEXED DOC ONLY**, not independent full-page verification.
5. **Rockwell Automation**, *Motor Protection Circuit Breaker and Motor Circuit Protector Specifications*, **140M-TD002G-EN-P January 2018**, original official PDF https://literature.rockwellautomation.com/idc/groups/literature/documents/td/140m-td002_-en-p.pdf — official search-indexed original PDF defines Type II and provides **combination-specific** 140M + 100-C tables. **DIRECTLY OPENED original 60-page PDF and VISUALLY INSPECTED printed page 31 (PDF page index 30)**. It confirms the Type II welding exception and shows a specific **400 V** 140M combination table with a footnote that **100-C16 contactors are Type I only** for the referenced row even though 100-C12 appears as the minimum Type II contactor. This is a strong manufacturer-specific counterexample to assuming a nominally larger contactor automatically inherits Type II; the example table is **not** an OpenPressBrake rating or a universal rule.

## Four different electrical claims — do not substitute one for another
| Claim | What it addresses | What it cannot by itself prove |
|---|---|---|
| **Breaker/fuse interrupting rating** | Capability of that protective device to interrupt a specified prospective fault under its conditions | Assembled starter Type II coordination, panel SCCR, safe mechanical stop |
| **Panel/machine SCCR** | Withstand/short-circuit rating of the assembled equipment at its actual supplies, using applicable methods | Post-fault contactor operability, absence of weld, personnel-safety function integrity |
| **Type 1 / Type 2 starter coordination** | Tested interaction of **specific** SCPD, contactor/starter, overload/related components under fault conditions | That the actual post-fault machine is safe, EDM is healthy, no other energy source remains |
| **Safety function / EDM / physical validation** | Monitored final-element and actual hazardous-energy/stop/hold/restart propositions | A substitute for fault-current coordination, electrical isolation or post-short-circuit equipment inspection |

**DOC-CONFIRMED:** Type II's permitted contact-welding possibility contradicts the shortcut **'Type II means no contact can weld'**. **INFERENCE:** after a short circuit, a faulted contactor could have changed its main-contact or mirror-feedback condition, and even a correct electrical reset cannot prove Y1/Y2 gravity restraint. No claim that such a failure has occurred on OpenPressBrake.

## Adversarial repair paths
1. **Fuse replaced; power comes back:** The fault cause and affected conductors/insulation/contactor/overload may be unresolved. Qualified electrical isolation and post-fault inspection precede reenergization; a new fuse alone is not a proof.
2. **'Type II' nameplate but substitute breaker:** Manufacturer's ATS48 FAQ makes the protection combination conditional. A different interrupting rating or mechanically compatible device does not inherit the original coordination test. Reassess SCCR, Type II claim and any dependent safety functions.
3. **Welded power pole with safety output OFF:** Correct mirror EDM can inhibit a future rearm if selected and wired properly; it cannot retroactively open the welded pole or prove that hazardous energy is absent. Inspect the actual final element and independent energy path.
4. **Overload relay appears resettable:** Type II tests include overload calibration and dielectric checks on the coordinated combination. Do not infer this installed unit passed post-fault acceptance merely because a reset button moves.
5. **Normal controller restarted:** LinuxCNC/HAL/ordinary FPGA or a contactor-driver IC fault bit cannot independently prove correct short-circuit protection, safety-rated stop, pressure release, gravity holding or a fresh deliberate start.
6. **Urgent production:** A cumbersome maintenance approval encourages 'replace fuse and press reset'. Provide an easy documented qualified post-fault workflow, exact replacement kits, clear fault-cause and affected-circuit labeling, and protected diagnostics. A safety circuit that needs frequent jumpering is a design problem, not an operator solution.

## Machine-class transfer and human factors
- **Press brake:** motor/pump contactor state is not proof of Y1/Y2 physical hold; preserve independent Pilz/AKAS and hydraulic final elements, service blocking and deliberate restart.
- **Mill/lathe:** even if motor power is removed, spindle coast, chuck/workholding and toolchanger pneumatic/hydraulic energy may remain.
- **Robot/cell:** a restored electrical feed and cleared safety PLC fault do not establish an empty safeguarded area or permit automatic restart.
- **Plasma/laser:** high-voltage supply, gas, extraction and motion may have different protective devices/energy isolation requirements.

## Required selected evidence and release gate
**AUTH/DEP:** identify exact short-circuit protective device, contactor, overload/drive, supply and manufacturer coordination table/conditions; reconcile actual prospective fault current, panel SCCR and separate safety output allocation. **CHG:** any replacement of SCPD, contactor, auxiliary, overload, fuse type, wiring or panel supply can invalidate previously accepted coordination and EDM evidence. **VAL/PHY:** qualified isolation, cause-of-fault investigation, inspection/testing under manufacturer procedure, correct mirror feedback, actual final-element energy interruption, hydraulic/mechanical safe-state proof, area clearance and separate fresh start. **UNKNOWN:** selected OpenPressBrake actual installed combination, fault-current data, protection coordination, K1/K2, hydraulic stop/hold and independent evaluator.

## Rockwell original PDF page-image verification — distinct Type II substitution counterexample
**2026-10-28 direct primary-source upgrade:** Opened [Rockwell 140M-TD002G-EN-P January 2018 original PDF](https://literature.rockwellautomation.com/idc/groups/literature/documents/td/140m-td002_-en-p.pdf), **printed p. 31 / PDF page index 30**, and visually checked the table/footnote. The Type II definition allows limited, easily separable contact welding; the **400 V** Type II table names **100-C12** as the minimum for a particular **140M-C2E-C16 / 140M-C2T-C10** row, and explicitly footnotes **100-C16 contactors Type I only** for that row. **DOC-CONFIRMED** for that *named historical Rockwell combination*, not evidence that all larger contactors are worse, that every 100-C16 is Type I in all applications, or that OpenPressBrake uses this equipment. This is an unusually clear **wrong-spare despite plausible size** example. Preserve exact voltage, starter/contactor combination and manufacturer edition in any actual selection. Rockwell's original page also distinguishes UL Type E/Type F controller classifications from IEC Type I/II coordination; do not collapse the labels.

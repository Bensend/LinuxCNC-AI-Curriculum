# 4400-VAC-LIFT-01 — E-stop may release a vacuum-held load: Schmalz FMP(-S) versus Piab piSAFE/VSV

**2026-10-29 | original manufacturer document + current OEM product pages + scope-limited OSHA | NON-GRADUATED / documentary only.** This is a 4000 safety-course source investigation, not an installed OpenPressBrake finding or a new scored competency. No physical experiment or machine actuation.

## Research question and novelty
If a robot, sheet feeder, overhead manipulator or CNC cell carries material by suction, does removing electrical power or compressed air during E-stop **retain** the payload or **drop** it? Is a green vacuum-switch indication proof of safe load holding?

Direct scans of the full 383,856-character canonical safety index and current progress/research found existing 2570 STO/braking, 2580 stored-energy/gravity, 2590 guarding, hydraulic hose, accumulator and air-exhaust branches, but no dedicated **vacuum payload retention / seal-loss / intentional-release** mechanism. This is a new *physical failure-path transfer* within existing 2520/2580/25F0 competencies, not evidence of a new independent certification or graduation.

## Causal failure paths (not interchangeable)
| Initiating event | Ordinary control observation | Possible physical outcome | What remains unproven |
|---|---|---|---|
| E-stop removes vacuum generator supply on **Schmalz FMP(-S)** example | Robot/CNC reports stop; vacuum-command OFF | OEM warns suction collapses and load can fall | Actual installed generator/valve/payload, drop time, guarding |
| E-stop removes compressed air from FMP-S pilot | Solenoid state/24 V may look safe | OEM warns pilot valve can close and load release | Actual pneumatic layout, stored air, physical load path |
| Power fails with **Piab piSAFE/VSV**, sealed workpiece and correctly selected non-return path | Pump OFF; vacuum status may still be high | OEM claims conditional retained vacuum | Leak rate, seal, release-valve configuration, endurance, physical load security |
| Porous, warped, oily or partly covered workpiece; cup damage | Some cups/pressure sensors may still indicate vacuum | Leakage or unequal load sharing may release/slip payload | Real seal integrity, load center, dynamic acceleration, suction area, redundancy |
| Intentional blow-off/atmospheric release commanded during a fault or reset | Ordinary output transition appears successful | Payload may release even if motion is stopped | Independent release authorization and mechanical support |
| Vacuum sensing wire/logic stale, disconnected or powered from failed rail | Green HMI `VACUUM_OK` remains displayed | No assured retention or person clearance | Sensor fault behavior, freshness, independent controller and physical witness |

**INFERENCE:** A stop that removes dangerous robot motion can simultaneously create a **falling-object** hazard. Retaining vacuum may be the correct interim safe action for a particular machine, but maintaining suction energy also creates service/isolation risks. The safety function must therefore specify **both** controlled motion stop and load securement, with a qualified route to lower/land/support the load and then isolate all stored energy. Neither “dump everything” nor “always keep vacuum on” is a universal safe state.

## Practical low-bypass architecture (conditional engineering proposal)
- **Boundary:** Identify workpiece mass, center of gravity, surface/porosity, orientation, acceleration, height, drop zone, other reachable pinch zones, and all modes (normal, teach, E-stop, power failure, air loss, fault, restart, maintenance). These are design inputs, not values supplied here.
- **Normal control:** LinuxCNC/FPGA/robot motion and pick/release sequences; ordinary vacuum gauge, alarm, leak trend, HMI. They do not independently authorize human entry.
- **Safety-related decision:** A suitably engineered independent safety controller evaluates actual guard/occupancy and the machine-specific safety functions. The safety requirements must cover whether a protective stop may leave the load suspended, how release is inhibited, and what happens on loss of the safety system itself. No safety integrity level is assigned here.
- **Physical final elements:** Selected non-return/reserve circuit, approved retention or secondary mechanical support, guarded/excluded drop zone, correctly rated handling structure, and controlled means to land the load. A check valve is not itself proof of holding for porous/partial seals. A vacuum switch is not the physical final element.
- **Reset/rearm:** Clearing an E-stop, restoring compressed air or power, reconnecting a vacuum line, or receiving `VACUUM_OK` must not automatically restart travel or trigger blow-off. Require hazard clearance and a **new deliberate start**, separately from safety reset and load-recovery authority.
- **Human factors:** Keep guards hinged/captive and clear; make load-support landing positions accessible; provide a plainly labeled vacuum-fault recovery procedure; do not demand personnel enter a drop zone to reset, re-seat a cup or retrieve a dropped sheet. Design out repeated nuisance vacuum alarms rather than inviting bypass.
- **Hard minimum:** If the machine cannot demonstrate credible load retention **or** reliably exclude people from the entire drop/strike zone under its foreseeable faults, **do not operate it with people exposed**. Any experimental motion must first be independently authorized, isolated/remote, and conducted with all people outside the danger zone.

## Same-session original Piab manual deepening — release synchronization and leakage are not a safe-hold clock

**DOC-CONFIRMED original manufacturer PDF, directly opened and visually inspected**: Piab, *piSAFE Vacuum Safety Valve for Centralized Systems*, file `0233044_rev03_pisafe_centralizedvsv_en.pdf`, ©2024, 36 pages: https://www.piab.com/globalassets/productimages/0233044_rev03_pisafe_centralizedvsv_en.pdf . The exact whole-file byte hash was not retained.

- **Printed p.6, §2.1–2.2:** Piab explicitly leaves complete robot-system safety and integrator approval to the system integrator, warns about crushing/entrapment and directs designers to consider E-stop placement. This confirms that the product's `safety` name and retention function do **not** establish the entire machine's safety function.
- **Printed p.9, §3.4:** retention is expressly conditional on a **sealed application** and a **release signal**; the VSV and decentralized piSAFE pump have **different manuals**. Do not substitute this central-VSV manual for the decentralized unit.
- **Printed p.13, §4.3:** central VSV supports Blow-Off (BO) or normally closed Atmospheric Quick Release (AQR NC), while VSV Mini has BO only. The exact installed cartridge and whether release/retention plumbing is separate or common must be identified; an ordinary HMI release bit cannot be a safe-load witness.
- **Printed p.23, §6.4:** the **common vacuum-and-release-port** configuration has a product-specific maximum of eight VSV units; the OEM says long hoses can slow release and unequal daisy-chained hose lengths can make multiple cups **release out of sync**. This creates a **second physical hazard**: a sheet may peel or rotate unexpectedly during intentional release, even though the robot is motion-stopped. Do not generalize eight to other configurations or claim a machine release time.
- **Printed p.30, §8.1:** the specified **leaking rate <0.75 kPa/min** is measured **toward a 10-mL volume** under the manual's stated test conditions. It is **not** a demonstrated workpiece holding time, not a vacuum decay curve for a large/porous sheet and not a safety-rated stop-time bound. No numerical hold time is inferred.
- **Printed pp.7, 25–29:** OEM calls for turning off electrical and air supplies and discharging residual pressure for maintenance, and allows quick-connect removal for service at a distance from the tooling arm. **INFERENCE:** a convenient service path is a useful anti-bypass feature, but only after the load is landed/positively supported and the hazard is controlled; quick removal under a suspended payload is not authorized.
- **Printed p.22:** vacuum switch is **optional** and is connected to a sensing port; a vacuum indication is still not proof of the load's center-of-gravity security or all-cup adhesion.

**New frozen design questions for a selected multi-cup system:** Are BO and AQR NC circuits separate or shared? Can a single misrouted release line energize multiple cups? Are cup release times asymmetric with hose length? Does the chosen mechanical support keep the payload secure through an incomplete or asymmetric release? These are **UNKNOWN** without selected OEM configuration and physical evidence. No simulation or unsafe release test is justified from generic documentation alone.

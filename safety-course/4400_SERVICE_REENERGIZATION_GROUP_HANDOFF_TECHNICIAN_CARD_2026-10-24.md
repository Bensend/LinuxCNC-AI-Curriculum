# 4400 — Service reenergization and group handoff: 12 technician scenarios
**2026-10-24 | PAPER ONLY / NON-BLIND / NOT A SELECTED-MACHINE PROCEDURE**

Read [original OSHA transition source audit](../research/4400_SERVICE_TEST_POSITION_GROUP_LOCKOUT_REENTRY_TRANSITION_AUDIT_2026-10-24.md) and [BLK-01 module](4400_BLK_01_GRAVITY_RESTRAINT_DYNAMIC_FORCE_AND_LOTO_MODULE_2026-10-24.md). For each case identify: (i) phase, (ii) people exposed, (iii) energy/physical support still uncontrolled, (iv) source/authority, (v) required re-verification, (vi) machine-specific UNKNOWNs, (vii) whether work may proceed.

1. Technician A owns the lock, technician B works under the ram without a personal lock. B asks A to jog.
2. Both workers withdraw, but one wrench remains in the tooling area before the temporary powered test.
3. A technician removes electrical LOTO, runs one positioning move, then returns under the ram without restoring hydraulic isolation.
4. A slide pin was engaged before the powered positioning move. Its engagement bit still reads ON afterward.
5. Contractor uses a lockbox, plant technician uses a separate isolation list; both believe the other isolated hydraulic pressure.
6. Night shift starts while day shift leaves; the machine remains under maintenance and the only lock belongs to a departing worker.
7. A low pressure gauge at the pump is used to justify entry while cylinder/accumulator volumes have not been mapped.
8. A 'safe speed' mode is selected, but a hydraulic hose leak could lower the ram.
9. An operator presses E-stop during the positioning test and assumes that is sufficient for the next hands-in service phase.
10. The qualified procedure requires clearing people before temporary energization, but the HMI 'area clear' checkbox was last set yesterday.
11. After servicing, the guard closes and the normal CNC queue still contains a cycle-start command.
12. A heavy ram block is stored far away and the shop routinely substitutes a loose wooden spacer. Propose a safer, easier-to-use design without inventing structural capacity.

Do not self-grade as a blind challenge. Critical errors include accepting any ordinary HMI bit as physical isolation, omitting personal control, assuming static support resists powered force, or authorizing hands-in work during temporary energized positioning. Record confidence and falsifying evidence; external information-separated evaluation remains open.

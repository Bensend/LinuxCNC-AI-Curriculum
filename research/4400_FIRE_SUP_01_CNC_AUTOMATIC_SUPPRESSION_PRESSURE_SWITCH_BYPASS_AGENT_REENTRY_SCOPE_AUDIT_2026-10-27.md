# 4400-FIRE-SUP-01 — CNC automatic fire suppression: pneumatic detection, optional machine shutdown, bypass after discharge, and safe re-entry

**2026-10-27 | Original 4400 safety source audit | NON-GRADUATED / DOCUMENTARY ONLY.** Applies conditionally to enclosed mills, Swiss lathes, grinders, EDM and automated cells with combustible coolant or other fire exposure. No installed OpenPressBrake fire-suppression system is asserted. No physical test, blind score, fire test, simulation or machine release.

## Bounded engineering question and pre-source prediction

A self-contained CNC enclosure fire system detects a fire and releases agent even when CNC power is lost. Can the LinuxCNC/HAL machine-stop bit, an optional cylinder pressure switch, the extinguished flame, or a recharged cylinder independently authorize restarting the spindle or opening the enclosure? **Prediction (INFERENCE): no.** Distinguish independent detection/agent discharge, ordinary machine/process shutdown requests, agent inventory and impairment, the actual fire state, airborne agent/decomposition products, safe access, and qualified restart. A fire-system accessory contact is not a certified machine personnel-safety final element unless the selected assembly and function are qualified.

**Falsifier sought:** a documented OEM safety-rated fire-shutdown/restart architecture with selected physical machine validation could change the authority allocation for that *specific* chain. No such machine-specific proof was found. A pressure switch triggering an ordinary E-stop request does not establish a safety-rated electrical path or a safe enclosure atmosphere.

## Safety function and physical authority decomposition

| Layer / proposition | Legitimate evidence | False shortcut |
|---|---|---|
| **Detect** — fire/heat reaches selected tubing or sensor | Selected detector routing, coverage, condition, manufacturer qualification | "Machine power off" or one tube means every ignition zone is protected. |
| **Discharge** — agent delivered into the intended hazard | Selected agent, cylinder mass/pressure, valve/tube/nozzle integrity, enclosure leakage, service record | Pneumatic trigger or discharge switch alone proves flame extinguished. |
| **Process stop** — avoid continued fuel/ignition feed | Qualified shutdown design for spindle, coolant/oil pump, mist collector and relevant process; actual machine state | Optional pressure switch wired into ordinary LinuxCNC E-stop/HAL is a safety-rated final element. |
| **Independent machine protection** — no unexpected hazardous motion | Retained independent machine safety circuit and physical final elements | Fire relay replaces E-stop, guard or motion safety chain. |
| **Atmosphere/access** — people can safely approach/open/enter | Agent-specific qualified ventilation, atmospheric assessment where indicated, fire-service/entry control, physical isolation | Flame out, green HMI or elapsed arbitrary minutes means air safe. |
| **Rearm** — suppressor and machine independently restored | Correct refill/replace/inspect, root cause corrected, verified unbypassed interfaces, approved fresh reset/start | Shorting pressure switch, toggling CNC reset or replacing only a cylinder closes the incident. |

**Conceptual sequence, not installed logic:** `ARMED -> FIRE_DETECTED -> AGENT_RELEASED -> MACHINE_STOP_REQUESTED -> DISCHARGED/IMPAIRED + ACCESS_RESTRICTED -> QUALIFIED_FIRE/ATMOSPHERE/MECHANICAL/PROCESS_REVIEW -> SYSTEM_RESTORED -> DELIBERATE_FRESH_START`. The fire and motion state machines may advance asynchronously; a completed machine stop cannot prove the fire safe, and successful fire suppression cannot prove mechanical safe-state. A qualified design may intentionally use different sequences, but must preserve these independent propositions.

## Adversarial failure-path review

1. **Loss of CNC power:** independent pneumatic discharge may remain possible (F1), but an *electrical* machine shutdown interface, annunciator, fan, valve or telemetry may lose power. Verify fail state and power domain for each. Do not assume all interfaces survive.
2. **Detection tube cut, disconnected or depressurized during service:** can discharge or impair depending on the exact system; a cylinder gauge alone cannot prove complete detector/nozzle coverage. Preserve the selected manufacturer's service isolation/rearm process.
3. **Pressure switch opens after discharge:** CNC sees a stop request, but neither switch contacts nor HAL prove agent delivered, fire out or the air safe. A wire short, stuck contact or disabled interlock could permit restart without a charged system.
4. **Mist-collector shutdown tradeoff:** stopping extraction may help agent retention or reduce fire propagation, but could also change smoke/agent movement. The correct fan/damper strategy requires selected fire-system design; never infer that all fans should always run or always stop.
5. **Fire reignition:** coolant pump, oil mist, hot tooling, tool-placement defect, chip accumulation and incomplete process stop can reignite even after visible flame disappears. Treat root-cause repair as separate from recharge.
6. **Operator bypass after an expensive discharge:** F3 and F4 show opposite reported shop decisions. The bypass incentive is not hypothetical; neither report establishes how the switches were wired. Design accessible spare agent cartridges/cylinders, clear IMPAIRED state, local service availability and a planned safe production alternative rather than relying on warnings.
7. **Agent substitution:** CO2, HFC-227ea, FK-5-1-12 and Class D powder have different approved applications, atmospheric and residue effects. A drop-in replacement without hazard/approval and human-exposure review invalidates prior proof.
8. **Door opened immediately after discharge:** enclosure breach may reintroduce oxygen or expose workers to smoke, CO2 or decomposition products. Do not instruct operators to open the door to inspect or hand-fight an active fire; emergency response and re-entry must follow the actual hazard/agent plan.
9. **False assurance from a single pressure indicator:** cylinder pressure may indicate inventory under limited conditions, not tube routing, nozzle obstruction, actual agent concentration, fire extinguishment or safe personnel access.
10. **Common-cause power/logic:** one PLC/HAL signal that both claims "suppression healthy" and commands a normal reset can hide stale or bypassed state. Fire-system impairment should remain visible and be independently reviewed before restart.

## Practical design and human factors

A useful retrofit pattern is a self-contained, appropriately approved fire detection/discharge system with **separate** fire-discharge/impairment indication and a selected machine shutdown interface, while the independent machine safety chain remains intact. The interface should be accessible for qualified verification, labeled, protected from accidental defeat, and make a discharged/unavailable suppressor conspicuous. **Do not** silently grant personnel-safety integrity to an unqualified accessory contact.

Make recharging easier than bypassing: stock the correct approved replacement, arrange local qualified service, give maintenance a single-page impairment and restart workflow, and offer an authorized alternative machine/process during downtime. Prefer a physical keyed service procedure and change record over a hidden "temporarily ignore pressure switch" software setting. Design maintenance access to avoid leaning into a smoke/agent-filled enclosure. If protection is required for the risk reduction case, operating after impairment without a qualified alternative is not acceptable; if minimum attended-operation protection is absent, no personnel-exposed operation.

## Independent exact-manufacturer manual depth — controlled bypass is an OEM variant, not a license to run unprotected

**Direct original PDF inspected:** Firetrace [Dual Pressure Switch Module Installation Manual, DIOM 800095-A, revision A, 2022-07-22](https://www.firetrace.com/hubfs/instruction-manual/PressureSwitchModule_InsallationGuide.pdf?hsLang=en), 18 pages, manufacturer-hosted and linked from [Firetrace's manual catalogue](https://www.firetrace.com/manual). **DOC-CONFIRMED exact OEM; published 2022, newly inspected by this course on 2026-10-27.** The manual **explicitly offers** a dual pressure-switch module **with a bypass key switch** and a standard version without bypass (§§2.1–2.2). The two contacts can be configured as supervisory + activation or two activation switches. This corrects any simplistic reading of the first-pass audit suggesting that *every* physical bypass provision is necessarily an illicit modification.

**The decisive mechanism:** §3.2 Table 2 enumerates 16 distinct keyed-variant configurations, including (a) bypass activation **and** service, (b) bypass activation but **force service state**, (c) bypass activation only, and (d) **no bypass**. Table 3 has four no-bypass standard configurations. §3.3 Tables 4–5 explicitly distinguish **normal/bypass**, **pressurized/unpressurized** and each switch's open/closed contact state. Thus a keyed bypass position does **not** have one universal electrical meaning; the chosen configuration and downstream wiring determine whether service indication remains visible, whether an ordinary stop request is suppressed, and whether the machine can restart. The 2022 manual's listed **24 V / 2 A** are module electrical limits only, not machine safety integrity or contactor ratings. **No PL/SIL or personnel-safety function was claimed.**

**Source-level discriminator and human-factor correction:** A service-oriented key bypass may be intentionally supported by the OEM; a shop *using* that key, defeating a wire or changing a controller parameter to resume **production with an uncharged system** still needs a separately justified machine/fire risk decision and cannot be assumed safe. An attractive architecture for review is an **activation bypass that forces a separate visible service/IMPAIRED signal**, with ordinary automatic production inhibited while the fire protection is unavailable. This is **INFERENCE/design candidate**, not a Firetrace-mandated wiring diagram or a certified interlock. Key possession alone does not prove authorization, state monitoring, access exclusion or safe restart. Keep the legitimate maintenance task easy without silently making full production possible under impairment.

**Physical witness addition:** for FS-3/FS-4, capture exact selected module/kit part, **2022 manual revision or newer applicable manual**, 16-option configuration identity, whether key bypass is present, which switch is activation versus supervisory, and which independent downstream input actually observes **SERVICE/IMPAIRED**. A contact continuity table is a component check, **not** proof of suppression delivery, machine stop, or safe atmosphere. FS-5 must preserve qualified installed configuration/inspection and post-discharge service record. §4.2 says an **authorized Firetrace distributor must be consulted after discharge**, and the suppression units must be removed/recharged under their specific manuals; this course does not reproduce cylinder-handling steps or authorize field servicing.

**Legacy drawing fence:** [Firetrace technical bulletin TB041 dated 2020-02-28](https://www.firetrace.com/hubfs/Dual_Switch_Wiring_Diagram.pdf?hsLang=en) shows an older dual-switch/solenoid wiring example for P/N 600405-24, 600605-24 and 600416-24. It is **not** the 2022 800095-A configurable module and must not be used as its pinout or pressure-setpoint oracle. Selected installed model remains UNKNOWN.

**Second-source agent scope:** [US EPA SNAP total-flooding agent table](https://www.epa.gov/snap/substitutes-total-flooding-agents) lists HFC-227ea/FM-200 as an acceptable agent in that end-use with NFPA 2001 safety guidance; the [EPA fire-suppression overview](https://www.epa.gov/snap/substitutes-fire-suppression-and-explosion-protection) warns that later technology-transition restrictions may apply. An EPA agent listing is **not** approval of an arbitrary CNC installation, a worker-safe post-fire atmosphere or proof of current product availability. Product and edition-specific agent approval still required.

### Independent physical-observability correction — tube pressure is not agent inventory

The original Firetrace DIOM 800095-A §3.7 (pp. 11–12) places a **pressure gauge at the end-of-line adapter of the pneumatic detection tubing** and uses it to check the **detection network's** pressure/leakage before activation. That is a *different physical zone* from the extinguishing-agent cylinder, nozzle flow and protected-space concentration. **DOC-CONFIRMED exact OEM:** `DETECTION_TUBE_PRESSURIZED` does **not** by itself establish `AGENT_CYLINDER_CHARGED` or `AGENT_DELIVERED`. Conversely, a cylinder pressure/weight indication cannot establish that the detection tube is correctly routed, pressurized and leak-free. §4.1 separately calls for inspection of tube damage/obstruction, pressure gauges, electrical connections and changes to the protected equipment. This changes FS-2 from a single "pressure OK" field into **separate tube pressure/coverage and agent inventory/discharge** evidence. No pressure or leak-test value from the manual is adopted as a universal CNC acceptance threshold; no field test is authorized.

## Exact extinguishing-system family source — a machine enclosure *can* be total flooding

**DOC-CONFIRMED direct OEM:** Firetrace [Pre-engineered Automatic Indirect HFC-227ea Manual P/N 800022-010, dated 2016-07-27](https://www.firetrace.com/hubfs/DIOMs/800022-010-DIOM-MANUAL-ILP-HFC-227EA.pdf?hsLang=en), original manufacturer-hosted **52-page** manual, §§1–4. This source **corrects any overbroad "CNC object protection is never total flooding" inference**: this particular Firetrace ILP system is explicitly designed for **total flooding within a normally unoccupied hazard enclosure**, and lists CNC/VMC machining centres among candidate applications (§3.1). Whether OSHA's workplace pre-discharge and exposure provisions apply depends on the actual installation/exposure and legal scope; "total flooding within an enclosure" does not automatically mean the entire occupied shop room is the discharge area.

**Exact selected-product physical requirements, not transferable numeric ratings:** §§4.3–4.6 require hazard survey, enclosure dimensions/temperature, fuel-specific agent concentration, enclosure integrity, selected cylinder/nozzle/piping limits, personnel exposure and auxiliary shutdown. §4.4.2 explicitly requires provisions to **close enclosure openings and shut off ventilation at discharge** for this system. This is stronger than the first-pass generic warning that fan response is design-specific: for *this exact family*, fan/opening closure is a documented design condition. Do **not** transfer that to other agents/systems or conclude that a CNC ordinary mist-collector output automatically accomplishes it. One machine enclosure may have leakage, a mist-extraction duct and a room exhaust with different roles.

**Material incompatibility discriminator:** §3.1 and §4.5 state that this HFC-227ea system should **not** be used where reactive metals including **magnesium, titanium and zirconium** may be present, alongside metal hydrides and other listed materials. The two Firetrace customer case stories about machining titanium/steel with **oil-based coolant fires** are not proof that the same HFC-227ea agent can extinguish a burning titanium chip bed. The exact case agent was not independently verified. FS-1 must identify **what burns** (coolant vs reactive metal), not just the workpiece material or an agent marketing category. This is an exact new link to METAL-01, not a re-teaching of generic Class D.

**Two independent pressure indicators in this exact family:** §3.2.1 describes a **cylinder-mounted pressure gauge**; §5.4.3 and the 2022 dual-switch module describe a **detection-tube end-of-line gauge**. They observe different physical zones. Neither proves correct nozzle flow or achieved design concentration. The 2016 manual also describes a pressure switch as an **optional** machine shutdown/notification interface (§3.2.3). Its original manufacturer-tested UL/FM product-family design limits do not certify an unspecified retrofit installation or safety-related CNC stop.

**Decomposition/exposure:** §2.1.2 states HFC-227ea can form halogen-acid decomposition products when exposed to sufficiently high temperatures; the manufacturer expects rapid discharge to reduce formation. This is an agent-specific post-fire access concern, not a measured concentration. The manual is **2016** (not current proof of edition validity) and references NFPA 2001 **2015**; selected current approvals/editions require separate review. No actual fire/agent testing justified.

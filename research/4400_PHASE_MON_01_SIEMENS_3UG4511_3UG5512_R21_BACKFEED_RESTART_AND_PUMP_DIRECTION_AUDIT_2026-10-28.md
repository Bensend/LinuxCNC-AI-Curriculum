# 4400-PHASE-MON-01 — Phase monitoring, load-backfeed blind spots, exact safety-rated variant and pump rotation
**2026-10-28 | original manufacturer documents, source-depth safety audit | NON-GRADUATED / DOCUMENTARY ONLY**

## Engineering question and novelty
After three-phase wiring is serviced, is a healthy phase-monitor LED sufficient to establish correct hydraulic pump rotation, reliable single-phase-loss detection, independent personnel-safe stopping and deliberate restart? **No.** The precise increment is (a) a manufacturer-documented phase-loss *false negative* caused by motor/load voltage backfeed in a particular monitor; (b) the exact suffix distinction between ordinary 3UG5 line monitoring and the documented **3UG5512-..R21** safety-function variants; (c) automatic re-energization of a monitor output on restoration; and (d) independent pump-shaft rotation/recommissioning evidence. This is not another generic E-stop or hydraulic temperature lesson.

## Causal failure-path analysis (paper only)
| Case | Trigger / observation | What can fail | Required independent proposition |
|---|---|---|---|
| PM-01 | One supply phase opens; motor/load backfeeds monitor input; 3UG4511 green/relay remains plausible | A sequence-only relay cannot be credited with robust phase-loss detection. | Verify exact order code and detection scope, supply and motor terminals, independent final-element behavior; do not infer from LED. |
| PM-02 | Upstream L1/L2/L3 remains correct, but motor or pump wiring/coupling is altered downstream | Monitor sees correct *upstream* sequence yet shaft direction can be wrong. | Qualified **physical rotation witness** at selected motor/pump after safe recommissioning; verify actual load path. |
| PM-03 | Phase failure clears and 3UG5512-..R21 output auto-resets | An automatic output reclose may restore permissive while a start demand remains latched. | Independent safety reset/rearm, area-clear/guard checks and **fresh deliberate start**, no auto motion. |
| PM-04 | Monitor trips a contactor but its main contacts weld or another supply path exists | Output OFF does not prove hazardous energy removed. | Correct final-element feedback plus independent physical stop/hold and stored-energy witnesses. |
| PM-05 | Hydraulic pump motor is stopped for phase fault | Ram can still move under gravity, trapped pressure, stored energy or valve faults. | Independently engineered Y1/Y2 stopping and **gravity-load holding**; maintenance isolation distinct. |
| PM-06 | A generic 3UG5 -..R20 or 3UG4 is substituted for -..R21 | Similar housing/function may not preserve claimed functional-safety rating. | Exact order-code/suffix, approved safety data and impact analysis before replacement. |
| PM-07 | Phase-monitor healthy, but downstream VFD output or motor wiring changes | Line-side phase sequence is not a measurement of actual shaft rotation or drive output phase order. | Selected drive and motor topology, approved commissioning evidence and physical direction witness. |

## Architecture and human factors
**Normal control:** LinuxCNC/HAL/FPGA may display phase-fault status, inhibit routine starts and log diagnostic events. **Safety-related control:** where the hazard assessment actually requires it, a *qualified exact-variant* monitor and independent safety architecture determine a safety-related stop/rearm demand; they do not inherit authority from LinuxCNC. **Physical energy:** selected contactors, motor power, hydraulic valves, independent load-holding restraints and isolation points establish what really stops/holds. A phase-monitor output is a signal, not a pressure gauge, pump-direction sensor or ram restraint.

Make the safer path easier: permanently labeled L1/L2/L3 and motor/pump direction, captive variant/suffix and terminal map, accessible read-only phase fault indication, approved service wiring diagrams, preprinted post-motor-work return-to-service checklist and **no “jumper the monitor to get running” workaround**. Keep a known-good spare exact variant; do not demand an energized test from an unqualified operator. Wrong-phase repair should not automatically start a machine.

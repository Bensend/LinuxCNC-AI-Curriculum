# 4400 — Press-brake guarding authority, historical “safe distance,” and maintenance-state transfer trap
**Original source-reconciliation research | 2026-10-23 | status: documentary, NOT machine validation.**

## Question and consequence
Can a LinuxCNC/OpenPressBrake designer transfer mechanical-power-press brake/guard formulas, a historical OSHA “safe distance,” or an HMI stop indication into a hydraulic press-brake personnel-safety claim? **No.** Three different questions must be answered separately: which requirements apply to the machine/task; which protective function is physically provided; and what energy must be isolated for servicing. This paper addresses *source applicability* only, not legal certification or a selected machine's conformity.

**Source hierarchy:** regulatory text > applicable current directives/interpretations for enforcement context > manufacturer application guidance > engineering inference. ANSI B11.3 edition and any state-plan requirements require a separate **current selected-site** review. The OSHA directive's 1997 date does not make its numeric examples a design approval for a 2026 retrofit.

## Causal safety and standards-transfer failure paths
1. **Wrong machine class:** Engineer copies a 1910.217 mechanical-press braking/guard formula into a hydraulic press brake, then announces compliance. The premise fails at O-217; any usable engineering method still needs its own applicable standard, actual response times and installed geometry. **Do not invent distances or use an unrelated 1910.217 formula as the acceptance oracle.**
2. **Stale historical minimum:** Technician sees 6 inches in the 1986 letter, or 4 inches in the 1997 directive, and programs an HMI “safe distance.” The 1986 page itself points to a later policy; the 1997 exception has substantial feasibility, production and procedural conditions. Neither number is a default modern safeguarding specification. **UNKNOWN:** site/edition/current exception applicability, hand/sheet/side access and actual stopping.
3. **Safe distance confused with stop distance:** Hand clearance from the die in a narrowly allowed task is not the approach-distance calculation for a protective device, and neither is evidence that a ram stops/holds before reach. A changing approach/bending/return mode can change the hazard. **INFERENCE** from O-1997/O-212 and machine safety method.
4. **Point-of-operation-only tunnel vision:** A correctly guarded front die does not eliminate rear/backgauge, sheet sweep, ejection, gravity ram, tool change, trapped-person or maintenance energy hazards. A guard defeat that makes routine loading faster is a design usability defect requiring redesign, not an acceptable workaround. **INFERENCE** requiring machine-specific hazard mapping.
5. **Production stop confused with lockout:** LinuxCNC “Machine Off,” FPGA inhibit, a guard stop, a safety relay healthy bit, or a locked HMI is not necessarily an energy-isolating device. An elevated ram, accumulators, trapped pilot/cylinder volumes and reaccumulation remain separate propositions under O-147. **DOC-CONFIRMED** obligation; physical topology UNKNOWN.
6. **Test mode confused with access permission:** A servicing technician requests an energized brake/valve test. OSHA 1910.147(f)(1), when applicable, distinguishes controlled temporary reenergization from resumed servicing; physical personnel exclusion and return to isolation must be designed before the test. **DOC-CONFIRMED** procedural boundary; no physical test authorized here.

## Design contract for selected-machine qualification
- **SCOPE:** record machine type, site jurisdiction, process/production mode, work task, applicable regulatory clauses, directive date/status, Type-C/ANSI edition and competent reviewer. Never copy a historical numeric example without a source-scoped applicability decision.
- **HAZ/ACCESS:** front/rear/side, die, backgauge, sheet swing, stored energy, maintenance, guard defeat and trapped-person escape. Define what people can actually reach in each mode.
- **SRS/PHY:** keep stop, load hold, access, escape, energy isolation and fresh deliberate restart separate; use the existing `IN-01..10` and `PHY-*` intake gates. A HMI green status, measured pressure at an unrelated port or a catalog “fail safe” label is not physical proof.
- **ENE:** map actual disconnects, accumulator/gas/fluid zones, pilot pressure, check/counterbalance paths, hose-to-actuator placement, mechanically restrained ram and safe test points. If reaccumulation is possible, plan continued verification by authorized staff. Do not prescribe a generic valve operation sequence without drawings.
- **HF:** make guarded loading, setup, troubleshooting and maintenance practical. Observe real task friction; revise guard placement, visibility, tooling access and fault recovery without transferring safety authority to LinuxCNC.
- **VAL/CHG:** select a competent independent reviewer, applicable machine-specific acceptance basis and qualified isolated/remote verification. Changed guard/laser/muting, control mode, tooling, valve or firmware invalidates dependent evidence until requalified.

## Adversarial counterexamples (paper only)
- “The 1910.217 brake has to self-engage, therefore our hydraulic press-brake valve is compliant.” **REJECT:** wrong machine class and wrong physical element.
- “OSHA says four inches, so the safety laser may be four inches from the die.” **REJECT:** directive's narrowly conditioned *operator safe-distance* alternative is not a universal presence-sensor placement rule.
- “Our shop is small and custom, so the exception automatically applies.” **REJECT:** infeasibility, production scope, training, history, supervision and applicable current authority all need proof.
- “We shut off the hydraulic pump and the gauge reads zero, so we can work under the ram.” **REJECT:** independently restrain load and verify each exposed energy zone; pressure can remain or reaccumulate elsewhere.
- “We reset the safety relay, so the cycle may resume.” **REJECT:** reset is not an ordinary fresh start or proof of safe physical state.

## Same-session adversarial source-text reconciliation
**DOC-CONFIRMED, source wording:** OSHA CPL 02-01-025 (1997) prints both **4 inches (10.16 cm)** and, later in the same paragraph, **4 inches (10.6 cm)**. These are inconsistent conversions, not two legitimate selected-machine tolerances. No physical limit is derived from either value here. The older 1986 interpretation's 6-inch language and its 2002 editorial redirection are further reasons to preserve *document date and scope* with every numerical claim. **INFERENCE:** blindly extracting a number from an official-looking page is an unsafe engineering workflow. **UNKNOWN:** current jurisdiction-specific applicability of any exceptional procedure.

**DOC-CONFIRMED, 2004-03-25 archived interpretation:** OSHA states laser guarding on hydraulic press brakes is not inherently prohibited under 1910.212, but explicitly makes effectiveness depend on the particular device, press and conditions, including whether the ram stops before contact. The page bears an **archive notice**; it is a historical source, not a present-day installation approval. This narrows rather than expands the original conclusion: a source can permit a technology in principle while providing **zero** selected-machine `PHY-STOP` or `PHY-ACCESS` evidence.

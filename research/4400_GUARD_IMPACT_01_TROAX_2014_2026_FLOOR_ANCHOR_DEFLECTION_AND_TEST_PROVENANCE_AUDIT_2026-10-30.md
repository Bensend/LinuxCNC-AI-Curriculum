# 4400-GUARD-IMPACT-01 — A fence can withstand an impact while deflecting into the hazard
**2026-10-30 | DIRECT ORIGINAL MANUFACTURER TEST SUMMARY + CURRENT PRODUCT SCOPE | NON-GRADUATED**

## Question and predecessor scan
For a CNC/robot/press-brake cell, does a published "1,600 J" or "2,100 J" guard rating prove that the installed fence, anchors and safety clearance protect people? **No.** The canonical safety index was scanned for `fenc*`, `floor anchor`, `impact test`, `deflection`, `Troax`, `Smart Fix`, `mesh panel` and `projectile containment`. Existing fixed-guard fastener and reach/guard-retention lessons remain predecessors; this is a **new original test-geometry and floor-anchorage evidence gap**, not a replacement for 2590/ISO 14120 or an OpenPressBrake as-built claim.

## Two important source-provenance/measurement traps
- **Anachronistic standards reference:** Troax report header/test dates are in **2014**, but its text says the pendulum method was per **ISO 14120:2015 Annex C**. A 2014 test cannot by itself demonstrate contemporaneous use of a not-yet-published 2015 edition. It may be a retrospectively updated report or reference, but **that explanation is INFERENCE and the document revision/history is UNKNOWN**. Do not claim ISO test-method compliance without manufacturer clarification and actual method records.
- **Deflection meaning:** "total deflection approximately 345–380 mm" is printed alongside "remaining deformation"; the one-page report does not provide full time-history or clearly distinguish peak transient excursion from residual deformation. Treat the reported number as an **OEM report observation**, not a universal minimum clearance or a validated maximum deflection envelope.

## Failure-path analysis
| Failure | Why headline impact rating is insufficient | Qualified witness needed |
|---|---|---|
| Panel survives but bows toward operator or moving tool | No penetration is not no hazardous intrusion; person could be within deflection envelope. | Actual hazard/people geometry, direction, dynamic clearance and tested configuration. |
| Retrofitter bolts posts to weak/slab-edge concrete | Report used M10×80 expander anchors in concrete; substrate, edge distance, embedment and installation torque unknown. | As-built anchor schedule, concrete condition, manufacturer installation requirements and competent structural verification. |
| Missing captive fasteners or altered post spacing | Published rating covers a particular panel/post/bracket/fixing system. | Actual post/panel/fastener assembly and inspection after service. |
| "2100 J" purchased as standalone mesh sheet | Current rating applies to a system/configuration, not a loose panel or arbitrary anchorage. | Exact current OEM test report, option/height/span/anchor matching. |
| Forklift barrier used as spindle ejection guard | Vehicle bumper and high-speed metal projectile differ in geometry, speed, contact and energy distribution. | Machine-specific hazard containment and guarding design; no generic energy-only equivalence. |
| Fence is struck, remains standing but bent | Deformation may create reach-through, shifted gaps, loose anchors and hidden damage. | Post-impact quarantine, replacement/inspection criteria and qualified recommissioning. |

## Human factors and minimum safe threshold
Use panels with **captive fasteners** and easy reinstallation, visible post/anchor inspection marks, straightforward damaged-panel replacement, and safe service gates so maintenance does not discard the fence. A guard that routinely blocks access needed for clearing a jam without a practical safe route invites defeat. Keep machine safety authority independent of LinuxCNC/FPGA; guard integrity and physical final-element stop/hold are distinct. If minimum safe-to-operate proof is missing, **do not operate with people exposed**; isolated/remote experimental operation requires separate approval and people outside danger zone.

No synthetic lab is justified; no workflow, physical test, machine operation or hosted/self-hosted compute.

Do not use any tabulated number as a universal separation distance, machine projectile rating, or OpenPressBrake release criterion.

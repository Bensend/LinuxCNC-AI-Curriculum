# 4400 CYL-STRUCT-03 — Rod buckling and side load despite acceptable hydraulic pressure
**Distinct safety research / NON-GRADUATED**, 2026-10-27. No selected OpenPressBrake physical conclusion.

## Novelty and question
A complete 963-line canonical learner index before this session had **zero** occurrences of `buckl`, `misalign`, `column strength`, `trunnion`, `Euler`, `piston rod` and `rod failure`. Existing HYD-HOLD/HOSE/TOOL/RIG lessons do not establish **structural column capacity and mounting alignment**. This branch asks: can a cylinder fail laterally under compression while its pressure remains below the nominal working limit, and can asymmetric Y1/Y2 loading create additional side load?

## Direct original manufacturer documentation
1. **Aggressive Hydraulics**, *Frequently Asked Questions*, original cylinder OEM, directly inspected 2026-10-27: https://www.aggressivehydraulics.com/resources/faqs/ (lines 123–142). **DOC-CONFIRMED:** column buckling is sudden unstable lateral deflection at a critical compressive load, potentially with stress below rod material yield; column capacity depends on rod size, stroke and total extended length. Manufacturer consultation required.
2. **Aggressive Hydraulics**, *How to Avoid Hydraulic Cylinder Misalignment*, original OEM, dated May 31, 2022 (page also displays November 19, 2025), directly inspected: https://www.aggressivehydraulics.com/how-to-avoid-hydraulic-cylinder-misalignment/ (lines 68–100). **DOC-CONFIRMED:** misalignment/side loading can produce bent rod, one-sided scuffing, uneven clevis/eye wear, bowing, damaged mounts/pins; mount selection, guiding and stop tubes are design-dependent, not universal remedies.
3. **Aggressive Hydraulics**, *Understanding Engineering Safety Factors in Custom Hydraulic Cylinders*, dated September 11, 2024 (page also displays November 19, 2025), directly inspected: https://www.aggressivehydraulics.com/understanding-engineering-safety-factors-in-custom-hydraulic-cylinders/ (lines 81–96). **DOC-CONFIRMED:** cylinder OEM checks distinct burst, tension and column-buckling modes; matching pressure rating alone is not proof of structural suitability.
4. **Enerpac**, *Safety Handbook for Hydraulic Cylinders*, directly inspected original manufacturer page: https://stagingtest.enerpac.com/en-US/resources/education/safety-handbook-for-hydraulic-cylinders (lines 3–22). **DOC-CONFIRMED for the covered portable lifting products:** centerline load, full saddle contact, avoiding side loading, positive blocking and never standing under a cylinder-supported load. Its suggested 80%-of-capacity/stroke **lifting setup rule is NOT a universal press-brake rod-buckling factor, PL/SIL or permitted working limit**.
5. **Enerpac**, *Hydraulic Cylinder Repair*, January 25, 2021, direct: https://blog.enerpac.com/hydraulic-cylinder-repair/ (lines 15–42). **DOC-CONFIRMED for its lifting equipment:** multi-point lifts can induce side loads, and valve sequencing can shift loads onto fewer cylinders. Not a measured OpenPressBrake Y1/Y2 behavior.
6. **Parker**, *Designing With Cylinders*, indexed PDF https://www.parker.com/parkerimages/mobilecylinder/cat/english/0001q.pdf describes column failure/side-load warnings, but direct PDF fetch returned **404**; do **not** treat indexed passages as directly inspected original clauses. **Aggressive Hydraulics** OEM evidence above independently supports the mechanism.

## Mechanism / explicit inference
For a compressed slender rod, column stability depends on **effective unsupported length, end conditions, bending stiffness, load eccentricity and alignment**, not just average axial stress or hydraulic pressure. Euler's ideal elastic-column expression `P_cr = pi² E I / (K L)²` is a **teaching relation only**, with ideal straight column, boundary factor `K`, and no imperfections; real cylinder design requires selected OEM methods including guide/stop-tube, rod extension, mounts, side load and fatigue. **No numeric buckling limit, pressure, stroke or Y1/Y2 load split is inferred.**

**INFERENCE:** If Y1 and Y2 do not share load as assumed, a ram could twist/side-load one rod or mount. A normal hydraulic pressure or axis-position display does not establish structural load sharing or safe gravity retention. Do not assume this failure occurs on the actual machine; its mechanism/geometry are UNKNOWN.

## Safety boundary and human factors
- **Normal control:** LinuxCNC/FPGA can coordinate position and report following error, but cannot certify a rod's structural capacity or an independent protective function.
- **Safety-related control:** independent stop/hold/guard/rearm functions must be designed and validated against the real load and failure paths.
- **Physical:** qualified cylinder, mounts/pins, guiding, frame/ram structure and positive mechanical supports must carry load. Stop/hold can fail mechanically despite valves being in their intended state.
- **Foreseeable defeat:** misalignment can make guards/maintenance difficult; do not grind pins, remove guides or defeat interlocks to gain access. Make alignment inspection, lubricating/replaceable pins and support/guard restoration convenient.
- **Change trigger:** cylinder/rod/mount/pin/guide/ram geometry, stroke, tooling/load distribution or Y1/Y2 synchronization change invalidates structural assumptions until reviewed.

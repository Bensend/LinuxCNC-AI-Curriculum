## Bounded question and predecessor comparison
What *actually* follows, and what does **not** follow, from loss of the retained 11K2-switched L7/L07 field domain in the proposed ordinary OpenPressBrake digital-output circuitry? In particular, may an FPGA-side low fault pin or a manufacturer isolator's “safe state” be treated as proof that a safety-controlled valve or the press-brake ram is safe?

Before consulting the original ST datasheets, **prediction (INFERENCE)**: field-side supply loss should make ordinary high-side outputs unavailable, but loss of a powered transmitter may force the receiving diagnostics to an inactive-looking value; an unpowered receiving side cannot be assumed to drive logic low. Independently, a switched-return fault may leave nonzero leakage. These are hypotheses about proposed ordinary I/O, not the installed machine.

**Novelty audit:** Existing [SNEAK-CUR-02](../safety-course/4400_SNEAK_CUR_02_LEAKAGE_BACKFEED_AND_FRESH_RESTART_FIELD_MODULE_2026-10-26.md) already teaches leakage, shared return, suppressor and coil-current paths; [OUT-01](4400_ROCKWELL_POINTMAX_SAFETY_OUTPUT_SA_BACKFEED_DETECTED_NOT_SAFE_OEM_AUDIT_2026-10-25.md) already teaches a detected safety-output backfeed that does not remove the load. [OPB-CUTOVER-02](4400_OPB_CUTOVER_02_PILZ_MUTE_GATE_POWER_DOMAIN_CROSS_REPOSITORY_SOURCE_AUDIT_2026-10-27.md) already identifies L7/L07, 11K2 and the separate Pilz-owned final elements. **Incremental contribution is not a new generic backfeed lesson:** exact STISO620/621 supply-loss truth tables and watchdog delay, exact IPS1025H ground/VCC-loss behavior, and an inspectable OpenPressBrake **FLT1/FLT2 channel-label contradiction**. ## Direct original manufacturer documents (DOC-CONFIRMED, exact edition)
1. STMicroelectronics **STISO621 DS13394 Rev 5, January 2025**, pp.4–5, 14–16, Tables 4/5/11, Figs.6/7: https://www.st.com/resource/en/datasheet/stiso621.pdf . With its receiver powered and opposite transmitter below UVLO, the receive output becomes logic LOW **after the isolator watchdog timeout**; a receiving side itself below UVLO is **UNKNOWN**, not a guaranteed low. Datasheet watchdog `tWD` is **2–6 microseconds**; power-up `tPOWUP` is 30 microseconds. Fig.6 explicitly marks an indeterminate power-transition region. 2. STMicroelectronics **STISO620 DS14110 Rev 3, January 2025**, pp.4,11–13, Table 4/10, Figs.4/5: https://www.st.com/resource/en/datasheet/stiso620.pdf . Its receiver is LOW after watchdog when its transmitter supply is absent but receiver remains powered; when receiver supply is below UVLO, output is **TRI-STATE**. `tWD` **2–8 microseconds**, `tPOWUP` 30 microseconds. Its two channels share a transmitter/receiver orientation; do not interchange its table with STISO621.
3. STMicroelectronics **IPS1025H DS13780 Rev 7, August 2025**, pp.3,9–10,15–17: https://www.st.com/resource/en/datasheet/ips1025h-32.pdf . Pin table: **FLT1 overtemperature**, **FLT2 overload**; both are current-source diagnostics, not isolated safety contacts. Section 7.4 says a disconnected VCC may continue to drive an active output until VCC falls below UVLO; Section 7.5 says a GND disconnection turns the channel OFF, but the OUT path can still carry a small `ILGND` current (Table 11 max **0.5 mA** under its stated conditions). Section 8 active demagnetization addresses stored inductive energy after turnoff. Do not infer actual coil holding from that 0.5-mA *device test limit* without the installed load circuit.

## Same-session adversarial source recheck — STISO621 is **bidirectional** (critical clarification)
The original ST **X-NUCLEO-ISO1A1 schematic pack Rev 1, March 2025**, directly inspected page 2 of 4, https://www.st.com/resource/en/schematic_pack/x-nucleo-iso1a1-schematic.pdf , shows the exact two opposite-direction paths of each STISO621: FPGA `QA*_CNTRL_L` → field `QA*_CNTRL_P` **and** field `FLT1_QA*` → FPGA `FLT1_QA*_L`. The STISO620 separately returns field `FLT2_QA*` → FPGA `FLT2_QA*_L`. This is direct **DOC-CONFIRMED** manufacturer topology (the ST reference uses the **-32** IPS variant, whereas the proposed OpenPressBrake primitive retains non-32 IPS1025H; diagnostic pin semantics remain manufacturer-defined).

**Correction to a potentially incomplete mental model:** When switched process power is absent but FPGA logic power remains, the STISO621 **command receiver** is unpowered/UNKNOWN, **and simultaneously its reverse FLT1 diagnostic transmitter is unpowered while the logic-side receiver is powered**. Thus **both** returned diagnostic paths—STISO621/FLT1 after its **2–6 µs** watchdog and STISO620/FLT2 after its **2–8 µs** watchdog—can settle to LOW. Neither LOW is an independent healthy/fault-free indication; both require explicit field-power validity. This is a two-direction analysis, not just a one-channel power-loss argument. The exact manufacturer timing is not a machine response or safety time. ST schematic labels ending `_L` identify logic-side nets; they do not establish active-low polarity.

**Evidence effect:** strengthens OPB-D06/D08's source-to-sink review of **both** FLT1 and FLT2 power-invalid masking and confirms the older Rev1 OpenPressBrake diagnostic-label swap is a **local semantic mapping problem**, not something inherited from ST's reference net names. No physical or FPGA implementation was tested. The independent safety/physical G0/G1 HOLD is unchanged.

## Adversarial power-return/rearm composition — held ordinary command is a separate risk
**Conditional timeline (INFERENCE, NOT an observed OpenPressBrake fault):** (T0) FPGA logic stays powered and an ordinary `CTRL_UP_COMMAND` or `CTRL_MUTE_COMMAND` request is HIGH; (T1) the independent system opens 11K2 and removes L7/L07, leaving the normal command in FPGA memory potentially unchanged; (T2) both returned FLT diagnostics become unavailable/LOW, which may be misinterpreted as healthy; (T3) after an independently authorized safety reset, 11K2 restores the process supply while the ordinary command is *still HIGH*; (T4) once both STISO621 sides exceed UVLO and its startup interval passes, the device resumes normal transmission of the **current** HIGH command, and IPS1025H can again respond according to its normal input/power conditions. **The component datasheets do not promise a fresh command edge or a latched OFF state across field-only power cycling.** Whether the actual FPGA clears its command, Pilz blocks muting, a physical actuator responds, or a separate fresh-start requirement is enforced is **UNKNOWN**.

This is distinct from merely asking whether output goes OFF on power loss: **OFF during absence of field power does not establish OFF upon its return.** A qualified restricted review should inspect both the independent Pilz reset/restart application and ordinary controller output-state policy after loss/restoration of 11K2, including a held pedal, stale mode and reset without new cycle command. No software-only clearing policy substitutes for independent safety functions or a physical load-hold/guard witness. STISO621 `tPOWUP` is specified as 30 µs at the component; IPS1025H power-on response has its own datasheet conditions. **Neither number is a press-brake restart time or safe-distance basis.** No live fault injection is authorized, and no numerical machine behavior is asserted.

**Human-factors design consequence (INFERENCE):** a clear not-ready state and a separate deliberate fresh start should be easier than a technician power-cycling 11K2 or bridging a return to silence a diagnostic. Restore guards and perform authorized service without forcing repeated nuisance resets. This supplements existing RST/REARM lessons with an exact *switched-process-power versus retained-FPGA-state* path; it does not claim reset/rearm is a newly discovered generic mechanism.


## Independent companion source check — ISO1212 normal Pilz-status inputs cannot distinguish OFF from open wire
**Direct OEM:** Texas Instruments **ISO1211/ISO1212 SLLSEY7 Rev G, revised February 2025**, p.18, Table 7-2, https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/iso1212.pdf . With control-side VCC1 powered and EN HIGH/open, an **open field IN/SENSE pair produces logic LOW**, the same reported logic level as an ordinary LOW input. EN LOW makes OUT high impedance; VCC1 powered down makes OUT **UNDETERMINED**. This is a device-level function table, not a proof of installed wiring. The ISO1212 does not require a separate field-side supply; its field input carries the signal energy. Its product description's “wire-break detection” refers to a **separate reference design**, not an unconditional per-channel fault flag.

**Direct OEM reference design:** TI **TIDA-01509**, https://www.ti.com/tool/TIDA-01509 , explicitly adds an optical-switch/opto-emulator element per channel, or a grouped additional-component arrangement, to distinguish broken wire. That reference architecture is **not** shown as implemented in the inspected OpenPressBrake `digital_input_24v/manifest.yaml` or Rev6 board-integration handoff; `INPUT_VALID` is described as optional and does not by itself establish a physical wire-break detector. This is a **SOURCE-CONFIRMED published contract limitation**, not proof that the installed Pilz controller or cabinet lacks its own diagnostics.

**Composition (INFERENCE):** loss of the L07-referenced status source/return, a disconnected wire, and a legitimate inactive Pilz status may all appear LOW to the ordinary FPGA input path. Simultaneously, FLT1 and FLT2 isolator return paths may also become LOW after process power disappears. A set of all-zero normal input/fault bits is therefore not necessarily an independent indication of a safely stopped, held ram; it can mean **invalid/missing process evidence**. Qualified G0/D06 review should trace the real L07 source, 11K2 contacts, any actual wire-break/rail-valid mechanism and independent Pilz/AKAS safety path. Do not add a “virtual safety OK” in LinuxCNC or assume a safety-rated input merely because the ISO1212 datasheet says “functional safety capable.” No physical test, PL/SIL or numerical stop threshold follows.

## TI broken-wire reference is **not** a plug-in L07 safety-return test
Direct inspection of TI **TIDA-01509 design guide TIDUDI9A Rev A (May 2025)**, pp.1–2,5–6, https://www.ti.com/lit/ug/tidudi9/tidudi9.pdf , reveals the **actual** mechanism behind the product-page “wire-break detection” claim: an added opto-emulator switch **temporarily disconnects the ISO121x field ground** and observes a conditional output pulse to distinguish connected/broken wiring.

**Interpretation guard:** The 10% comparison applies to the documented fAB-versus-fZ plausibility monitor, not a universal permissible speed error. The 4× tolerance applies to signal-level feasibility checking, not automatically to every fault class. The filter 99% figures are examples of filter response, not a physical stopping-distance calculation. **INFERENCE:** multiple timing stages can contribute to delayed protection, but simply adding every quoted figure would be an invalid worst-case proof without the exact configuration and timing definitions.

## Fault-path and authority matrix (paper analysis; not a tested truth table)
| Initiating fault/shortcut | Potential misleading observation | Required independent evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Broken shear pin between encoder and hazardous load | Encoder frequency looks valid for motor, not for load | Sensing location and mechanical transmission fault analysis; independent downstream motion/hold proposition as required. |
| Very slow movement or motion too short to meet activation/tolerance | No broken-shear-pin diagnostic trip yet | Actual minimum-speed, ratio, pulse counts, hazard approach and permissible response; no generic low-speed exemption. |
| Selector wire opens in 16-set mode | Different *valid* parameter set may be selected | Wiring/parameter table, monotonic safety thresholds, diagnostic-disable location, controlled change records. |
| Installer chooses slower filter or longer invalid-level tolerance to reduce nuisance trips | Cleaner status display but later reaction | Exact setting, monitor response and measured machine stopping envelope; correct EMC/installation instead of defeating protective function. |
| Drive reports stopped; guard unlock requested | Motor/encoder is stopped but a vertical ram or saw blade remains hazardous | Independent load restraint, coasting/stopping and guard-release validation; machine-specific physical witness. |
| Safety relay output returns automatically after fault clears | Operator assumes this authorizes a new cycle | Independent deliberate start/rearm and occupancy/guard verification; ordinary LinuxCNC/HAL must not own personnel safety. |

## Human-factors design: make correct setup easier than defeat
Use a short, versioned configuration sheet next to the qualified safety controller: exact selected sensor locations, ratio and speed units, enabled plausibility diagnostics, parameter-set truth table, filter/tolerance/delay values, safe output wiring and who approves changes. Keep a practical method for legitimate slow setup without turning off independent guards. If nuisance trips lead users to increase delay or disable checks, treat this as an engineering defect in wiring/EMC, sensor placement or usability—not an instruction to accept an unverified bypass. Maintenance after sensor replacement, gearbox service, belt work or coupling change reopens the mechanical-proposition review.

## Further original-source trace — diagnostic coverage depends on motion and sensor construction
This is **additional exact-edition depth**, not an instruction to operate a machine for a diagnostic exercise.

- **DOC-CONFIRMED (§13.3.1, printed p.111):** for square-wave TTL/HTL or safe sensors, Pilz requires *forced dynamisation*: within **8 hours**, the axis must move enough for signals to change on **all connected tracks**. The manual states that longer intervals increase the PFH and gives correction factors **1.01** for within 4 weeks and **1.02** for within 8 weeks. These are conditional OEM calculation factors, **not permission to move an exposed machine**, not a general proof-test interval for every component, and not a selected-machine PFH calculation. If safe periodic motion is not possible, a qualified designer must re-evaluate the selected function and evidence rather than bypass guarding or drive the ram solely to satisfy a timer.
- **DOC-CONFIRMED (§13.3.4.1, printed p.113):** for the particular *non-safe 1 Vss sin/cos encoder with mechanical fault exclusion* architecture, the sine and cosine channels must be independently generated from optics through interface, not synthesized by a common processor or derived electronically one from the other. Do not generalize this condition to every encoder type; the selected safety function must use the exact applicable architecture and sensor evidence.
- **DOC-CONFIRMED (§13.3.6–13.3.7, printed pp.116–118):** even a **safe encoder** must be compatible with the PNOZ s30's error-detection expectations. Encoder certification alone does not establish complete machine safety or a suitable evaluator/encoder pairing.
- **DOC-CONFIRMED (§13.3.9–13.3.10, printed pp.120–122):** two-proximity-switch arrangements with reduced diagnostics have different sensor-subsystem diagnostic assumptions: **60% DC** in the reduced-diagnostics example, versus **90% or 99%** in the standard arrangements. The illustrated *achievable* safety level for the reduced-diagnostics sensor/monitor configuration is **PL d (Cat.3)** versus **PL e (Cat.4)** for the specified standard dual-switch arrangement. These are manufacturer **conditional architecture examples**, not achieved values for OpenPressBrake or any selected installation. The reduced-diagnostics arrangement still requires supply-voltage monitoring and separately laid cables.
- **DOC-CONFIRMED (§13.3.2, printed p.112):** PNOZ s30 provides monitoring outputs; the **reaction function** (for example, shutting down a drive or applying a brake) is explicitly outside the monitor and must be designed by the machine/plant developer. This makes the independent final-element boundary unusually clear.

**Additional failure-path question:** A low-duty machine remains stationary for a week. A green standstill indication cannot establish that every TTL/HTL track has exercised within the OEM's 8-hour condition; review the qualified sensor/evaluator calculation and safe commissioning strategy. Never create hazardous motion just to satisfy a paper diagnostic requirement. **UNKNOWN:** actual sensors, version, operating schedule, safe proof strategy, calculated PFH, physical final elements. No test, lab or machine operation.

## Qualified review worksheet — fill from existing as-built records, not by exposing personnel
This is a **blank paper gate**, not a claim that the OpenPressBrake hardware uses this monitor. It prevents the tempting shortcut of choosing a low standstill threshold, accepting a green LED, and unlocking a guard without proving the sensor and physical load path.

| Evidence needed | Record from selected hardware/configuration | Fail/hold criterion |
|---|---|---|
| Machine hazard and dangerous load | Hazard ID, axis/shaft, downstream load, maintenance access, modes | No defined physical proposition -> HOLD |
| Monitor identity | Exact part, hardware/firmware, manual edition, approved configuration revision/CRC | Unidentified or mismatched configuration -> HOLD |
| Sensor architecture | TTL/HTL/sin-cos/safe encoder; independent sensor location, mounting, coupling/gear paths, mechanical fault exclusions | Two signals sharing one unassessed failure path -> HOLD |
| AB/Z plausibility | Enabled? selected mode, fAB/fZ ratio, vABZ, minimum speed and pulse-count activation | Disabled/unknown or below active diagnostic region -> cannot claim broken-shear-pin detection |
| Signal plausibility timing | Track AB/Z/S tolerance settings; wiring/EMC assumptions; signal error reaction | Unbounded invalid-level detection -> HOLD timing claim |
| Speed filtering/output timing | Slow/average/fast, switch-off delay, output mapping, selected mode and start type | Auxiliary output or unvalidated delay -> HOLD protective stop |
| Forced dynamisation | Sensor type, documented track exercise, safe maintenance/operation strategy, OEM PFH assumption | Cannot justify assumed exercise/reliability -> reopen safety calculation, **not** force exposed movement |
| Independent final elements | Safety relay/PLC path, drive STO/SS1 if applicable, gravity hold/brake/valve and feedback | Monitor-only/HAL-only output -> HOLD |
| Validation and change control | Qualified reviewer, independent physical stop/hold witness under approved isolated/remote conditions, revalidation after coupling/sensor changes | No selected physical witness -> G0/G1 HOLD; no personnel exposure |

**Adversarial acceptance check:** Ask the reviewer to point to the *physical* member that stops or holds the dangerous load if the encoder shaft decouples, and to explain what happens before the pulse-count plausibility detector becomes active. If the answer is only an HMI green bit, a second channel on the same failed shaft, or a filter setting, the safety proposition is not demonstrated. No generic stopping time or PL is assigned here.

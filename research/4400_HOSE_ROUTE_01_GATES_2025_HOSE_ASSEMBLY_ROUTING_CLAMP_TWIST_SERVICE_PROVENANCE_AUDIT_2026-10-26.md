# 4400 HOSE-ROUTE-01 — Hydraulic hose routing, assembly substitution and serviceability as a safety design problem

**Source inspection:** 2026-10-26 UTC. **Status:** documentary engineering research; NO selected-machine validation, NO pressure testing, NO physical release. **OpenPressBrake as-built hose, valve, clamp, guard and Y1/Y2 topology: UNKNOWN.**

## Exact question and novelty boundary

A hydraulic load-holding valve or burst-flow fuse may be correctly specified, yet the hose between the protected port and the machine's moving equipment may still be routed so that predictable abrasion, twist, kink, pressure-driven length change, hot surfaces, or a bad replacement assembly makes failure more likely. What can be established *before* any hazardous test?

This is a **prevention / maintenance-access / change-control** branch. It **extends but does not rediscover** the prior 2026-10-24 [pinhole injection and isolated-pressure audit](4400_HYDRAULIC_PINHOLE_INJECTION_HOSE_FAILURE_ISOLATION_SOURCE_AUDIT_2026-10-24.md), the [flow-triggered hose-burst fuse audit](4400_HOSE_BURST_VELOCITY_FUSE_FLOW_WINDOW_AND_UNSAFE_TEST_TRANSFER_AUDIT_2026-10-24.md), and the [2026-10-25 cylinder-side load-holding valve work](4400_REXROTH_AVBC90_EMERGENCY_SCREW_RELIEF_AND_PARKER_E2_LOAD_HOLD_SOURCE_AUDIT_2026-10-25.md). Those address **after-failure** fluid energy / retention; this investigation adds manufacturer-evidenced **failure prevention through geometry, compatible assemblies and usable service access**.

   - Printed p. 3: Gates warns that hoses, couplings, crimp tooling and crimp tolerances are not generically interchangeable; mismatched assemblies can fail.
   - Printed p. 6: STAMPED selection includes size, temperature, application, medium, pressure **including surges**, ends and delivery/flow.
   - Printed pp. 14–16: avoid abrasion and moving contacts; clamp correct size (oversized clamps permit movement/wear); avoid insufficient bend radius, heat, sharp fitting-end bends, twisting and flexing across incompatible planes; provide slack for pressure-related length change and access for inspection; use qualified protective sleeves, guards and adapters where appropriate.
   - Printed p. 18: verify actual routing/length, avoid twist and use proper tightening method; the manufacturer's low-pressure post-installation check is **not permission** for an unsupervised hazardous machine run.
   - Printed p. 23: Gates recommends inspection based on equipment and manufacturer; absent specific guidance, its **generic stationary-equipment example is every three months**. **Not** a machine-specific legal requirement or validated OpenPressBrake interval. The generic inspection list begins with power lockout and a safe position, but is **not a complete pressure-zone, trapped-energy, gravity-load and personnel-isolation procedure**.
   - Printed pp. 24–26: abrasion, hose-body burst, coupling-end burst, leak, coupling blowoff and twist have distinct root-cause categories; replacement without correcting routing or compatibility can repeat the failure.

## Practical architecture and adversarial synthesis

**Normal control:** LinuxCNC/FPGA can request pump/valve motion and display diagnostic warnings, but no software state makes a damaged hose safe. **Monitoring:** leak, pressure and travel sensors may support maintenance, but their validity, zone coverage and failure modes require independent evaluation. **Safety-related control:** selected independent safety logic and monitored final elements own protective stop/inhibit only where qualified. **Physical mechanism:** rated load restraint, protected hydraulic segments, physical isolation/depressurization and accessible guarding each need their own proof.

**Adversarial counterexample:** A new abrasion-resistant hose is substituted with a compatible-looking end fitting and routed tightly behind a sealed cover. A bench pressure test passes, but the hose twists at full Y2 travel and the cover is difficult to remove, so maintenance stops inspecting it. Neither the stronger cover nor a passed stationary pressure test proves safe routing over the actual lifecycle. The low-cost fix is often a better bracket/clamp path and easy inspection, **not** more software or a higher hose burst rating.

**Gates/OSHA scope reconciliation:** Gates' generic p. 23 eight-step maintenance list is a *hose inspection overview*. OSHA 1910.147 adds covered servicing requirements for isolation, hazardous stored energy, verification and restoration. It would be an error to copy Gates' abbreviated list as a complete press-brake LOTO procedure. A load-supporting hydraulic circuit may have incompatible goals (retain pressure to hold vs release pressure to service); qualified independent mechanical restraint and documented zone-by-zone procedures must resolve that conflict **before** opening a line.

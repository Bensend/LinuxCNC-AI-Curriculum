# 4400-GUARD-FAST-01 — Make the guard easier to restore than to defeat

**2026-10-28 | Technician / machine-designer one-pass review | NON-GRADUATED, no machine test**

Read [original EU/OSHA/manufacturer source-depth audit](../research/4400_GUARD_FAST_01_EU_ANNEXIII_CAPTIVE_FIXINGS_FALSE_CLOSED_AND_MAINTENANCE_BYPASS_SOURCE_AUDIT_2026-10-28.md) with existing [GUARD-01](4400_GUARD_01_PRESS_BRAKE_JOB_SHOP_SAFEGUARD_FEASIBILITY_AND_ANTI_BYPASS_MODULE_2026-10-25.md). Do not treat this card as permission to modify or run a machine.

**Physical review at the drawing/as-built evidence level:** Can a fixed panel sit in its opening with the bolts missing? Do all intended fasteners remain attached to the guard or frame during removal? Is the selected fixing/tool appropriate and its load/impact path established? How many times per shift must the guard be opened? Can normal adjustment, cleaning and die change occur without removal or safety defeat? Is a frequently accessed guard better implemented as a supported, independently interlocked hinged/sliding door with an appropriate guard-locking decision? Does closure merely permit a separate deliberate start rather than restarting the machine?

**Source-confirmed boundaries:** EU Regulation 2023/1230 Annex III 1.4.2.1 addresses tool-removable fixed guards, retained fixings and guards not appearing installed without fasteners where possible; §1.4.2.2 addresses attached movable interlocked guards and stopping/locking conditions. OSHA's guarding eTool distinguishes fixed-guard simplicity from maintenance inconvenience and interlocked-guard ease of access, and warns against automatic restart on closure. Clarendon/MW captive-screw designs retain loose hardware but **do not certify a barrier**. The EU text is official **indexed** evidence with direct fetch challenge; OSHA/manufacturer pages directly inspected.

**Do not claim:** a captive screw is tight, a panel switch proves physical retention, an interlock proves stopping distance, a closed guard means the hydraulic ram is supported, or a product catalog confers safety PL/SIL. Avoid tool-less quarter-turn fasteners as a silent substitute for tool-required **fixed** guarding where that requirement applies. If frequent access is expected, engineer the access task and safety function rather than expecting operators to reassemble a difficult panel every time.

**Use existing Stage A E/F/G only**, no new questionnaire: qualified guard geometry, fastening/hinge retention, physical access exclusion, independent stop/hold, maintenance isolation and deliberate restart. **OpenPressBrake applicability UNKNOWN; G0/G1 HOLD; G2 NOT RUN; G3/G4 NOT AUTHORIZED; G5 HOLD.** No person exposed until qualified physical protection is established. No compute, test or evaluator score.

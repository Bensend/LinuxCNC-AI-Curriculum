# 4400-PFL-01 — Robot force-limit accuracy is not proof of safe human contact
**2026-10-30 | original manufacturer online-manual and official publisher-scope audit | NON-GRADUATED / NO PHYSICAL TEST**

## Distinct question and predecessors
The 2026-10-29 ENABLE-02 / ROBOT-STATUS-01 / ROBOT-PLANE-01 work covered 3PE mode semantics, misleading status polarity and late safety-plane activation. This investigation instead asks: **can a force-limited robot be treated as safe for people merely because its internal force limit, speed limit, PL d/Cat 3 rating, or product name is advertised?** No prior dedicated PFL/contact-compliance branch was found by exact-term scan of the full safety-course index (collaborative, PFL, power and force, quasi-static, speed and separation). This is a separate application-level contact/entrapment mechanism, not a repeat of robot safety-plane geometry.

## Source ledger (directly inspected 2026-10-30 UTC)
1. **[UR10e PolyScope SW5_23 — Configurable Safety Functions](https://www.universal-robots.com/manuals/EN/HTML/SW5_23/Content/prod-usr-man/complianceUR10e/H_g5_sections/safetyFunctionsAndinterfaces/configurable_functions.htm)**, live manufacturer HTML, lines 80–117 in inspection. **DOC-CONFIRMED:** force limit applies at configured tool/end-effector positions and elbow, and safety function table gives **25 N force-limit *accuracy*** for this model/manual context (PL d, Cat 3); this is **not** an acceptable contact-force limit. OEM explicitly warns of **two force-limiting exceptions**: extended-arm radial knee-joint leverage and tool near the base with short lever arm/tangential motion. OEM also notes 250 mm/s manual hand-guiding recommendation unless risk assessment justifies more; that is **not** a blanket collaborative-contact acceptance speed. Its stopping time/distance limits dynamically constrain robot speed; the configured limit is not a measured cell stopping result.
2. **[UR PolyScope SW5_26 — Robot Limits](https://www.universal-robots.com/manuals/EN/HTML/SW5_26/Content/prod-usr-man/software/PolyScope/content/safety_g5/Robot_limits_g5_en.htm)**. **DOC-CONFIRMED:** factory presets are suggestions, not substitutes for application risk assessment; user-defined tool positions and flange/elbow are separately monitored; power/momentum/force/speed/stopping limits are different safety propositions. **Do not silently transfer SW5_26 screen descriptions to a selected SW5_23 installation without version check.**
3. **[UR g-Series PolyScope X SW10_15 — Operational Mode](https://www.universal-robots.com/manuals/EN/HTML/SW10_15/Content/prod-usr-man/complianceG7/PolyX_SW/psx_ops_mode.htm)**. **DOC-CONFIRMED:** in Automatic Mode people may enter/remain in safeguarded space only with active safeguarding **or a validated PFL collaborative application**. Manual mode, Freedrive and 3PE are distinct. Mode changes must restore suspended safeguards; manual high-speed mode requires application risk assessment. This is **not** evidence that any selected application has passed validation.
4. **[ISO 10218-2:2025 official catalogue](https://www.iso.org/standard/73934.html)**, publisher metadata/scope only: Edition 2 published February 2025, covers robot **application and cell integration**. **[ISO/TS 15066:2016 official catalogue](https://www.iso.org/standard/62996.html)**: published, revision planned, scope collaborative industrial robot systems/work environment. **[ISO official explanatory article, 2016-03-08](https://www.iso.org/news/2016/03/Ref2057.html)**: collaboration is a property of the **application**, and different techniques include monitored stop, hand-guiding, speed/separation monitoring, PFL. **DOC-CONFIRMED publisher summaries, NOT full normative clauses**; do not copy contact-force/pressure numbers from secondary sites or claim standards-compliant design.

## Fault and contact-path analysis (INFERENCE unless otherwise labeled)
- **Free transient contact ≠ quasi-static trapped contact.** A person struck with a free retreat path differs from a hand trapped between robot/tool/workpiece and a hard fixture. Contact geometry, sharp edge, body region, compliance and reachable fixed surfaces can dominate even with an apparently low TCP speed.
- **Internal limit ≠ measured contact pressure.** A force-limit algorithm controls/monitors selected robot quantities. It does not measure contact patch area, every protruding workpiece edge, all external fixtures, or downstream tool actuation. Force and pressure require separate application-level assessment; no numeric acceptance values are assigned here.
- **Accuracy ≠ limit ≠ measured force ≠ PL.** The UR10e **25 N** is the stated *accuracy* of a particular safety function in SW5_23, not a universal maximum permissible force. PL d/Cat 3 describes a component safety function's architecture/performance, not the complete robot cell's risk reduction.
- **Near-base/extended-arm pose trap.** The manufacturer explicitly identifies exceptional lever geometry that can produce high forces even at low motion speed. A speed-only safety argument is insufficient at those poses.
- **PFL does not stop non-contact hazards.** A powered gripper, sharp sheet, welding torch, rotating spindle, vacuum payload, hot workpiece, laser, or falling load may require other independently validated safeguards. Ordinary LinuxCNC/FPGA cannot become personnel-safety authority by relaying robot telemetry.
- **Mode/restart trap.** A 3PE center grip during manual teaching does not certify automatic PFL. A safeguard clear or successful PFL parameter edit must not silently authorize hazardous process restart.

## Minimum practical architecture (proposed, not certified)
1. First remove/reduce sharp edges and trapping geometries; relocate fixtures, use rounded compliant tooling where appropriate, keep pinch points out of the collaborative envelope. This often reduces bypass incentive and verification cost more effectively than tuning a robot limit alone.
2. For remaining exposed hazards choose an appropriate **application-specific** validated strategy: physically separate hazardous operations, safety-rated monitored standstill during access, safety-rated separation sensing, or PFL **only** where all relevant contact conditions are validated. External tool energy needs its own safety functions.
3. Record each contact scenario: person/body part, robot link/tool/payload/fixture, transient vs trapped, modes and approach directions, safety-function identity and selected configuration, physical measurement/acceptance method, guard/access and deliberate restart. An operator must be able to use the safe path without routinely defeating safeguards.
4. Where the application cannot demonstrate basic safe-to-operate conditions, **no personnel-exposed operation**. Experimental movement requires a separately qualified, isolated/remote plan with people outside the danger zone and residual risks stated.

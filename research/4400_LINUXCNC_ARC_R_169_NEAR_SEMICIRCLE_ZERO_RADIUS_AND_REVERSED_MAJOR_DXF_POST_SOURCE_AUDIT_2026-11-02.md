## A. R-format execution chain and a different tolerance from IJK
`convert_arc` rejects mixed R+IJK, selects active plane and requires nonzero feed; `convert_arc2` sends an R word to `arc_data_r` with the **fixed** `RADIUS_TOLERANCE_MM = 0.00005*25.4 = 0.00127 mm` (or `RADIUS_TOLERANCE_INCH=0.00005 in`). **This is NOT the configurable `CENTER_ARC_RADIUS_TOLERANCE_MM` used by `arc_data_ijk`.** R-format uses the chord midpoint `M`, `h=|end-start|/2`, `a=abs(R)` and tests `h-a > tolerance`; if `h/a > 1-TINY` it **clamps h to a** before `asin(h/a)`. It computes the center offset `a*cos(asin(h/a))` along the chosen perpendicular. The sign of R together with G2/G3 chooses which perpendicular; `turn=-P` for G2 or `+P` for G3. `interp_check.cc` requires P to round to at least 1 and approximately an integer. Only after successful return does `convert_arc2` call `ARC_FEED`.

**Near-semicircle correction, source-derived:** start (0,0), end (10,0), `G2 X10 Y0 R4.999` in G21/G17 has `h=5`, `h-a=0.001 mm <=0.00127 mm`. The source does **not** raise “radius too small”; it clamps h to 4.999, calculates offset zero and produces center (5,0), geometrically a **5-mm semicircle** rather than the requested 4.999-mm radius. At `R4.998`, `h-a=0.002>0.00127` so the source returns the radius-too-small error. These are static/numeric predictions, **not executed**. A CAM post should reject geometrically impossible R rather than depend on the interpreter's tolerance-based repair.

**Zero-R/tiny-chord edge, source-derived and unverified:** start (0,0), end (0.001,0), `R0` in G21: h=0.0005 mm, so `h-a=0.0005 <=0.00127` does not trigger the first error. The next `h/a` divides by zero, can lead to h=0 then `asin(0/0)`, and the computed center may become non-finite. The source has **no explicit R==0 / finite-value guard in this function**. C++ floating-point environment, upstream error handling, Task/TP acceptance, and actual runtime response are **UNKNOWN**; do not claim a crash, actual motion, or successful program acceptance. This case merits a disconnected interpreter and sanitizer/finite-value test **only after runner isolation**.

**Major/minor discriminator:** for G2 from (0,0) to (10,0), `R+6` produces center approximately (5,-3.316625), the minor clockwise arc; `R-6` center (5,+3.316625), the major clockwise arc. `P2` adds a turn, but is not a substitute for the R sign. These are numeric inferences from `theta` branch and official semantics.

## B. Real open-source DXF→post failure: reversed major ARC
PathForge `GcodeBuilder.stream` gathers DXF ARC endpoints (already **rounded to 2 decimal places** by `geometry/endpoints.py`), calls `arrange_entities` / `reorder_chain` which can **reverse** a segment when its end connects to the prior segment, then passes `arranged_start/end` to `write_arc_entity`. The writer calculates `R=-radius` for a natural DXF CCW span >180°, but uses `determine_arc_direction` (the **cross-product sign of the two radius vectors**) and a natural-start comparison to select G2 versus G3. The cross-product sign only disambiguates the shorter angular direction; it cannot encode whether the intended sweep is >180°.

**Concrete two-entity discriminator:** a DXF LINE from (-1,-1) to (0,-1) followed by an ARC centered (0,0), radius 1, start angle 0°, end angle 270°. The optimizer's leftmost-start seed selects the LINE, then connects to the ARC's natural **end** (0,-1), so it can reverse the ARC to (0,-1)→(1,0). Correct reverse traversal is **G2 X1 Y0 R-1** (clockwise major arc, center 0,0). The writer computes cross `(0,-1) × (1,0) = +1`, calls it “counterclockwise,” and chooses **G3 X1 Y0 R-1**. LinuxCNC's R center selection for that G3 major arc yields center **(1,-1)**, not (0,0). All of these are source-derived and arithmetic, **not a reproduced G-code trace or field cut**. This error is conditional on the reversed-major-arc arrangement, not a claim every PathForge ARC is wrong.

The existing PathForge tests cover a forward major arc's negative R but not reversed major direction. The new [review-only pytest regression](../examples/4000_arc_r_169/test_pathforge_arc_direction_review_only.py) freezes forward/reverse minor/major expectations. It should fail on the reversed-major case against the pinned implementation; **NOT RUN**. The separate [G2/G3 paired NC files](../examples/4000_arc_r_169/) isolate LinuxCNC center selection without PathForge's unrelated previously documented `G04 X` problem.

**Additional quantization boundary (unexecuted inference):** `geometry/endpoints.py` rounds ARC endpoints to **two decimals** even when `ConversionParams.output_precision=3`. An ordinary +Z ARC of radius 0.004 mm at angles 0°→90° has distinct geometric endpoints (0.004,0) and (0,0.004), but both become (0.00,0.00) at this intermediate stage. This is a **conditional** small-feature geometry-loss example, not a claim that all posts or every real DXF exhibit it. A robust post must compare original versus quantized geometry and fail closed rather than silently emitting a degenerate arc.

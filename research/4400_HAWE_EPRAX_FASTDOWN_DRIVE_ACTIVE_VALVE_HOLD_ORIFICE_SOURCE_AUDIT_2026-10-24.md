## Engineering question and original source
How can a press-brake ram accelerate when a servo drive is not actively controlling a hydraulic throttle? Which signals represent *normal control*, which components are *safety-related final elements*, and which physical propositions remain unproved even if valve-position switches indicate a plausible state?

Directly inspected HAWE Hydraulik SE, **B 6340, ePRAX modular operating instructions, 04-2026, revision 1.5 EN**, 94-page original PDF, generated 2026-06-11: https://productfinder.hawe.com/downloads/B6340-en.pdf . Specific printed pages below correspond to PDF pages minus one. The manual describes a **partly completed machine** and explicitly delegates whole-machine safety to the integrator. It is a useful *real industrial architecture example*, **not evidence that ePRAX is installed on OpenPressBrake**.

## Critical failure path: normal servo speed authority disappears
1. **Initiating condition (OEM-described):** during gravity-assisted FAST DOWN, speed control of an electric drive is inactive or ineffective.
2. **Mechanism (OEM):** the hydraulic outlet-throttle arrangement can no longer regulate descent as intended. **Drive active** is a useful monitored status, not independent proof of a safe hydraulic state.
3. **Required independent boundary (OEM + INFERENCE):** selected safety logic must be able to command the qualified hydraulic stop elements even when ordinary drive speed control is unavailable. Independent physical proof must establish actual Y1 and Y2 motion arrest, holding and residual energy.
4. **Potential common cause (INFERENCE):** if drive-active diagnostics, valve commands and their 24 V returns depend on one failed power domain or one ordinary controller, the assumed independent stop may disappear at the same moment as normal speed control. Trace supplies, final elements and diagnostics separately.
5. **UNKNOWN:** any installed OpenPressBrake pump, valve, controller, timing, pressure, position-monitoring, common-cause topology or response.

## Orifice-versus-monitoring tradeoff: do not transplant numbers
The manual's specific factory orifice diameters and illustrative descent limits are **product-specific engineering assumptions** requiring cylinder/load verification. Its conditional linear-scale-monitoring alternative depends on the *actual safety controller, sensor, diagnostic, stopping and holding design*. Neither approach establishes universal acceptance criteria or a safe stopping distance. Treat omitted/replaced orifices and scale/software changes as change-control events requiring physical revalidation.

## Human-factors and maintainability
Make independent stop faults legible: display **Drive status**, **Safety valve feedback**, **Y1/Y2 movement witness**, **Hydraulic holding not proved**, and **New start required** as separate concepts. A mechanic must not be forced to bypass a guard to see the valve label or pressure test point; arrange accessible, guarded, labeled qualified verification locations. A status LED and a convenient 'clear all faults' control are not substitutes for safe access or deliberate rearm.

## Evidence still required before any personnel-exposed operation
Exact installed hydraulic schematic and OEM part revisions; separate Y1/Y2 final elements and feedback source; electrical/safety control allocation and power domains; gravity/accumulator/pressure compartments; guard coverage; physical stop/hold under credible faults; deliberate new start and maintenance isolation. No values, truth tables, safety PL/SIL, timings or pressure thresholds may be copied from the ePRAX example to the selected machine. **G0/G1 HOLD; G2 NOT RUN; G3/G4 NOT AUTHORIZED.**

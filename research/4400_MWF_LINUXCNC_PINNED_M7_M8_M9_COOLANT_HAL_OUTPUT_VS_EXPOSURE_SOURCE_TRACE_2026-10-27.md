## Critical architecture consequence
`M9` is an **ordinary control request to HAL output pins**. It is not a physical stop sensor and not a biological or breathing-zone monitor. A program can send M9 while a stuck relay, blocked pipe, pressure accumulator or persistent mist defeats the intended physical effect. M5 spindle control is a **separate command family**; do not infer a synchronized physical stop/air-clearance relationship from M9 alone. A timed post-cycle enclosure wait can support **occupational-hygiene exposure control** if actually validated, but must not be promoted into a personnel-safety guard/stop function without independent design and physical proof.

**Counterexample:** a HAL net connects `iocontrol.0.coolant-flood` to a normal output that is shorted ON. Task records flood OFF and the HAL output pin becomes false, yet the pump remains energized. A second counterexample is that coolant flow ceases while the airborne aerosol remains. These are **INFERENCE fault paths**, not tests of the selected machine. For a relevant safety function, independent physical stop/hold/guard and energy-removal witnesses remain necessary.

## Limits and next evidence
No HAL configuration from OpenPressBrake was inspected, no pump/LEV feedback or power wiring was examined, no live or simulated M-code test was run, and no `TEST-CONFIRMED` machine behavior exists. Do not infer `M7/M8/M9` is used for extraction on a real installation; an integrator could assign these normal outputs to other functions or remap them. Before any validation, collect exact as-built HAL mapping, external final elements, motion-safety architecture and occupational-hygiene assessment. A source-only trace is sufficient to reject **software-status-as-physical-proof**; no lab is justified to establish that elementary boundary. **G0/G1 HOLD, G2 NOT RUN, G3/G4 NOT AUTHORIZED.**

## Additional pinned abort/E-stop source trace — a non-obvious LEV reuse hazard
At the **same pinned revision**, [`src/emc/task/taskclass.cc` L386–401](https://github.com/LinuxCNC/linuxcnc/blob/c9a5a0a5034177c8db64cb44abf14d4f2eb166f7/src/emc/task/taskclass.cc#L386-L401) shows `Task::emcIoAbort()` sets both coolant status fields and both coolant HAL pins **false**; [L404–410](https://github.com/LinuxCNC/linuxcnc/blob/c9a5a0a5034177c8db64cb44abf14d4f2eb166f7/src/emc/task/taskclass.cc#L404-L410) shows `emcAuxEstopOn()` calls `hal_init_pins()` which also initializes coolant outputs false. [`src/emc/task/emctask.cc` L324–372](https://github.com/LinuxCNC/linuxcnc/blob/c9a5a0a5034177c8db64cb44abf14d4f2eb166f7/src/emc/task/emctask.cc#L324-L372) invokes I/O abort and coolant-flood OFF in OFF, ESTOP_RESET and ESTOP transitions; ON also commands flood OFF. **Design implication (INFERENCE, not a physical test):** because M7/M8 pins are generic HAL outputs, an integrator might connect `iocontrol.0.coolant-flood` to a mist extractor or to sump circulation. Then a normal Task abort/E-stop can request the extractor/circulation **OFF** at a time when HSE MW1 says residual airborne mist may persist, or MW5 says avoiding stagnation matters. **Do not** infer that a single universal response is to leave the extractor running: electrical isolation, fire/dust, fumes, stored energy and maintenance hazards may require different action. Instead define a separate qualified extraction/circulation energy and stop policy for the actual machine; never let ordinary CNC coolant semantics silently decide personnel exposure control or safety-critical fan/valve behavior. A software output being false does not prove a physical fan stopped or residual mist gone. This is a **cross-domain source-confirmed command behavior plus inferred design hazard**, not evidence that any OpenPressBrake uses these pins.

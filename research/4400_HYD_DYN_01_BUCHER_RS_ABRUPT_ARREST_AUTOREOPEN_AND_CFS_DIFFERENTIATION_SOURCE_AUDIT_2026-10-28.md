## Exact question, novelty check and predecessor reconciliation
When a gravity-loaded hydraulic axis loses a hose, is a valve that closes rapidly always the safest possible response? **No generic answer follows.** The valve must prevent the dangerous loss of support **and** its own dynamic response must be compatible with the actuator, structure, tooling, workpiece and exposed people. An arrest can be preferable to an uncontrolled fall while still creating a different transient hazard.

The existing [HYD hose-burst source audit](4400_HOSE_BURST_VELOCITY_FUSE_FLOW_WINDOW_AND_UNSAFE_TEST_TRANSFER_AUDIT_2026-10-24.md) already establishes flow-window discrimination, minimum-load failure, partial leakage, location, orientation and the difference between a flow fuse and continuous load holding. The [HAWE LB source-depth](4400_HAWE_LB_LINE_RUPTURE_FLOW_THRESHOLD_ORIFICE_SURGE_SOURCE_AUDIT_2026-10-24.md) already establishes decompression-surge nuisance trips and orifice variants. [STRUCT-01](4400_STRUCT_01_CINCINNATI_PRESS_BRAKE_ECCENTRIC_OFFCENTER_STRIPPING_LOAD_SOURCE_AUDIT_2026-10-28.md) already establishes machine-specific structural capacity and [FORM-HAZ-04](4400_FORM_HAZ_04_NIOSH_FACE_05OK005_PRESS_BRAKE_WORKPIECE_EJECTION_INCIDENT_ADDENDUM_2026-10-28.md) establishes a distinct real workpiece-ejection mechanism. **This is not a newly discovered hose-fuse or generic overload lesson.** Increment: the *exact* Bucher RS 2024 OEM warning that its rapid closure generates **very large deceleration**, its **automatic hydraulic reopening** rule, a 40%-above-normal-flow OEM selection rule distinct from prior manufacturers, and an explicit cross-hazard dynamic-arrest witness for a press-brake review. ## Physical sequence and why a “successful trip” can still be insufficient
**RS 2024 original OEM sequence:** Under ordinary flow the valve is open; high Z→P flow raises the pressure differential until the seat closes. When P pressure becomes greater than Z pressure the OEM says the valve **opens again automatically**. Its documented closing-flow selection must be **at least 40% above maximum normal operating flow** for this RS series. These are product-specific conditions, not a generic hydraulic truth table. Exact closing response and resulting actuator deceleration depend on circuit, fluid, flow, load and compliance. A *selected* valve could be different, absent, reversed or set differently.

| Failure or transition | Primary intended benefit | Distinct remaining hazard / witness | Classification |
|---|---|---|---|
| Full hose rupture yields sufficient flow | RS seat arrests outflow in its protected direction | OEM explicitly warns of **very large deceleration**. Need independent actuator/structure/tool/part dynamic response and containment witness, not just “valve closed.” | DOC-CONFIRMED deceleration warning; INFERENCE machine consequence |
| Partial rupture or slow leak below trip flow | None established for this fault class | Drift and injection may persist; existing HYD audit already covers threshold blind spot. | INFERENCE |
| Normal rapid switching or decompression | No rupture | Prior HAWE source establishes nuisance surge; a trip itself may impose abrupt mechanical load and encourage field defeat. | DOC-CONFIRMED predecessor; INFERENCE transfer |

### Dynamic-arrest proposition (qualitative, not a simulated acceptance calculation)
A stop is not a single scalar. A selected press-brake validation would need to establish **(a)** no uncontrolled gravity descent after credible failure, **(b)** stopping motion and peak transient loading do not create a second hazard (tool/part release, structure failure, ejection, hose/connection overpressure), **(c)** the holding state remains stable for the required exposure, and **(d)** pressure restoration cannot initiate hazardous movement without separate deliberate rearm and a fresh cycle request. The force/time and pressure/time histories, structural response, load restraint, and allowable limits are **UNKNOWN**. A simple theoretical impulse relationship can suggest what to measure but cannot validate a press brake without actual effective mass, stiffness, hydraulic compressibility and constraints; therefore **no numerical deceleration, pressure spike or safe distance is assigned**.

**Important negative evidence:** the RS OEM's warning concerns **transporting people**. It does **not** say an RS is categorically prohibited on every press brake, nor does it prove a particular press-brake part will eject. The 2022 CFS article concerns excavators; its controlled lowering and speed claims are not a press-brake safety approval. The existing NIOSH workpiece ejection was caused by loss of die engagement, **not** by an RS valve trip. Do not merge those incidents into a fictional event.

5. **Before any personnel-exposed operation:** prove selected machine's independent stop/hold, energy isolation, guarding and fresh restart. If the minimum threshold cannot be met, no personnel-exposed hazardous operation; experimental work only under separately approved remote isolation with all people outside the hazard zone.

## Second original-OEM source-depth — BBV load-hold can open through pilot or optional pressure relief (2026-10-28)

This is **additional exact-BBV source depth within HYD-DYN-01**, not a newly discovered thermal-relief or pilot mechanism. The earlier [Sun/Parker counterbalance and thermal-relief audit](4400_COUNTERBALANCE_PILOT_THERMAL_RELIEF_AND_MOTOR_BRAKE_BOUNDARY_2026-10-23.md) already establishes load/pilot/thermal-relief opening and the motor-brake sequence; this adds the Bucher BBV **specific internal X-pilot/SV flow path and housing-versus-cartridge availability**, not a new generic competency. It attacks the opposite error from “the burst valve arrests too abruptly”: **“a leak-free load-control valve must remain shut regardless of pilot pressure and relief operation.”** Exact product family and physical path matter.

- **Bucher Hydraulics**, *Leak-Free Load-Control Valve, Size 6*, **300-P-9050020-EN-04, issue 08.2020**, nine-page [original BBV 6 flanged/piped PDF](https://www.bucherhydraulics.com/datacat/files/Katalog/Ventile/LeckfreieBremsventile/010%20-%20BBV/BBV-6-S_300-P-9050020-en.pdf), directly inspected pp. 1, 3 and 7–9. Its §4.1 describes zero B→A leakage **only in the specified neutral condition: B loaded, A and pilot X depressurized**. §4.3 describes X pilot pressure first unseating a pilot ball, then moving the control spool to meter B→A lowering flow. §4.4 states the **optional secondary relief** at cylinder port B raises pilot-chamber X pressure, opens the control spool and relieves B→A. **DOC-CONFIRMED exact OEM internal path**, not proof that relief actuation necessarily causes ram motion in any particular machine.
- **Bucher Hydraulics**, *Leak-Free Load-Control Cartridge Valve, Size 6*, **300-P-9050008-EN-04, issue 08.2020**, nine-page [original BBV 6 C cartridge PDF](https://www.bucherhydraulics.com/datacat/files/Katalog/Ventile/LeckfreieBremsventile/020%20-%20BBVC/BBV-6-C_300-P-9050008-en.pdf), directly inspected pp. 1, 3 and 6–9. The cartridge has the pilot-operated B→A opening mechanism but **does not contain the flanged/piped family's optional secondary relief assembly**. The [current manufacturer BBV family page](https://www.bucherhydraulics.com/en/products/valves-and-control-block-solutions/safety-relief-valves/load-control-valves/bbv) explicitly says the secondary relief option is for the housing design, not the cartridge. **DOC-CONFIRMED family/option difference.**
- The 2020 flanged BBV manual says adequate **actual-use safety tests** are required and qualified design knowledge is necessary; the cartridge manual repeats that requirement. The manufacturer's website lists **presses** among BBV applications, but that does not identify a specific press brake or qualify OpenPressBrake.

**Causal distinction:** A pressure-relief path can deliberately defeat a static load-holding closure to relieve excessive cylinder pressure. Whether that produces any hazardous descent, drift, tool movement or secondary pressure event depends on the actual hydraulic circuit and load; that consequence is **INFERENCE/UNKNOWN**, not a witnessed machine failure. Conversely, omitting the optional integrated relief in a cartridge does not prove the whole machine lacks another appropriate relief. A replacement between BBV housing and cartridge variants cannot inherit the same pressure/holding/safe-state evidence. The BBV *pilot X source* and its failure/pressure-restoration behavior belong in the selected safety dependency map, even if ordinary LinuxCNC is not commanding lowering.

# 4400 — Independent OEM press-brake source: side-door asymmetry, BendGuard mute, remote service and disassembly hazards

## Source, edition and question
Direct primary source: TRUMPF, *Operator's manual TruBend Series 2000 (B35)*, B1161en dated 2023-05-01, official 114-page PDF: https://www.trumpf.com/filestorage/TRUMPF_US/user_upload/PIM_TruBend_Serie_2000_B35_us_V3.pdf . Examined chapter 1 printed pp. 1-19 through 1-22, 1-29, 1-33 through 1-36 (PDF pp. 18–21, 29, 32–35) and installation/remote-support references. **DOC-CONFIRMED** statements below apply to the manufacturer's described model/edition, not automatically to LinuxCNC/OpenPressBrake.

Question: Does a commercial OEM provide a *single* safe-state bit or a simple rule “open guard / E-stop / pump off = all hazards removed”? **No.** Its manual distinguishes several protective functions and modes; it also explicitly identifies unbraked moving assemblies after power removal and hazards when dismantling hydraulic components.

## Two commercial OEMs: real architectural differences, no transfer
The [Cincinnati PROFORM+ audit](4400_CINCINNATI_PROFORM_RAM_RESTRAINT_LOTO_RESCUE_MANUAL_ERRATA_AUDIT_2026-10-23.md) traces an OEM-specific ram-support and residual-pressure sequence and separate power/no-power entrapment response. The TRUMPF manual explicitly warns of hydraulic disassembly leading to beam fall and differentiates side/rear door reactions, muted BendGuard, STO backgauge and remote UI access. These are **different documented architectures**. Neither is proof of a selected LinuxCNC/FPGA safety implementation, and neither manual's model-specific numeric or procedural instructions should be transplanted.

## Proposed four-layer machine architecture (INFERENCE, not an installed system)
1. **Ordinary control:** LinuxCNC/FPGA/UI manages commanded motion, programs, modes, diagnostic logs and remote support session display. May request a stop; must not alone authorize hazardous access, personnel safety rearm or safe-speed claims.
2. **Monitoring/diagnostics:** mode, guard input states, timestamps, active/latched faults and communication freshness shown clearly. A diagnostic “door closed” or “STO active” is not physical stop/hold.
3. **Independent safety-related control:** selected and validated safety functions for side/rear access, front presence sensing/muting, E-stop, restart prevention, backgauge STO and other hazards. Response to **one versus both** guards and rear access must be derived from the selected circuit and SRS, not assumed from TRUMPF prose.
4. **Physical energy/load mechanisms:** rated electrical isolators, hydraulic isolation/bleed by verified zones, mechanical ram support/holding, safe escape and suitable restraint of gravity loads. Loss of power or STO cannot substitute for these physical propositions.

## Remote support failure path and minimum controls
**DOC-CONFIRMED:** OEM recognizes remote UI access and injury risk. **INFERENCE for LinuxCNC web UI:** treat remote diagnostic access as read-only by default; separate remote configuration/maintenance from hazardous motion authority; require a physically present authorized person and locally controlled, separately validated safety rearm and start; prevent stale remote state from claiming safety; log actor, action, machine mode, time and change version. Remote network connectivity loss must not remove independent protective functions. Do **not** infer that TRUMPF itself uses this exact design or that its remote service can start a ram; the source does not prove that.

## Paper-only adversarial review
- “One side door open stops axes; therefore both side doors and rear guard are equivalent.” **REJECT:** manufacturer explicitly distinguishes reactions. Physical safety outcome still needs qualified verification.
- “BendGuard is muted but speed is reduced, so people can safely reach the tooling.” **REJECT:** no selected safe-speed monitoring, residual risk or access geometry.
- “Backgauge drives still have power, so STO did not occur.” **REJECT:** powered drive electronics can coexist with manufacturer-described STO. **Also reject** “STO proves press beam held.”
- “A remote support engineer can see the HMI, so remote motion is safe.” **REJECT:** diagnostic access and motion authority differ; the manual does not state remote motion capability.
- “Main switch padlocked means hydraulic disassembly cannot drop the beam.” **REJECT:** the OEM explicitly warns about press-beam fall and unbraked assemblies after power removal.
- “Our front laser works, so helper and backgauge are protected.” **REJECT:** rear/sheet sweep/step change remain distinct hazards.

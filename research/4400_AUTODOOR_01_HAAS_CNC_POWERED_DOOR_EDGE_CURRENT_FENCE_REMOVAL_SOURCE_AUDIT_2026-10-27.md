# 4400-AUTODOOR-01 — Powered CNC door: closing-edge hazard, guard authority, and automation-removal safety regression
**Date:** 2026-10-27. **Status:** source/documentary research only; NON-GRADUATED. No physical machine, safety-function rating, or evaluator result.

## Why this is a distinct 4400 safety branch
The canonical safety index, research plan and progress history were searched for automatic/powered doors, closing-edge hazards, sensor substitution, and robot-fence removal before selection. Earlier GUARD/ESCAPE/RESET, ENABLE, LASER-ENC, M66/PAUSE and cell-entry work covers **access interlocking, egress, and ordinary CNC commands**, but not this *second hazard created by the moving guard itself* or a **configuration-change deletion of an alternate safeguard**. Do not reopen the graduated 3100/3200/3500 machine-specialization tracks. The source examples are Haas OEM implementations, not OpenPressBrake as-built evidence.

## Hazard/authority decomposition
| Contract | Hazard / proposition | Correct authority and physical witness | Invalid shortcut |
|---|---|---|---|
| DOOR-CLOSE | Fingers/hand/body caught between powered leaf and frame or adjacent leaf; obstruction at closing edge | Risk-assessed, independently qualified protective edge/obstruction prevention, limited-force or geometry measure appropriate to selected door; validate actual closing/reversal with qualified safe method | “Motor current went high”, “Input 128 toggled”, “CAN alarm eventually appeared” |
| ENCLOSURE | Chips/projectiles, rotating spindle, robot and cutting motion exposed when enclosure not secured | Qualified guard/interlock and stopping/locking/occupancy architecture; physical hazard cessation proof where necessary | Door open/closed ordinary proximity switch alone, spindle M5 command, program M00/M01 |
| CHANGE | Removing fenced robot/APL but retaining an edge-less powered door | Formal CHG review, restore/install selected edge protection or another validated measure *before* standalone operation | “Robot was disconnected so fence is unnecessary” |
| RESTART | Cycle Start both closes door and may later command machining | Independent safety rearm, fresh ordinary start, known-clear guarded area, validated sequencing | Reset accepted, cell-safe bit HIGH, door command complete |
| SERVICE | Door track/motor/rollers, stored energy, falling leaf, pinched edge cable | Selected electrical/pneumatic/mechanical isolation, physical door support, qualified maintenance procedure and revalidation | Powering off normal CNC motion or trusting edge input |

**INFERENCE — conditional failure chain:** A machine originally uses a fenced robot/HE Autodoor configuration without an edge sensor. During robot removal the fence is discarded but the automatic door remains. The next Cycle Start commands door closure while a person reaches into the aperture. A normal motor-current algorithm may react only after an obstruction produces load, while no edge channel exists. A door-closed switch may subsequently satisfy an unrelated guard condition. This is a **plausible change-control failure**, not an observed injury, fault, or assertion about a particular Haas or LinuxCNC installation.

**INFERENCE — human factors:** The cheap/easy-to-use architecture includes a permanently accessible door-edge service kit, protected cable routing, a conspicuous as-built safeguard label, easy inspection/replacement, and a machine-configuration checklist that makes removal of the robot/fence automatically reopen the door risk assessment. A retrofit whose only route to operation is defeating a troublesome door lock is a design failure; reduce nuisance trips by correcting alignment/cabling rather than bypassing the sensor.

## Adversarial review (documentary; no simulated machine tests)
- **Current limit set too high / motor stalled / low-friction obstruction:** Motor-current monitoring does not by itself demonstrate a safe pinch-force envelope. Mechanical contact and stopping behavior require selected machine verification.
- **Edge cable pinched/open/shorted:** OEM INPUT 128 functional check cannot establish open-/short-circuit diagnostic coverage. Determine selected sensor/controller topology, supervision and fault reaction; UNKNOWN.
- **Door fully open switch stuck:** End-of-opening process completion is not the same as guard-closed/locked or a personnel-safe opening. Separate witnesses and functions.
- **Robot fence removed / cell-safe spoofed:** A permissive ordinary bit cannot replace removed physical guarding. Treat automation topology and parameter changes as CHG, invalidate prior VAL evidence.
- **Reset held / power return:** Re-energization must not silently drive door or machining; independent safety reset/rearm and fresh command must be designed and proven for the selected architecture.
- **Door height changed:** Haas specifically links adjustment/shims to lock operation; revalidate lock, edge coverage, cable clearance, mechanical retention, and actual stop behavior after maintenance.
- **Person trapped in cell:** Edge contact and door-reversal alone do not prove inside escape or safe machine motion; retain independent egress and occupancy measures.

## Same-session exact OEM source-depth: CAN versus 8M are NOT interchangeable
The full original manufacturer-hosted English service page **was directly opened** at https://legacy-aem.haascnc.com/service/online-manuals/autodoor---service-manual/autdoor---troubleshooting.html (TG0044 Rev E 09/2026 and TG0019 sections). This **supersedes the source-ledger caveat** that TG0044 was available only through indexed text. The direct modern single-article URL redirects, but the original manufacturer's legacy English combined manual contains the complete detailed sequence.

**DOC-CONFIRMED — CAN DC close sequence, TG0044:** motor engage → door unlock → drive close; software monitors reported current and reverses after its configurable excess-current duration; CAN firmware detects over-commanded current for >250 ms, enters its *CAN-node* protection state, signals software and software reverses; a missing close switch produces an alarm/message. These are described **normal-controller reactions**, not a documented independently qualified protective-edge safety circuit. The text calls a CAN-node condition “safe state”; it must **not** be promoted into a machine functional-safety claim without a safety manual and system validation.

**DOC-CONFIRMED — 8M DC, TG0019:** separate architecture uses a pneumatic cylinder to engage a friction wheel, a 12 V motor, timer-based opening and a door-closed switch to stop closing. Its published close sequence energizes the close relay, waits for door-closed input, has a timeout alarm, waits a further configured **P1405 motor-on hold time**, then de-energizes close relay and permits program continuation. This 8M sequence does **not** document the CAN current-detection/reversal logic. Do not assume CAN firmware collision detection is present on an 8M machine; do not treat P1405 as a safe force/time limit. The manual's headings for open sequences with/without the fully-open switch appear internally inverted relative to the described steps; record this as an **OEM text inconsistency**, not a basis for swapping sensor logic.

**DOC-CONFIRMED — variant/maintenance risk:** TG0019 identifies an IO.xml output mapping fault after field installation or software update, and notes that a jammed motor can shut down its 12 V supply. Neither is proof of a valid protective response. The original Haas maintenance page https://haascnc.com/service/online-manuals/autodoor---service-manual/autodoor---maintenance recommends chip removal from rails as needed, door alignment checks every six months and air-line inspection every six months; those are product maintenance examples, **not safety proof-test intervals**.

**Adversarial change:** A service technician migrates an 8M installation to CAN and reuses its old timed door-close expectation, or vice versa. The ordinary fault reaction changes and a “works on my machine” diagnostic assumption fails. Record controller type, firmware, option/parameter image, edge device and selected guard/lock architecture before any safety claim. Physical selected-machine validation remains UNKNOWN/NOT RUN.

## Same-session independent manufacturer example: safety-edge *system*, not a switch bit
**DOC-CONFIRMED direct original Schmersal catalogue, selected product SE-400 C item 101153920**, https://products.schmersal.com/en_US/se-400-c-evaluation-unit-101153920 . This is an inspectable *alternative architecture example*, **not** a compatible Haas replacement or selected OpenPressBrake part. The manufacturer pairs SE 40/SE 70 optical/tactile edge with SE-400 C evaluation unit; it lists wire-break, cross-circuit, earth-fault detection, feedback monitoring, start/reset-edge functions, two safety contacts and a separate status output. It illustrates monitored downstream contactors KA/KB. The **32 ms maximum is an evaluation-unit reaction specification**, not total door stopping time or an acceptable finger-crush response. Product classification **up to PL e, Category 4** and PFH 2.47e-8/h are **product/defined-use claims**, not the complete machine SF achievement; the catalogue qualifies the PFH with cycle assumptions and warns that correct installation/connection is required. Auto-reset is a configurable product capability, **not permission to auto-restart hazardous door or machine motion**.

**DOC-CONFIRMED direct original Schmersal SE-P40 profile catalogue**, https://products.schmersal.com/en_CH/se-p40-nbr1250-pack-rubber-prof-125-m-101174453 . Manufacturer explicitly warns that at **approximately 60 mm at each end of an SE 40 edge**, finger protection is not guaranteed. The published profile must be paired with separate aluminium rail, emitter/receiver and selected evaluator; merely ordering the rubber profile is not a working safety system. This creates a **geometry/end-zone hazard** even if the electronic evaluator and its diagnostics are healthy. The quoted 60 mm is a *selected Schmersal product caveat*, not a universal allowable gap or machine safe distance.

**DOC-CONFIRMED direct original Schmersal SE-100 C item 101153919**, https://products.schmersal.com/en_US/se-100-c-evaluation-unit-101153919 , has a different documented **up-to-PL-c / Category 1** architecture and one safety contact, even though it can monitor SE 40/70 edges. Thus **same rubber edge ≠ same safety function** when evaluator/feedback/reset/final-element family changes. This is a useful low-complexity product-family counterexample; do not select a part by matching “edge sensor” or reaction-time alone.

**INFERENCE — independent failure-path review:** Edge actuated at end blind zone, sensor correctly evaluated yet the person's finger still encounters a nip; or a replacement swaps SE-400 C for SE-100 C and silently changes the safety architecture. The physical geometry and complete safety function must be validated; product ratings and a clean controller status cannot close the case. This example does not establish electrical compatibility with Haas INPUT 128, Haas door-drive power, or a particular press brake. No lab, physical test or selected machine claim.

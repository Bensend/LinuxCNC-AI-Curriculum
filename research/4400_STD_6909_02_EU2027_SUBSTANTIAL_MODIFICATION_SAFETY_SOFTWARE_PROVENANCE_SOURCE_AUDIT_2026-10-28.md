## Four distinct questions that must not be collapsed

**Q1 — Which machine-family standard exists?** ISO 6909:2026 is a newly published press-brake-specific standard. The older EN 12622 and ANSI B11.3-2022 remain meaningful in their respective date/jurisdiction contexts. ISO 16092-1 excludes press brakes; hydraulic power alone does not move a press brake into the ISO 16092-3 hydraulic-press family. The 2026 ISO abstract does not retroactively declare an older press brake compliant or unsafe.

**Q2 — Which EU legal regime is relevant at the claimed date?** The September 2026 Commission decision cites EN ISO 6909:2026 under the **Machinery Directive**. The new **Machinery Regulation** generally applies 2027-01-20 and needs its **own** implementing citation list. A summary spreadsheet row showing `20.01.2027` is **not proof that EN ISO 6909 itself is obsolete on that day**. The Commission specifically cautions that the date may be a consequence of the Directive's repeal. A fresh official OJ/Commission check is needed for any claim about presumption of conformity **after** the transition. Even before that date, a standard citation only addresses the essential requirements within its covered scope, not a blanket certification.

**Q3 — Does a retrofit count as a substantial modification?** Article **3(16)** is **cumulative**, not a shortcut based on the word `retrofit`: an after-market/in-service physical **or digital** modification must not have been foreseen/planned by the manufacturer, must create a new hazard or increase existing risk, **and** require either (a) guards/protective devices whose processing requires modification of the existing safety control system or (b) additional stability/mechanical-strength protection. Article **18** treats the substantial modifier as a manufacturer for the affected machinery or, for a multi-machine assembly, the safety-affected machinery shown by the risk assessment. It contains a limited exception for a **non-professional user modifying their own machinery for their own use**. This exception is **not** a general safety exemption and cannot be presumed for a commercial machine or a U.S. installation. Article 52 also contains transition provisions for products placed on the market under the Directive before 2027-01-20. **Do not infer that any specific OpenPressBrake retrofit triggers or avoids these articles** without machine identity, country, timing, change analysis and qualified review.

**Q4 — Which safety software and machine state can actually be proven?** Annex III **1.1.9** requires protection against corruption and accessible identification of safety-critical installed software, and evidence of legitimate/illegitimate intervention or software/configuration modification. Annex III **1.2.1(f)** describes a **five-year enabled tracing log** of safety software uploads/interventions for the specified conformity-demonstration purpose, under the Regulation's applicability. These are **not** a universal requirement to retain every ordinary LinuxCNC/FPGA diagnostic sample for five years, nor proof that a particular installed Pilz model supports a named checksum or logging menu. An offline project checksum, software upload log or HMI READY indication **cannot** prove that the installed program is active, that independent final elements have opened, or that Y1/Y2 are physically held.

## Practical safety change-control chain (no energized tests)

1. **Machine identity and date:** existing Stage A A/F record of country, manufacture/put-into-service date, original machine type, selected safety program/model, before/after configuration, and whether automatic loading or additional equipment is present. An old machine can be used as a design-reference case for modern safety methods without silently claiming new-standard legal applicability.
2. **Change classification:** list each proposed hardware, wiring, parameter, software, guard, mode, hydraulic and maintenance-access change. Identify whether the OEM planned it, what new hazard/risk it creates, and whether protection requires modifying the safety control system or structural measures. Record a qualified jurisdiction-specific classification; do not mark all safety improvements automatically `substantial`.
3. **Software provenance:** for any safety-related application, preserve approved revision/identity, authorized tool and read-only retrieval method, active installed version/CRC **category where supported**, change author/date/intent, configuration delta and restoration procedure. Separate ordinary LinuxCNC/HAL/FPGA release hashes from the **independent** safety application and its manufacturer-approved identity mechanism.
4. **Independent authority:** map each change to `SRS -> AUTH -> DEP/ARC -> PHY -> VAL -> CHG`, including Pilz/AKAS/11K2, Y1/Y2 independent stopping/holding, hydraulic pressure zones, guards, muting, E-stop and deliberate new start. A software version or safety signature is **configuration identity**, not physical function validation.
5. **Fault and bypass review:** explicitly review partial power restoration, stale ordinary commands, switched L7/L07 returns, permissive wire 75, removed/modified guards, service bypasses, and replacement parts. The safer option must be straightforward to reinstall, diagnose and operate; any bypass that operators will predictably use is a design issue, not a completed validation.
6. **Release evidence:** qualified documentary review before any bounded isolated/remote physical test; require the independent final-element physical witness and documented revalidation after material changes. No people exposed if the minimum safe-to-operate threshold is not met. `G0/G1 HOLD` remains the default until verified.

### Adversarial desk examples (unscored, not a new challenge bank)

- **A:** An old press brake receives a new normal FPGA controller while the Pilz application is unchanged. **UNKNOWN**, not automatically `substantial` or `non-substantial`: assess whether the retrofit was planned, hazards increased, and protection architecture changed; independently validate retained safety functions.
- **B:** A robot loader is added and the press-brake Type-C file is presented as covering the whole cell. **Reject:** ISO 6909's public scope excludes automatic loading/unloading safety; analyze the interface and occupied-space protection separately.
- **C:** A 2027 spreadsheet shows `EN ISO 6909 — end 20.01.2027`. **Reject:** do not infer technical withdrawal; check the **new Regulation citation** and the Commission's general sunset warning.
- **D:** A signed safety program export and five-year software log are present but the ram can drift after a stop. **Reject physical PASS:** software provenance does not replace load holding, pressure/energy isolation and qualified measured stop/hold witnesses.

# 4400-MECH-01 — Twelve unscored mechanical stored-energy reasoning cases

**PUBLIC PRACTICE ONLY — NOT BLIND, NOT SCORED, NOT A PHYSICAL TEST PLAN.** Use [MECH-01 module](4400_MECH_01_SPRING_BALANCER_GAS_STRUT_THREE_FAILURE_PATHS_MODULE_2026-10-26.md) and primary manufacturer audits. No answer key or hidden evaluator material is published. For each case record the **broken link**, **actual device coverage**, **missing physical witness**, **independent safety/energy boundary**, **safe human workflow**, and **UNKNOWNs**.

1. **Spring ruptures:** A named ENDO EWF spring breaks, the drum/rope are intact and the tool is suspended. Which mechanism is documented, and which unverified conditions prevent a machine-level safe claim?
2. **Top hook shears:** The main overhead hanger fails while the balancer body, rope and tool remain attached. Which separately anchored mechanism is described, and why would a backup attached to the same hanger be weak?
3. **Tool rope parts:** A wire-rope termination fails below the drum. Why can neither the internal spring-rupture pin nor the upper auxiliary chain automatically establish tool retention?
4. **Marketing label:** A catalog says "fall arrest." Contrast ENDO's spring-rupture description with CMCO/Yale YBA's rope-whip arrest after a break. Does locking a broken rope prove the detached tool stays up? What exact model/manual evidence is required?
5. **Translation conflict:** The ENDO manual both describes spring-rupture fall arrest and cautions that the device is not designed to prevent tools falling. State the narrow defensible conclusion and how to resolve the conflict without pretending it vanished.
6. **Wrong weight:** A technician engages the drum lock, removes a tool and installs a heavier one. Which documented restriction and release hazard applies? Why is a maintenance lock not necessarily personal LOTO?
7. **Unloaded cable:** A worker pulls an unloaded balancer rope and lets go. Identify the stored mechanical-energy hazard independent of LinuxCNC or E-stop.
8. **Strut seal leak:** A raised heavy guard relies on a gas spring; safety controller says guard open and STO is active. What separate physical support and evidence are needed before reach-in?
9. **Shared bracket:** A gas-spring locking shroud engages correctly but shares a cracked upper pivot with the cover. What common-cause path invalidates the simple "engaged" claim?
10. **Improvised pipe:** A mechanic proposes a loose pipe over a strut as a support. Which load cases, geometry, engagement, material and restraint questions must be resolved by qualified design rather than guessed?
11. **Pressure release:** A strut must be replaced. Why is motor disconnect insufficient, and what does the manufacturer say about supporting the load and disassembling pressurized springs?
12. **Cross-machine transfer:** A press brake has a heavy hinged electrical enclosure but no evidence of spring balancing the ram. Distinguish the enclosure hazard from Y1/Y2 hydraulic holding; state what would make the optional spring module N/A.

**Assessment rule:** do not score these as blind competency. No live fault injection, dropping tools, loosening brackets, removing pressurized struts or placing people under unsupported loads. A fresh evaluator must choose an unseen case and preserve hidden oracle separation.

# 4400-HYD-SURGE-01 — Hose peak pressure, gauge blindness and hazardous proof-test fixtures
**2026-10-28 UTC | Original manufacturer/regulator documentary audit | NON-GRADUATED. No pressure test, physical machine observation or compute.**

## Exact unresolved question and predecessor check
Can an apparently healthy press-brake hydraulic pressure gauge and a passed static pressure test justify a replacement hose assembly or repaired line when its *actual peak* pressure and the integrity of the test fixture are unknown? **No.** This is a narrower, distinct discriminator from existing lessons:
- [INJ-01](4400_HYDRAULIC_PINHOLE_INJECTION_HOSE_FAILURE_ISOLATION_SOURCE_AUDIT_2026-10-24.md) already teaches invisible injection jets, trapped pressure and urgent medical care. This audit does **not** claim to discover injection injury.
- [HOSE-ROUTE-01](4400_HOSE_ROUTE_01_GATES_2025_HOSE_ASSEMBLY_ROUTING_CLAMP_TWIST_SERVICE_PROVENANCE_AUDIT_2026-10-26.md) already teaches routing, crimp compatibility, pressure surges as a *selection consideration*, and service access. This audit adds the original Parker **instrument-response limitation** and HSE **test-fixture safety**.
- [HYD-CUSH-02](4400_HYD_CUSH_02_AGGRESSIVE_HYDRAULICS_INTERNAL_CUSHION_PRESSURE_VS_PORT_GAUGE_AUDIT_2026-10-27.md) already teaches an *internally isolated cushion pocket* whose pressure is absent at a port. Here the question is different: a gauge connected to the **correct line** may still fail to capture a **short-duration transient**.
- [HYD-DYN-01](4400_HYD_DYN_01_BUCHER_RS_ABRUPT_ARREST_AUTOREOPEN_AND_CFS_DIFFERENTIATION_SOURCE_AUDIT_2026-10-28.md) already teaches a rupture valve's abrupt-arrest dynamics. This audit does not infer a particular transient magnitude from that mechanism.

## Original sources, exact sections and limits
1. **Parker Hannifin, Parker Publication 4400-B.1**, *Safety Guide for Selecting and Using Hose, Tubing, Fittings, Connectors, Conductors, Valves and Related Accessories*, manufacturer-hosted compilation **updated 2024-06-06**; original PDF: https://www.parker.com/content/dam/Parker-com/Literature/Hose-Products-Division/Industrial-Hose-Safety-Guide.pdf . **§2.2, PDF p. 3 / printed p. 144**: hose/tube/fitting assembly maximum working pressure is bounded by the **lowest-rated constituent**; transient peaks must also remain below rated maximum working pressure; a mechanical pressure gauge does not establish those peaks; manufacturer describes millisecond-scale electrical measurement as normally necessary. Burst-pressure test ratings do **not** authorize operation at burst pressure. **§5.2–5.6, PDF p. 5 / printed p. 146**: specific hose damage requires shutdown/replacement; pinhole streams may be hard to see, check valves can retain pressure after pump-off, and a failed hose must not be patched. PDF p. 3 and p. 5 page images independently inspected for wording. **DOC-CONFIRMED manufacturer general selection/maintenance guidance, not installed-machine evidence.**
2. **UK Health and Safety Executive, GS4 Fourth edition (2012)**, *Safety requirements for pressure testing*, original nine-page PDF https://www.hse.gov.uk/pubns/gs4.pdf ; **PDF pp. 1–5, 8**. **DOC-CONFIRMED regulatory guidance:** a proof test itself adds hazardous stored energy and possible connector missiles, hose whip and injection; the written safe system must define test article, medium, rated fittings, remote controls, segregation, pressure control, draining and pressure verification; repeated test-fitting cycles can degrade threads; close inspection of an energized assembly is hazardous; remote observation and suitable shielding/exclusion reduce exposure; reused/re-ended hoses are explicitly discouraged. GS4's example enclosure material, test media, and testing stages are **not** a universal design recipe for an assembled press brake. HSE guidance is not an OpenPressBrake validation plan.
3. **HSE FOD 4-2014, 2014-09-23**, https://www.hse.gov.uk/safetybulletins/hydraulic-injection-injury.htm ; original fatal grease-fitting incident and urgent medical response are already documented in INJ-01 and used only as **predecessor safety context**, not a new discovery. Its anecdotal low-pressure injury estimate is **not** a safe threshold.
4. **Gates 2025 hydraulic pocket guide**, original manufacturer evidence already traced in HOSE-ROUTE-01: pressure **including surges** is a hose selection input. It does not by itself prove that a specific gauge, logger, hose or test station captures a peak.

## Four independent propositions (do not merge)
| Proposition | What a reading/test might actually show | What it cannot show by itself |
|---|---|---|
| **P1: Static line pressure** | A calibrated gauge connected to a known, open hydraulic volume may witness its pressure at the time of observation | Maximum short-duration surge, disconnected/trapped volume or pressure inside an isolated cushion pocket |
| **P2: Dynamic line peak** | A qualified fast sensor and acquisition chain at the **correct physical volume**, with justified bandwidth, calibration and timestamp, may measure a transient envelope | Safety PL/SIL, other uninstrumented segments, guaranteed worst-case operating envelope without a validated test plan |
| **P3: Hose assembly rating** | Exact hose + fitting + adapter manufacturer's published **working** ratings, compatibility and installation geometry | Safe operation at burst-test pressure, a fabricated assembly's quality, absence of damage or unmeasured surges |
| **P4: Pressure test acceptance** | A correctly planned isolated test may establish specified integrity/function of the tested assembly in the tested configuration | Machine-level Y1/Y2 stopping, gravity holding, future fatigue life, guard adequacy, or safe exposure of test personnel |

## Failure-path analysis (engineering inference; selected machine UNKNOWN)
- **SURGE-1 — False normal:** normal panel gauge displays an acceptable steady value while a fast valve reversal or other transient (if present) exceeds the selected hose/fitting working limit. **DOC-CONFIRMED Parker gauge limitation; actual transient source and magnitude UNKNOWN.**
- **SURGE-2 — Weakest link:** replacement hose has a high advertised burst figure but a lower-rated fitting, adapter or crimp system; assembly's maximum **working** rating is limited by its weakest constituent. **DOC-CONFIRMED Parker; installed components UNKNOWN.**
- **SURGE-3 — Wrong pressure volume:** a sensor at pump outlet reads normal while a check-isolated Y1/Y2 branch, rod-side chamber or local cushion volume experiences different pressure. **INFERENCE from topology and HYD-CUSH-02; selected schematic UNKNOWN.**
- **SURGE-4 — Proof-test injury:** a well-intentioned mechanic pressurizes a newly repaired line near an unqualified temporary test fitting and stands beside it to watch for seepage. Failure may launch a fitting, whip the hose or inject fluid. **DOC-CONFIRMED HSE GS4 general hazard; no such incident asserted on OpenPressBrake.**
- **SURGE-5 — Evidence invalidated by repair:** a passed stationary test precedes a different installed hose route, cyclic motion, replacement fitting, fluid/temperature change or later transient. **INFERENCE**: scope-limited evidence does not automatically transfer.
- **SURGE-6 — Pressure logger false confidence:** the sensor is high bandwidth but sampling/filtering, plumbing restriction or acquisition timestamp loses the peak. **INFERENCE**, not a claim that any actual logger has this fault.

## Practical architecture: make the safer procedure the default
**Ordinary control:** LinuxCNC/FPGA may command proportional valves, capture pressure traces and flag anomalies; these functions are diagnostic and **not** independent personnel-safety authority.

**Monitoring/diagnostics:** provide clearly labeled, manufacturer-qualified **zone-specific** pressure witness points, accessible outside potential jet/ram/whip areas. Document sensor identity, range, bandwidth, mounting/port topology, sampling, calibration, recording and maximum observed operating conditions. A visible analog gauge can be useful for an appropriate **static** task while remaining unsuitable as a **peak** witness. Do not promise a universal sampling frequency or infer that a transient is absent because a logger missed it.

**Safety-related control:** only the qualified independent safety chain may own protective stop/restart decisions. No hose rating or peak trace substitutes for actual Y1/Y2 stop/hold and guard validation.

**Physical mechanisms:** correct hose/fitting working ratings and compatible assemblies; protected routing; independent gravity restraint; isolating/bleeding each stored-pressure volume; remote or enclosed qualified proof testing; barriers/shields and exclusion; tested/traceable pressure fixtures. A safe test point should be easier to access than an improvised cracked fitting. Dedicated inspected connectors and simple replacement kits reduce foreseeable shortcuts.

**Human factors:** keep an approved test setup with known-compatible connectors, a rejection bin for worn parts, obvious labels for pressure zones, remote readout and drain, and a short technician checklist. Avoid placing a test gauge behind a guard that must be defeated to read it. The cheapest effective change may be an accessible test port or fixture, not a more elaborate CNC fault screen.

## Qualified evidence intake (reuse existing Stage A; no new owner packet)
When an actual machine record is available, qualified reviewers must establish **A/B/F** exact Y1/Y2 hydraulic schematic, hose/fitting/adapter IDs, component working ratings, any accumulators/check-isolated volumes, existing pressure-test/peak records and revision identity; **E/G** guard/access/exclusion and deliberate restart; **PHY/VAL/PT** separate evidence for static pressure, transient envelope and selected assembly integrity. If no suitable pressure instrumentation exists, record **UNKNOWN**, do not invent a transient or prescribe a live test. Only design an isolated, written, qualified test if source/design reasoning cannot resolve the specific question. Never create a live pinhole, force a burst-valve trip or loosen a pressurized connection to gather evidence.

## Adversarial reviewer (unscored documentary reasoning)
1. Is a gauge physically connected to the volume being claimed, and can it capture the **time scale** of the claim?
2. Does a hose's burst rating authorize that pressure in operation? **No.**
3. Is a correctly selected fast sensor sufficient if an isolating valve hides another chamber? **No.**
4. Is a pressure test a harmless way to verify a new assembly? **No; test fixture and personnel exclusion have their own safety case.**
5. Does a passed test prove the ram cannot fall? **No; machine-level load-holding remains separate.**
6. If an operator cannot read the proper port without reaching behind a guard, which part of the design should change?
7. What change (hose, fitting, valve, firmware, flow profile, fluid, sensor, guard) makes prior evidence stale?

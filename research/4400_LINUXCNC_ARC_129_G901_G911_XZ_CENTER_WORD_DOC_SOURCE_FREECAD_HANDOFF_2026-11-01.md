## Documentation conflict: same-revision evidence
The **G90.1/G91.1 subsection** of both pinned official manuals says that absolute-center mode needs I/J in XY or **J/K in XZ**. But their own G2/G3 plane syntax correctly lists **I/K for XZ (G18)** and **J/K for YZ (G19)**. In both interpreter revisions, G18 explicitly rejects a **J** word (`NCE_J_WORD_GIVEN_FOR_ARC_IN_XZ_PLANE`), and checks **I** and **K** for absolute-center mode. The G90.1 subsection therefore appears to have written *XZ* where *YZ* was intended. The editorial cause is **INFERENCE**; the DOC/SOURCE disagreement is directly observable.

| Plane | Required center words with G90.1 | Forbidden out-of-plane center word | Source path |
|---|---|---|---|
| G17 XY | I and J | K | `convert_arc` XY branch |
| G18 XZ | I and K | J | `convert_arc` XZ branch |
| G19 YZ | J and K | I | `convert_arc` YZ branch |

With G91.1, a missing in-plane offset can be treated as zero; with G90.1, the two center coordinates must be explicitly provided. In all three planes R-format arcs use a different path and must not mix R with IJK.

## Actual interpreter call chain, state and observable effect
1. Interpreter `rs274ngc_pre.cc` **initialization** sets `_setup.ijk_distance_mode = MODE_INCREMENTAL` for compatibility. Do not confuse this with `setup::setup()`'s constructor field initializer, which is absolute before the interpreter initialization override. A program that explicitly emits G91.1 makes its intent independent of assumptions about prior modal state or host lifecycle.
2. `convert_ijk_distance_mode` handles G90.1/G91.1 by changing `settings->ijk_distance_mode`; `convert_arc` checks that mode and the active plane. This is **interpreter userspace**, not HAL, a servo thread or an encoder.
3. In G18, `convert_arc` passes **(Z,X)** as the canonical in-plane coordinates and **(K,I)** as the center pair to `convert_arc2`. `arc_data_ijk` uses the supplied center pair as absolute coordinates under G90.1 or adds it to the starting point under G91.1.
4. The function checks start/end radii before emitting `ARC_FEED`. An out-of-plane J or missing I/K in G90.1 is rejected before this canonical arc. Successful canonical output proceeds through Task/motion; no claim about real motion or physical clearance is made.
5. G90 (linear endpoint mode) is **independent** of G90.1 (arc-center mode). G90 does not force G91.1.

**Source-derived geometry discriminator (metric, G18, no cutter compensation):** start X10 Z0; end X0 Z10; center X0 Z0. Under G90.1 use `G2 X0 Z10 I0 K0`. Under G91.1 use `G2 X0 Z10 I-10 K0`. Both describe radius 10 mm from the same center. Copying `I0 K0` from the absolute example into G91.1 would put the center at the starting point and hit the zero-radius rejection. These are calculated expectations, **NOT RUN**.

## FreeCAD handoff: narrow verified claim
At pinned FreeCAD commit, LinuxCNC post default preamble is `G17 G54 G40 G49 G80 G90`; the default safety block is `G40 G49 G80`. Neither includes G91.1. The shared `Processor::_convert_arc_move` calls `_convert_move`, which serializes the Path.Command center parameters and drops the **out-of-plane** center word for G17/G18/G19. `MachineState.Tracked` contains XYZABC/F/S/T/etc. but **not IJK**, so the general duplicate-parameter suppression does **not** remove repeated IJK merely because their numeric values match. This avoids a tempting but false diagnosis.

**Conditional integration exposure:** if a controller/interpreter enters an exported program with G90.1 still active, the post's G90 preamble does not correct that mode. IJK offsets produced for incremental-center semantics may then fail validation (missing center word) or be interpreted as absolute centers. The pinned source does **not** establish that a normal FreeCAD operation leaves the controller in G90.1, nor that a particular generated job contains malformed arcs. Program-end/reset behavior and machine-specific interpreter lifecycle must be verified separately; adding `G91.1` explicitly to the preamble is a robust CAM contract *when the post's IJK are confirmed relative*. Do not insert G90.1 merely to follow the erroneous XZ sentence.

## Practical transfer and adversarial reasoning
For mill/router G17, plasma DXF posts, and lathe G18, enforce explicit plane, units, G90, G91.1 **or** a fully verified absolute-center contract, plus correct center-word pair. Check a center against both start and end points after any DXF INSERT rotation/scaling. For an XZ lathe arc emitted as `G18 G90.1 G2 X0 Z10 J0 K0`, the misleading premise is “the official G90.1 subsection says J/K, so LinuxCNC should accept it”; source rejects J. For a G18 G91.1 post that emits `I-10 K0`, switching to G90.1 without transforming center coordinates is wrong even if both words are present.

**Uncertainty/promotion:** runtime error text, exact canonical output, FreeCAD generated IJK semantics for a specific job, and modal persistence across host invocation are unverified. They block calling this an executed/validated integration. They do **not** reopen already graduated levels. Independent personnel safety and OpenPressBrake G0/G1 physical gate remain authoritative.

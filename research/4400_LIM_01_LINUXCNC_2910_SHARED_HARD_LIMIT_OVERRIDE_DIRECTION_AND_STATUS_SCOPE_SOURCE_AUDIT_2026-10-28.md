# 4400-LIM-01 — LinuxCNC v2.9.10 shared-limit recovery, direction ambiguity and status-scope source audit
**2026-10-28 | SOURCE-CONFIRMED pinned upstream; DOC-CONFIRMED upstream manual; NO RUNTIME/PHYSICAL TEST**

## Engineering question and predecessor check
Can a builder rely on LinuxCNC's ordinary limit-switch override to determine which direction is physically safe after a hard-limit trip, particularly when the positive and negative switches share a signal? Does the per-joint `override-limits` status prove that particular joint was overridden? This is a source-level follow-on to the existing 4400 LinuxCNC E-stop/HALUI, mode/stop authority and maintenance-recovery studies, **not** a new personnel-safety function. A repository code search before this lesson found no earlier `joint_jog_ok` / `overrideLimitMask` trace.

## Pinned upstream evidence (LinuxCNC tag `v2.9.10`)
- **SOURCE-CONFIRMED — command mask scope.** [`src/emc/motion/command.c` blob `0b79061d39be915bbea548b2c33bb794d31ab302`, lines 675–707](https://github.com/LinuxCNC/linuxcnc/blob/v2.9.10/src/emc/motion/command.c#L675-L707): `EMCMOT_OVERRIDE_LIMITS` with negative joint number clears the mask; otherwise it rebuilds the mask for **all joints' currently tripped** positive/negative inputs, not just the command's nominal joint argument, clears joint error flags and resets the `overriding` tracker. It does **not** record a physical direction of escape.
- **SOURCE-CONFIRMED — critical ambiguity.** [Same blob, `joint_jog_ok`, lines 105–150](https://github.com/LinuxCNC/linuxcnc/blob/v2.9.10/src/emc/motion/command.c#L105-L150): if both negative and positive override bits are set for a joint, the routine explicitly says a shared input makes the safe direction unknowable and returns success **before its subsequent hard-direction and soft-position checks**. If only one hard limit is asserted, the normal direction checks reject a jog farther into that asserted limit. This is **not** evidence that every subsequent planner check is bypassed or that physical travel actually occurs.
- **SOURCE-CONFIRMED — ordinary fault suppression.** [`src/emc/motion/control.c` blob `b6e45d590c7d11030654d8f7d13d118f581b6439`, lines 801–831](https://github.com/LinuxCNC/linuxcnc/blob/v2.9.10/src/emc/motion/control.c#L801-L831): active, enabled joint hard-limit faults are gated by the corresponding override-mask bits; homing also has its own special path. Neither is an independent safety-rated channel.
- **SOURCE-CONFIRMED — transient, but not a timed lease.** [`control.c`, lines 1260–1286](https://github.com/LinuxCNC/linuxcnc/blob/v2.9.10/src/emc/motion/control.c#L1260-L1286), [1404–1459](https://github.com/LinuxCNC/linuxcnc/blob/v2.9.10/src/emc/motion/control.c#L1404-L1459), and [980–999](https://github.com/LinuxCNC/linuxcnc/blob/v2.9.10/src/emc/motion/control.c#L980-L999) clear the override when a tracked jog ends or when entering coordinated mode. `command.c` also permits explicit cancellation. No general wall-clock expiry is demonstrated by these source paths; do not present it as a timed, fail-safe bypass.
- **SOURCE-CONFIRMED — per-joint display can mask global scope.** [`src/emc/task/taskintf.cc` blob `96a041d1feef8560f37159a2f18b109171f084c3`, lines 730–742, 955–967](https://github.com/LinuxCNC/linuxcnc/blob/v2.9.10/src/emc/task/taskintf.cc#L955-L967) sets **each** joint's `overrideLimits` status to `!!emcmotStatus.overrideLimitMask` (a global any-override boolean). [`src/emc/usr_intf/halui.cc` blob `6aa51870512c9d29919452aaec0377338e350a04`, lines 2231–2239](https://github.com/LinuxCNC/linuxcnc/blob/v2.9.10/src/emc/usr_intf/halui.cc#L2231-L2239) exports this per-joint status. Therefore a true `halui.joint.N.override-limits` is **not** a source-confirmed per-joint mask witness.
- **SOURCE-CONFIRMED — homing is a distinct exception.** [`src/emc/motion/homing.c` blob `13f74f43a1169606e87c7bd8d48f44a2cad6fa74`, lines 183–200](https://github.com/LinuxCNC/linuxcnc/blob/v2.9.10/src/emc/motion/homing.c#L183-L200) aborts homing on asserted limit unless `HOME_IGNORE_LIMITS` is set. [`src/emc/task/taskintf.cc`, lines 312–322](https://github.com/LinuxCNC/linuxcnc/blob/v2.9.10/src/emc/task/taskintf.cc#L312-L322) transmits that flag separately from ordinary jog-limit override.
- **DOC-CONFIRMED — upstream operating warning.** [`docs/src/gui/axis.adoc` blob `24c79cc3bf8d6cdc103659a64abfb7ea8f32d87f`, lines 528–535](https://github.com/LinuxCNC/linuxcnc/blob/v2.9.10/docs/src/gui/axis.adoc#L528-L535) says ordinary override is a one-jog recovery, with correct-direction enforcement **when positive and negative limit switches are separate**, and that soft limits are a separate constraint. [`docs/src/config/ini-homing.adoc` blob `1793d78d3f450c2a66bd96f54923a1be61b0a937`, lines 43–50, 169–178](https://github.com/LinuxCNC/linuxcnc/blob/v2.9.10/docs/src/config/ini-homing.adoc#L169-L178) warns that wrong-side homing with `HOME_IGNORE_LIMITS` can cause a hard crash and that the ignore flag is per homing joint, not a global safety policy.

## Source-derived state table (paper reasoning, NOT TEST-CONFIRMED)
| Inputs/condition | Ordinary LinuxCNC behavior supported by pinned source | Physical safety proposition |
|---|---|---|
| Distinct + limit asserted; ordinary override selected | Command mask includes + bit; free-jog request farther + is rejected by `joint_jog_ok`; a negative request can pass its direction test subject to other conditions. | **UNKNOWN** until physical direction, switch integrity, travel margin and hazards independently verified. |
| Distinct - limit asserted | Symmetric - direction rejection; a positive request can pass direction test subject to other conditions. | **UNKNOWN**; no personnel-protection claim. |
| Both + and - flags asserted for one joint (shared signal or fault) | Both bits masked; `joint_jog_ok` returns before its local direction and soft-position tests because direction is ambiguous. Other planner checks may still constrain travel. | **NO SAFE DIRECTION CAN BE INFERRED** from that signal; treat recovery as engineering fault investigation, not routine operator jog. |
| Another joint tripped while nominal override command references one joint | Mask generation scans all joints and includes all currently tripped limits. | Command's joint argument does not prove single-joint exception scope. |
| Any one mask bit set | Each Task per-joint `overrideLimits` status becomes true. | HMI/HALUI per-joint boolean is not a per-joint active-override witness. |
| `HOME_IGNORE_LIMITS=YES` for shared home/limit | Homing's own limit check may ignore that joint's limit input during homing. | Not equivalent to jog override; wrong-side start/geometry can crash; no personnel safety claim. |
| Override selected but no jog yet | No verified general elapsed-time expiration in inspected code. | Do not assume an automatic timeout or fail-safe clearing. |

## Practical architecture and human-factors design (INFERENCE)
1. Keep **hard end-of-travel machine protection**, independent personnel-protective safety functions, and mechanical stops/clearance as separate design layers. Ordinary LinuxCNC hard-limit and soft-limit logic is **not** a certified protective stop.
2. Prefer separate, labeled directional limit sensors and observable status where feasible; if a shared signal is retained for I/O economy, do not offer a one-click, ambiguous-direction recovery as if the software knows the correct direction.
3. Make the normal recovery **stop -> inspect actual position/obstruction and switch wiring -> restore known physical direction/clearance under a qualified safe procedure -> restore limit monitoring -> independently verify fresh normal control**. For gravity-loaded Y1/Y2, a conventional jog is not a substitute for hydraulic load holding or a safety-rated setup mode.
4. Distinguish UI `limit tripped`, `override requested`, `override mask scope`, `motion disabled`, `independent protective stop`, and `physical safe state witnessed`. Avoid green per-joint lamps based solely on `halui.joint.N.override-limits`.
5. A guard/limit that requires frequent defeat is a design/maintainability defect. Correct travel geometry, switch mounting, diagnostics and operator workflow instead of normalizing bypass.

## Same-session HAL wiring proof and configuration-name trap
**DOC-CONFIRMED at pinned `v2.9.10`:** [`docs/src/config/stepper.adoc` blob `588cca46281e8ad8da5aa2e6c3786214755ee112`, lines 122–149](https://github.com/LinuxCNC/linuxcnc/blob/v2.9.10/docs/src/config/stepper.adoc#L122-L149) explicitly illustrates (a) a shared *home* signal feeding multiple `joint.N.home-sw-in` pins with `HOME_IS_SHARED`/`HOME_SEQUENCE` and (b) separate +/− limit pins versus a single `Xlimits` signal wired to **both** `joint.0.neg-lim-sw-in` and `joint.0.pos-lim-sw-in`. The manual itself warns shared limit wiring cannot identify which switch/axis tripped and can lead to a hard stop during recovery. This directly corroborates the source's both-bits-asserted direction ambiguity without assuming any particular physical machine uses that example.

**Three distinct concepts must not be collapsed:** `HOME_IS_SHARED` is a homing start/sequence configuration for shared home switches; `HOME_IGNORE_LIMITS` is a homing-state exception; `EMCMOT_OVERRIDE_LIMITS` is an ordinary jog-recovery command/mask. They are neither interchangeable nor safety-rated. The same pinned stepper manual [lines 90–98](https://github.com/LinuxCNC/linuxcnc/blob/v2.9.10/docs/src/config/stepper.adoc#L90-L98) contains a sample `iocontrol.0.user-enable-out` to `iocontrol.0.emc-enable-in` **software E-stop loopback**. This is a configuration tutorial example, **not** proof of a separate protective E-stop circuit or a suitable real-machine design.

**INFERENCE for a low-cost practical design:** spending an additional input on independently observable positive/negative limit channels can improve fault localization and recovery direction clarity; it does **not** create redundant personnel safety, safety integrity, or an independent safe-state witness. If inputs must be shared, make ambiguous recovery a qualified inspection/repair workflow, not a one-button operator bypass.

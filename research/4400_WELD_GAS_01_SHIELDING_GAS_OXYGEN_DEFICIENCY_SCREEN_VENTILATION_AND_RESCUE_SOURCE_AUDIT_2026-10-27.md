## Causal chain and independence
1. **Normal process:** LinuxCNC/robot controller commands a weld episode, arc enable, shielding gas pre/post-flow, movement and perhaps extraction. These are *ordinary control*. The actual wiring, valve fail position and timing are **UNKNOWN** for any selected cell. No safety credit follows from M3/M5, an M-code, a motion pause or a generic "weld done" bit.
2. **Gas energy/material:** A cylinder/manifold and lines can contain pressure even when the robot/arc stops. A commanded gas-solenoid OFF may fail through stuck valve, leak, alternate purge branch or manual bypass; this is **INFERENCE** requiring a selected gas-path diagram and physical evidence. It is not a proved failure on a specific installation.
3. **Air/exposure:** Shield/purge gas can accumulate inside the workpiece, low pits, adjacent service areas or behind screens. A motor/arc E-stop removes neither existing gas nor proves oxygen/contaminant conditions. Gas has no reliable human warning sensation; a worker can be incapacitated before self-rescue (HSE/NIOSH).
4. **Protection composition:** Arc-radiation screens, robot-motion guards, fume capture, shielding-gas isolation, fire protection, and atmospheric/entry controls are **different physical functions**. Closing an arc screen must not silently destroy necessary ventilation; keeping a fan running for gas clearance must be reconciled with hot-work fire/collector/duct hazards, not decided by a universal E-stop fan rule.
5. **Human factors:** A worker investigating a suspected leak, reaching through a workpiece for a purge dam, or opening a robot cell to retrieve a part may enter an unrecognized atmosphere. Make remote purge removal, outside-of-space shutoff, accessible isolation and simple safe inspection easier than leaning into the hazard. An untrained spontaneous rescue can add victims.

**Failure-path discriminator:** Consider *arc OFF + robot stopped + gas still leaking + extractor stopped + person leans into workpiece*. The first two normal indications can be TRUE while the oxygen-deficiency hazard remains. This is a **hypothetical composition**, not a selected-machine test. Review GAS-1 through GAS-5 separately; reject any design that collapses them into one green `READY` lamp.

## Practical architecture (INFERENCE; not a prescribed wiring diagram)
- Reduce the hazard first: minimize unnecessary purge volume and dwell, route/contain gas lines away from accessible pits, avoid human entry and make remote inspection possible.
- Provide a clear gas isolation/maintenance method independent of the CNC program; do not rely on software command alone for a person entering a potentially oxygen-deficient space.
- Treat fume extraction and gas/air clearance as separate physical services with independently defined loss-of-power, fire, maintenance and restart behavior; do not wire the safety authority into normal LinuxCNC/FPGA for convenience.
- Show separate operator indications: **ARC/MOTION STOPPED**, **GAS ISOLATED/VERIFIED**, **ATMOSPHERE/ENTRY AUTHORIZED**, **FIRE/HOT-WORK CONTROL**. These are not interchangeable; ordinary HMI indications alone are not safety-rated.
- If basic gas/atmosphere/entry safeguards cannot be demonstrated, **no people may be exposed to the hazard**. Experimental work, if later justified, needs qualified remote isolation with people outside the danger zone and stated residual risks.

## Same-session source-depth: remote atmosphere sampling, head-only entry, and residual argon after process completion
**DOC-CONFIRMED direct HSE, [same original page](https://www.hse.gov.uk/welding/asphyxiation-hazards.htm), "Safe systems of work" and "Emergency arrangements", updated 2025-01-10:** (a) even head-and-shoulders intrusion can be dangerous; (b) after inert-gas use, vent/purge and test **without entering**; (c) measurements should cover the entire volume, particularly deepest locations; (d) oxygen should never be used to enrich ventilation; (e) plan rescue before entry, with appropriate trained attendance/equipment rather than assuming a distant fire service will supply the immediate rescue. These are UK guidance, not a US permit substitute or a field procedure authored by this curriculum.

**DOC-CONFIRMED direct second independent NIOSH incident:** [FACE 91-14, 1991-08-09](https://stacks.cdc.gov/view/cdc/164315) records a furnace operator fatally overcome by **residual argon at the bottom of a vessel while retrieving dropped objects after unloading**. NIOSH recommended redesigning the vessel/using an extraction tool to avoid entry. This is particularly valuable independent evidence for the *post-process residual atmosphere and human retrieval* causal mechanism; it is **not** a welding robot or OpenPressBrake incident. The two FACE cases support distinct leak-investigation and post-process retrieval failure paths, not two test confirmations of a particular safety function.

**Physical witness refinement:** GAS-3 requires a qualified spatial/temporal atmosphere assessment **without exposing the sampler**, including low regions, representative tasks and a plan for changing conditions; GAS-5 should first eliminate head/torso entry by remote retrieval and define non-entry rescue and trained emergency arrangements. A single pre-entry reading, an oxygen-rich "purge" or a remote-service promise is not adequate by analogy. No live measurement or site permit is specified here.

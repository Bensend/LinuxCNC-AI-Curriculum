# 4400-AIR-CLEAN-01 — original OSHA field evidence: body cleaning, taped hoses, drying and defeated safeguards
2026-10-27 | Documentary case audit only; no new physical test or adjudicated machine finding.

## Three direct OSHA sources
1. **OSHA 1994-01-14 interpretation** [Using compressed air for cleaning an employee's body and clothing](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/standardinterpretations/1994-01-14). OSHA states employers **should not allow** employees to clean their bodies/clothing with compressed air in general industry. The letter distinguishes the explicit prohibition in maritime 1917.154 from the general-industry standard; do **not** falsely claim that the exact maritime wording is in 1910.242(b). The letter repeats <30 psig plus effective chip guarding and PPE for cleaning. **DOC-CONFIRMED**, scoped interpretation.
2. **OSHA 2011 PJ Trailers citation**, [original citation PDF, page 18 of 54](https://www.osha.gov/sites/default/files/citations/PJTrailers314181876.pdf), inspection 2011-03-28 to 2011-09-27, issuance 2011-09-27: OSHA cited two air lines **taped together** for trailer drying/cleaning, each >100 psig without a dead-end-limiting nozzle, and compressed air used on skin/clothing in the paint booth. **DOC-CONFIRMED citation allegations**, not proof of injury, final adjudication or OpenPressBrake conditions. This demonstrates a real *human-factors defeat path* rather than an invented scenario.
3. **OSHA 2015 American Plating citation record**, [inspection detail](https://www.osha.gov/ords/imis/establishment.violation_detail?citation_id=01001&id=1019956.015), issued 2015-03-27, final order shown 2015-04-10: alleged compressed-air drying of electroplated objects without pressure reduction below 30 psi. **DOC-CONFIRMED OSHA record** in its stated scope; no independent workplace measurement here.

## Adversarial transfer
A gun marketed 'safe' may still be modified (relief ports taped, nozzle replaced, two hoses coupled) because the standard tool is too weak for the task. A body/clothing-cleaning shortcut can become routine when no convenient vacuum/brush/wash station is provided. Redesign the task: provide a useful vacuum/contained drying solution, an appropriate intact dead-end-safe nozzle and shielding when blowing remains necessary, clear separation from body cleaning, easy replacement and inspection, and a conveniently located service air isolator. Avoid blaming workers while leaving the unsafe shortcut as the fastest option.

## Scope and claims
The 1910.242(b) **cleaning** rule and its static dead-end interpretation do not define all pneumatic process limits; a separate OSHA 1910.244(b) abrasive-blast nozzle provision has a manually held-open valve requirement and must not be silently transferred to an ordinary air gun. Neither a low nozzle pressure nor chip shield proves acceptable aerosol, noise, safe motion, or stored-energy isolation. **TEST-CONFIRMED none; INFERENCE** design/defeat review; **UNKNOWN** selected machine and work process. No physical testing authorized.

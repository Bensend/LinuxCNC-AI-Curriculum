# 4400-AMR-FLEET-01 — Fleet coordination is not an interlocked collision-prevention function
**2026-10-30 | Original manufacturer + standards-scope documentary audit | NON-GRADUATED**

## Question and novelty
Can an automated cell treat a green Wi-Fi/Fleet Manager/map/route signal as proof that two autonomous mobile robots, a forklift, or a stationary CNC machine cannot collide? **No.** This is a new mobile-machine integration hazard not present in the directly fetched 391,446-character canonical safety index (AGV/AMR/driverless/mobile robot/fleet manager/docking search zero). It transfers the existing 2520 physical-proposition, 2590 presence-sensing and 4400 scanner-case methods; it does **not** reopen 3000 or claim a LinuxCNC-controlled AMR exists.

## Original-source evidence (scope and edition matter)
1. **DOC-CONFIRMED — Omron Mobile Robot LD Safety Guide I616-E-06, 18393-000 Rev H (2019), printed pp. 16–17**: Multi-AMR Avoidance explicitly says Fleet Manager shares X/Y/heading/size/path information over Wi-Fi but **is not an interlocked collision-prevention method**; the end user/integrator must provide an interlocked method. It describes facing robots' lasers seeing into the other's scanner slot and mis-estimating physical dimensions. It says co-located robots must share a Fleet Manager. Source: https://files.omron.eu/downloads/latest/manual/en/i616_mobile_robot_ld_safety_guide_system_manual_en.pdf?v=1 (PDF pp. 16–17, rendered original p.17).
2. **DOC-CONFIRMED — Omron LD-250 Platform User's Manual I642-E-09 (2025)**, official manufacturer indexed text at https://www.omron.com.vn/data_pdf/mnu/i642-e-09_ld-250.pdf , printed p.1-22: Fleet Manager shares a common map, dynamic pose and route; its own precaution explicitly says Fleet Manager/map features do **not** replace physical collision prevention such as interlocked gateways or barriers. **INDEXED-ONLY** for this 27-MB manual: full PDF fetch exceeded web viewer limit; do not call the whole document visually inspected.
3. **DOC-CONFIRMED — Omron I616 Rev H printed p.13**: a dropoff/loading-dock/stair hazard needs continuous physical barriers detectable by the scanner; logical forbidden areas are additional, not the sole protection. Manufacturer's illustrated 190-mm sensor height / 250-mm suggested barrier applies to its specific LD installation and is **not** a generic AMR dimension. Printed p.20 requires physically blocking unsafe travel zones in addition to map zones.
4. **DOC-CONFIRMED — ISO catalogue, ISO 3691-4:2023, edition 2, published June 2023**, https://www.iso.org/standard/83545.html : scope includes driverless industrial trucks, AGVs and AMRs and covers their system/operating zone. It expressly does not cover some special load and trailer hazards; a 2026 DIS is under development, **not** a published replacement. Only public abstract/status inspected, not normative clauses.
5. **DOC-CONFIRMED — I616 Rev H printed p.16**: traffic control proposes a switchable forbidden map area from a discrete input, but this does not grant that map function a safety integrity rating. A non-safety traffic bit must not be treated as a certified gate interlock.

## Causal failure-path and boundary table
| Initiator | Normal-control appearance | Unproven physical proposition | Safer engineering disposition |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wi-Fi drop, stale fleet pose or map/localization error | Route conflict-free, last fleet state green | Another vehicle/person cannot enter crossing | Independent selected safety-rated vehicle/cell interface and physical barrier where required; fail closed on lost valid permission |
| Facing AMRs see scanner slots | Both maps show separated footprints | True outer contours separated | Qualified installation geometry and collision prevention, not inferred from fleet telemetry |
| Forklift and AMR share transfer doorway | Ordinary PLC route reservation high | Crossing is empty and stationary machine safe | Safety-related interlock/guarded transfer boundary where risk assessment requires, with distinct physical witnesses |
| Dropoff marked only in navigation map | Forbidden polygon appears correct | Wheel cannot reach edge under localization failure | Continuous physical boundary plus route planning; never substitute map for barrier |
| Lost communications after job dispatched | Fleet dashboard stale/green | AMR and stationary process cannot restart unsafely | Explicit stale/fault handling, independent safety authority, deliberate restart and qualified verification |

**INFERENCE:** A two-party permission handshake for production logistics is useful, but ordinary LinuxCNC/HAL, a PLC traffic tag, fleet API, Wi-Fi status or scanner visualization must not be promoted to personnel-safety authority. Select safety-rated logic/endpoints/final elements for each physical hazard and verify behavior under communication loss, both-party reset, crossing occupancy and power return. **UNKNOWN:** actual AMR, scanner, fleet version, installed cell, stopping, safety integrity, physical barriers and site traffic. **TEST-CONFIRMED:** none.

## Practical minimum and human factors
Choose the layout that does not require workers to defeat a narrow gate to load parts: clear transfer lane, easy-to-use gate, visible stop/restart station outside crush zone, physical edge barrier, and recovery access. If a guard is repeatedly bypassed to clear jams, redesign the transfer path and recovery procedure. A fleet route that looks clear is not a person-clear witness. If the basic crossing/stop/hold/egress protection is unverified, **no personnel-exposed operation**; any justified experiment must be independently isolated/remote with people outside the danger zone.

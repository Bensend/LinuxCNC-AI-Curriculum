# 4400-ROUTER-SCOPE-01 — A CNC router's safety-standard scope can exclude its projectile path and robot interface
**2026-10-30 | original regulator + standards-publisher source audit | NON-GRADUATED | no machine test**

## Exact question and predecessor check
Can a builder claim that a CNC router's light curtain, ISO 13855 separation-distance calculation, or generic ISO 19085-3 conformity proves that ejected cutters/workpieces and integrated robot loading are controlled? **No.** This is a *machine-class scope and system-boundary integration* gap, not a newly discovered generic ejection or access hazard. Direct canonical learner-index, research-plan and historical progress scans found **no ISO 19085-3 / 700-mm router-opening scope entry**. Existing predecessors already cover [DUST-01](../safety-course/4400_DUST_01_WOOD_ROUTER_LEV_CAPTURE_FIRE_AND_CNC_COMMAND_BOUNDARY_MODULE_2026-10-25.md) (LEV/fire), [GUARD-REACH-01](4400_GUARD_REACH_01_ISO13857_2019_ISO14120_2015_VS_ISO13855_2024_SCOPE_AND_ACCESS_PATH_AUDIT_2026-10-28.md) (reach vs stop vs ejection), [GUARD-IMPACT-01](4400_GUARD_IMPACT_01_TROAX_2014_2026_FLOOR_ANCHOR_DEFLECTION_AND_TEST_PROVENANCE_AUDIT_2026-10-30.md) (tested structural deflection), [CHUCK-01](../safety-course/4400_CHUCK_01_ROTATING_WORKHOLDING_DYNAMIC_GRIP_GUARD_AND_RELEASE_MODULE_2026-10-25.md) (retention) and [AMR-TRANSFER-01](4400_AMR_TRANSFER_01_OMRON_I680_CONVEYOR_LIFT_HANDSHAKE_VS_INDEPENDENT_STOP_SOURCE_AUDIT_2026-10-30.md) (external interface). Increment here: **exact 2021 CNC-router product-standard exclusions, 2014/2022 regulator guidance with different edition references, and their effect on the real integration boundary**. ## Causal failure paths and authority allocation
**Path A — 'light curtain green' but a cutter fragment leaves the machining zone.** An ESPE may stop motion upon a human approach, but does not act as a physical projectile barrier. An opening/strip curtain, actual cutting-tool family and worst credible ejection trajectory require a **separate containment proposition**. ISO 13855 distance is not a projectile-energy rating. Normal LinuxCNC spindle-off, RPM limit or feed override is **not** independent proof of retention/containment.

**Path B — a seemingly standard-compliant router gains a robot loader.** The router product standard's **integrated loading/unloading exclusion** makes the *interface* a separate hazard analysis: robot reach and trapping, transfer gripper/vacuum loss, guarding of shared openings, stop propagation, who can be inside, recovery after E-stop, and no automatic restart. The actual cell architecture and validated interfaces are UNKNOWN. A robot-ready handshake or green CNC bit is not a safety function.

**Path C — an existing curtain or tool is replaced.** Different curtain overlap/material, enlarged opening, taller stock, 5-axis head angle, changed cutter/tool family, or changed vacuum clamping may invalidate containment and access assumptions even if the safety-controller checksum is unchanged. Require exact configuration identity and qualified design-specific acceptance; do not invent ejection energy, stop time or impact threshold.

**Path D — maintenance/setting convenience drives defeat.** WIS22 distinguishes full enclosures, partial guards, mats, bumpers, and deliberate setting controls; the safe architecture should make ordinary load/unload and tooling service practical without removing a barrier. An operator's need to remove a strip curtain every cycle is an engineering usability defect. Guard restoration must not itself initiate a spindle cycle.

## One-pass reviewer contract (no energized machine test)
Record separately: (1) machine model, manufacturing year, edition and cutter/tool families; (2) human reach/stop protective geometry; (3) **projectile containment** through every door/opening and changed 5-axis trajectory; (4) integrated loader/robot/transfer hazard boundary; (5) vacuum clamp and tool-retention physical assumptions; (6) independent guard/stop authority, safe access, reset/restart and isolation; (7) dated manufacturer containment evidence matched to exact configuration; (8) qualified reviewer/physical validation plan. All selected-machine entries **UNKNOWN/NOT RUN** until supplied by authorized team. Do not request ad hoc projectile firing, live cutter failure, guard defeat or person-exposed tests.

## Cross-machine transfer and limitations
Press brakes can eject a sheet/tool fragment; mills and lathes can eject workpieces; robot cells can transfer payloads. The **scope discipline** transfers, not ISO 19085-3 numerical rules, router curtain impact data or any router mode limit. OpenPressBrake G0 A–G remains the primary physical evidence gate and must not be replaced by a router worksheet. **SOURCE-CONFIRMED** repository predecessor/novelty scan; **DOC-CONFIRMED** original ISO/HSE/OSHA statements; **INFERENCE** interface/containment review; **TEST-CONFIRMED none**; **UNKNOWN** actual machine, guarding, containment, integration, stopping, PL/SIL, physical proof and independent evaluator. ## Same-session original 2017 edition cross-check — 600 mm versus 700 mm is an **exclusion-boundary change**, not permission to enlarge
Directly opened the **original ISO 19085-3:2017(E) national-standards-body SIST preview**, printed p.1, section 1 Scope: https://preview.sist.si/sist-preview/63898/9aee6d80bee14bb88b330b1a0721016d/ISO-19085-3-2017.pdf . The 2017 scope excludes specified ejection through curtain-guarded openings when the distance from workpiece support to the partial-enclosure lower edge **exceeds 600 mm**. The ISO publisher's **current 2021** scope uses **exceeds 700 mm** for the corresponding class of ejection. The official ISO 2017 history confirms withdrawal on **2021-11-08**, replaced by 2021: https://www.iso.org/standard/63898.html . This is **DOC-CONFIRMED exact edition/scope wording**; it does **not** establish that a 650-mm opening is safe in 2021 or that the normative 2021 curtain test/installation details are unchanged. 2017 references EN 847-1/-2:2013 whereas 2021 scope references EN 847-1/-2:2017. The 2017 preview also excludes large-machine internal step-on/entry hazards; omission from the 2021 *abstract* alone cannot prove that hazard was newly addressed. **Required next source if a real router is selected:** full applicable 2021 normative 5.9 curtain/impact/wear requirements and the actual machine/curtain test documentation. Never silently combine 2017 geometry, 2014/2022 HSE impact example and 2021 product claims.

**Cross-test non-equivalence:** HSE's illustrative 100-g/70-m/s curtain impact corresponds to 245 J, but the [Troax GUARD-IMPACT-01](4400_GUARD_IMPACT_01_TROAX_2014_2026_FLOOR_ANCHOR_DEFLECTION_AND_TEST_PROVENANCE_AUDIT_2026-10-30.md) 1,600–8,180-J fence tests use different specimens, impact bodies and fixings. Comparing headline joules cannot validate a router strip curtain, a fence, or their installation. **No new lab or selected-machine claim.**

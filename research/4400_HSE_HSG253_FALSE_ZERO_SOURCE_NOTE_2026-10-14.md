# 4400 HSE source note — pressure-witness false zeros

**Status:** DOC-CONFIRMED generic process-isolation guidance; press-brake application INFERENCE; selected machine UNKNOWN; no test run.

UK HSE *The safe isolation of plant and equipment* HSG253 (2006), §§170–176, documents that small-bore drain/test connections may become blocked and give misleading depressurization results; high-range pressure gauges may not respond adequately at low pressures; gauges should be proven before use; and isolations require re-proving and monitoring where applicable. Source: https://www.hse.gov.uk/pubns/priced/hsg253.pdf (PDF pp. 35–36). HSG253 principally addresses process industries and is not itself a press-brake circuit specification.

HSE's 2025-12-17 inspection statement separately identifies overlooked non-return valves/trapped fluids and inaccurate as-built drawings in offshore process isolation work: https://press.hse.gov.uk/2025/12/17/offshore-process-isolation-failures-present-major-accident-hazard-risk/ . OSHA 29 CFR 1910.147(d)(3)–(6) addresses isolation, stored energy, reaccumulation and verification: https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.147 .

**Engineering inference:** A zero display proves neither that its sensor is connected to the hazardous volume nor that the sensing path is healthy, nor that other stored energy and gravity loads are controlled. Keep pressure proof, independent machine safety authority and maintenance isolation separate. Do not operate with people exposed while the minimum safety case is unproven; any remote experimental work requires a qualified hazard assessment and people outside the danger zone.

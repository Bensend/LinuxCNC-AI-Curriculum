# 4400-VFD-RESTART-01 — Auto-reset can hide a VFD fault while a maintained RUN later restarts the spindle
**2026-10-30 | original manufacturer source | 4400 safety | NON-GRADUATED | NO PHYSICAL TEST**

## Exact question and predecessor reconciliation
An ordinary LinuxCNC/Modbus or discrete spindle RUN command is retained when a VFD experiences a recoverable fault or a brief supply interruption. Does a drive's ordinary fault relay immediately prove that a fault is present, and does reset require a new operator start? **Not in the specific documented Schneider configuration.**

This is an **exact OEM/edition evidence delta**, not a new general reset competency. ## Original OEM provenance and bounded claims
**DOC-CONFIRMED:** Schneider Electric, *Altivar 12 variable speed drives — User manual*, **BBV28581 07/2018**, manufacturer-hosted original PDF [iportal.se.com/Contents/docs/SQD-ATV12P075M2_USER%20GUIDE.PDF](https://iportal.se.com/Contents/docs/SQD-ATV12P075M2_USER%20GUIDE.PDF), directly inspected 2026-10-30. Page numbers below are **printed manual pages**; this is a live manufacturer URL, not a pinned immutable PDF hash.

1. **Printed p.31, Basic functions:** R1 is normally energized with drive power and no detected fault and de-energizes on detected fault or supply loss. This is the *ordinary default* description, **qualified by the automatic-restart exception** on p.91. Do not infer that the same immediate indication holds when Atr=YES.
2. **Printed pp.48–51, I/O:** `tCC=2C` selects 2-wire control; `tCt=LEL` uses the input's maintained **level** to command RUN/STOP. In contrast, `tCt=trn` requires a **transition** to initiate operation, described as helping prevent accidental restart after power interruption. Changing `tCC` resets `tCt` and input assignments to defaults; this is a configuration-change hazard. Neither level nor transition control is a safety-rated function.
3. **Printed p.52, R1 assignment:** `r1=FLt` (factory assignment) is **No error detected**, not a safety-rated zero-speed or torque-off output. Other assignments such as `rUn` report different process states. Verify actual `r1` assignment and physical wiring before interpreting the relay.
4. **Printed p.91, Atr menu:** `Atr=nO` is the listed factory setting; when `Atr=YES`, the drive can automatically attempt restart after eligible detected faults disappear and other conditions permit. The manufacturer gives retry intervals **1 s, 5 s, 10 s, then 1 minute**, with maximum-restart-time `tAr` options **5 min to infinite**. **Critically, the OEM warns that R1 indicates the fault only after the retry time-out** and states the drive status relay remains activated during this function. This is a documented *delayed fault indication*, not evidence that the drive is healthy or stopped during retry. This applies to this named product/configuration; do not extrapolate the schedule to other drives.
5. **Printed pp.91–92, FLr:** `FLr=YES` permits **catch on the fly** when a RUN command remains asserted after supply loss/disconnection, fault reset/auto-restart, or freewheel stop. It estimates the rotating motor's speed before ramping toward reference and requires 2-wire level control. `FLr=nO` is the listed factory setting. This is not a braking or protective-stop function.
6. **Printed p.5:** the OEM warns that critical emergency-stop, power-loss and restart control paths require independent consideration and separate/redundant paths. **Printed p.5 also warns that a motor can generate voltage when its shaft rotates; p.5 specifies isolation, DC bus discharge wait and qualified voltage verification for service.** These are model-specific service requirements, not generic electrical test instructions.

**Cross-model corroboration only (not a substitute for the exact installed manual):** Schneider's [ATV32/ATV320 automatic-restart FAQ FA173390](https://www.se.com/uk/en/faqs/FA173390/) (published 2013-10-10; last modified 2025-07-17) independently describes `Atr=YES`, `tCC=2C`, `tCt=LEL`, held RUN, and the same staged retry schedule. Its search-indexed page was available but the direct page fetch returned 404, so classify this cross-model item **manufacturer-indexed DOC**, not a second directly inspected manual. The ATV12 primary evidence stands without it.

## Failure path: the fault relay can appear permissive during retries
| Event | Ordinary drive behavior supported by ATV12 manual | Unsafe interpretation to reject | Separate safety requirement / evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| A recoverable drive fault occurs, `Atr=YES`, `tAr` not expired | R1 may remain in its normal energized/healthy indication while the drive attempts reset | "R1 is energized, so no fault or future motion" | Independent safety authorization; fault/retry diagnostic distinguished from safe physical state |
| Fault disappears while `tCC=2C`, `tCt=LEL`, RUN and reference remain asserted | Automatic restart can resume motor operation | "Reset only clears an alarm; a new START edge is guaranteed" | Remove ordinary retained RUN on stop/recovery; separate deliberate new start, independent protective path |
| Supply is interrupted while shaft coasts, `FLr=YES` | Drive can estimate existing rotation and resume toward reference on recovery | "Motor was de-energized, so it was stationary or safe to access" | Independently established standstill/access and suitable isolation/guarding |
| `tAr` expires without successful restart | Drive remains locked until power cycled per OEM; R1 fault indication can then appear | "The prior R1 high meant a successful stop" | Distinguish trip lock, drive feedback, physical stop and safe rearm |
| A controller crashes or Modbus link fails | LinuxCNC shutdown and client timeout are **not** the drive's independent physical safety | "A missing command or red HMI guarantees torque off" | Independent selected safety circuit and final element; drive-side comm-loss response separately reviewed |

**INFERENCE:** If an HMI maps `R1=energized` to "SPINDLE SAFE" or a safety PLC relies on that ordinary relay alone, it can make a dangerous false-permissive inference during the manufacturer's auto-retry window. This is a design failure mode to review, **not** an assertion that any actual OpenPressBrake or LinuxCNC machine has such wiring. A drive status contact is not a certified protective stop, a proven mechanical brake, standstill, electrical isolation, or load holding.

## Practical architecture and human factors
- Keep VFD RUN/speed, `Atr` and `FLr` configuration, relay diagnostics and Modbus status in the **ordinary control/diagnostic** domain. Record exact product, firmware, parameter export, terminal polarity, fieldbus mapping and control source. Label **DRIVE FAULT/RETRY STATUS UNKNOWN** when the selected diagnostic cannot establish fault freshness; never label R1 alone **SAFE TO ENTER**.
- Define the independent safety-related protective function from the hazard assessment (e.g., suitable guard interlock and selected safety drive input/STO or other validated final element). Do **not** assume this ATV12 has a safety-rated STO input; the cited manual establishes no such selected capability. Protect coasting tools and any gravity/kinetic loads separately. Electrical maintenance still requires task-specific isolation and verification.
- After guard restoration, power recovery, fault clearance or reset, require deliberate **fresh normal START** according to the selected machine SRS, and ensure ordinary retained RUN cannot bypass it. A monitored reset edge and an ordinary run edge are different propositions. Do not let a single "Reset All" button clear the fault and resume the spindle.
- Avoid nuisance resets that lead maintainers to bridge interlocks. Provide a clear fault/retry/communication status and quick safe access for clearing chips, tool changes and maintenance; guard reinstallation should be easier than bypass.
- The independent safety chain must retain authority even if the LinuxCNC PC, normal FPGA, fieldbus or VFD status relay fails. For a press brake, this VFD example **does not** establish any hydraulic Y1/Y2 stop or gravity hold.

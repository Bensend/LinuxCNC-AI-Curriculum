## Important correction found during adversarial source tracing

Pinned `emctaskmain.cc` shows that an externally reported `io.aux.estop` condition uses the main-loop subordinate-state synchronization path: when trajectory is enabled it calls `emcTrajDisable()`, aborts Task/I/O work, stops spindles, cleans up and resynchronizes.

It does **not** call `emcAuxEstopOn()` on that external-input path. Therefore `iocontrol.0.user-enable-out` must not be required to mirror `iocontrol.0.emc-enable-in=FALSE`. `user-enable-out` belongs to the explicit controller-originated `emcAuxEstopOn/Off()` command path.

Artifacts and the lab oracle were corrected before execution.

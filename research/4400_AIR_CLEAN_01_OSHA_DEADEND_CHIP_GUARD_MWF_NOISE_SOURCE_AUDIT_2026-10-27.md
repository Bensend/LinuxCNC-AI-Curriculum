## Direct primary sources and bounded claims
1. **DOC-CONFIRMED — OSHA 29 CFR 1910.242(b)**, *Hand and portable powered tools and equipment, general*: compressed air used for cleaning must be reduced to **less than 30 psi**, and effective chip guarding **and** PPE are required. Exact source: https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.242 (read 2026-10-27). Do not equate this specific cleaning provision with a universal limit on all pneumatic actuators.
2. **DOC-CONFIRMED — OSHA Directive STD 01-13-001**, information date 1978-10-30, *Reduction of Air Pressure below 30 psi for Cleaning Purposes*: pressure is evaluated at the nozzle/opening under **all static conditions**, including a **dead-ended/obstructed outlet**; flow and upstream pressure alone are not the criterion. The directive describes chip guarding as preventing particles from reaching eyes or unbroken skin of operator **or other workers**; a nozzle cone may need barriers for bystanders. https://www.osha.gov/enforcement/directives/std-01-13-001 (read 2026-10-27). The directive says less than 30 psi; avoid treating an exactly-30-psi reading as a documented margin.
3. **DOC-CONFIRMED — OSHA 1978-04-14 interpretation letter**: a higher upstream pressure may be compatible with the cleaning rule **if** a relief arrangement reduces pressure below 30 psi upon dead-ending; long extension pipes are not inherently excluded if the requirements are met. OSHA does **not** approve or endorse a manufacturer's product. https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/standardinterpretations/1978-04-14-0 (read 2026-10-27). A nameplate or catalog 'OSHA approved' claim is not evidence of OSHA certification.
4. **DOC-CONFIRMED — OSHA metalworking-fluids best-practices manual**: compressed-air blow-off to dry parts should be avoided unless ventilation captures the resulting airborne mist. This is a **separate aerosol-exposure problem** from nozzle dead-end pressure and flying chips. https://www.osha.gov/metalworking-fluids/manual (read 2026-10-27). Actual MWF presence on OpenPressBrake remains UNKNOWN.
5. **DOC-CONFIRMED — OSHA 1910.95(a),(c)**: general-industry 8-hour 90 dBA permissible exposure level versus 85 dBA hearing-conservation **action level**. A vendor's statement that 85 dBA is the OSHA 'maximum allowable' level is imprecise. A product sound reading at a specified distance is not a worker's measured shift exposure. https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.95 (read 2026-10-27).
6. **DOC-CONFIRMED — OSHA 1910.147 and interpretive guidance**: servicing/cleaning with exposure to unexpected energization or stored energy generally invokes hazardous-energy control; the standard has scope/exceptions, including certain routine minor servicing with effective alternative protection. Do not claim that every guarded production blow-off requires lockout, or that an E-stop is an isolating device. https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.147 and https://www.osha.gov/enforcement/directives/std-01-05-019 (read 2026-10-27).
7. **MANUFACTURER-DOCUMENTED, not TEST-CONFIRMED — EXAIR case study 1603**, https://www.exair.com/media/productcms/casestudies/1603_Soft_Grip_Safety_Air_Guns_replace_loud_unsafe_air_guns_1.pdf (2025 manufacturer case, read 2026-10-27): describes a replacement gun with engineered nozzle and chip shield, claimed lower consumption and sound levels. These are **supplier case figures**, not an independent field test, general performance guarantee, or an OSHA approval. Its broad noise-compliance language must be reconciled with 1910.95 actual TWA exposure and 85/90 distinction.

## Practical design priority
Eliminate routine open-air blowing where a vacuum, chip brush used with machine safely stopped, captive chip collector, or contained parts washer achieves the task. If blowing is necessary, make an **engineered, hard-to-modify dead-end-safe nozzle**, chip shielding/bystander containment, convenient tool storage, accessible isolation and legible replacement instructions the default. Do not trade away guard access, physical stop, breathing-zone capture or service isolation to simplify a normal LinuxCNC M-code.

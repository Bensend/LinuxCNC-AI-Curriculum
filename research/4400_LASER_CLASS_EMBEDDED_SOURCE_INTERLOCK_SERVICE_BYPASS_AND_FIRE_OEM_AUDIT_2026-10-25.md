# 4400-LASER-01 — Laser processing enclosure, service bypass and non-optical hazards: primary-source audit
**2026-10-25 | Original manufacturer and official public documentation | documentary study only | no machine inspection**

## Question and non-equivalence to existing modules
A builder sees a closed CNC laser with a normal LinuxCNC motion-synchronized output, a green lid-switch LED, water-cooling OK and exhaust commanded ON. Which facts establish that the optical beam is physically contained, and which do not? What changes when a service technician bypasses an interlock for troubleshooting? This differs from 4400-DUST-01 (wood dust capture) and 4400-GUARD-01 (press-brake physical access): the hazard is **invisible/direct/reflected laser radiation** as well as fire, fumes and high voltage, with a documented OEM service-bypass trap.

## Primary evidence inspected
1. **Boss Laser, LS Series Operators Manual**, document 855-901 Rev 006, **2024-04-26**, 48-page original PDF: https://bosslaser.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/ls-series-user-manual.pdf . Printed p.1 calls the product a **Class IV CO2 Laser Machine**; printed p.17 describes two hood interlocks intended to prevent laser emission with hood open; printed p.10 warns against unattended firing and lists hazardous materials including chlorine-bearing products; printed pp.15–16 address high voltage, optical path and fire. Printed pp.39–40 include an alignment warning that a misdirected beam can escape into the room. **DOC-CONFIRMED for this manual/edition, not any selected machine.** The manual describes an intended interlock function, not a witnessed or independently rated stop.
2. **Boss Laser Support Hub**, *Laser Not Firing: Interlock Testing*, work instruction **WI-855-407 Rev 002**, live official service page inspected 2026-10-25: https://team.bosslaser.com/support/interlock-testing/ . Its troubleshooting route explicitly includes temporary **interlock bypasses** and laser-emission checks; it warns about high voltage and says bypasses must be removed before normal operation. **DOC-CONFIRMED** that this OEM service literature introduces a real restoration/defeat hazard. **Do not copy its bypass wiring, connectors, pinouts, alignment or live-firing procedure into this curriculum.** OEM publication is not a machine-wide authorization for unqualified people or a generic safe procedure.
3. **Trotec Laser official laser safety FAQ**, inspected 2026-10-25: https://www.troteclaser.com/en-us/resources/faqs/how-safe-is-a-laser-engraver . Trotec describes some enclosed Speedy configurations as **laser class 2** because of their positioning pointer, whereas certain hatch-equipped variants are **class 4**; opening an interlocked protective cover interrupts the laser process. **DOC-CONFIRMED** only for described configurations. Laser **source class**, accessible **product class**, and machine **operating/service mode** must not be conflated.
4. **OSHA Technical Manual**, Section III, Ch. 6, *Laser Hazards*, https://www.osha.gov/otm/section-3-health-hazards/chapter-6 and OSHA historical directive https://www.osha.gov/enforcement/directives/std-01-05-001 . Official indexed text describes housing interlocks, beam shutters/remote interlock, Class IV controlled-area considerations, service restrictions and eyewear/optical paths. **DOC-CONFIRMED only as OSHA guidance**, not a selected-machine compliance certificate. Full OSHA pages returned HTTP 403 to direct fetch; indexed text was reviewed, so preserve access limitation.
5. **ISO publisher scope**, ISO 11553-1:2020, https://www.iso.org/standard/67658.html . The publisher identifies laser processing machines and laser-radiation hazards as its scope, confirmed current in 2025. **Publisher metadata/scope only; paid normative clauses not inspected.** Do not claim the standard settles ventilation, fire, specific MPE/NOHD or a selected product's PL/SIL.
6. **LinuxCNC official M-code reference**, https://linuxcnc.org/docs/html/gcode/m-code.html . M62/M63 queue motion-synchronized ordinary digital-output changes until the next motion; M64/M65 change output immediately at receipt, and all require HAL connections. **DOC-CONFIRMED for published manual, not a source trace or physical beam test.** These are normal process outputs, not independently validated laser safety functions.

## Failure-path reasoning, not an energized test plan
- **L1 service bypass retained:** technician restores normal panels but misses a temporary diagnostic override; normal UI sees 'lid closed'. A single status LED cannot demonstrate restored protective coverage.
- **L2 lid closes while a stale process output remains high:** a motion-synchronized or immediate LinuxCNC output can represent process intent; independent safety-rated emission inhibit and fresh-start handling must be established by actual design.
- **L3 optical breach without interlock trip:** damaged viewing window, reflector or misaligned beam may expose personnel despite healthy switch contacts. Optical containment is a physical property.
- **L4 coolant/exhaust conflation:** coolant-flow permissive may protect a laser tube; an extraction command does not measure capture and neither proves personnel optical protection. Fire can continue after commanded emission ends.
- **L5 service alignment and invisible beam:** an OEM warns of room escape from misalignment. Unenclosed/defeated service belongs only under separately qualified controlled-area procedures, not a maker tutorial.
- **L6 class confusion:** product variant with an optional hatch/open path can change accessible class; a copied marketing 'Class 1/2' claim does not follow a custom retrofit.

## Human-factors design finding
The OEM's published bypass-based troubleshooting is a **real-world source-confirmed incentive** to leave an easily accessible override behind. A safer product would provide non-emitting diagnostics (separate lid channels, coolant status, shutter feedback, exhaust/capture health), service authorization, conspicuous temporary-mode indication, captive restoration checks, and a signed return-to-service proof. This is a design proposal (**INFERENCE**), not a claim that the OEM lacks such controls or that any proposed feature is safety-certified.

## Decision
**No laser machine operated, no beam emission, no simulation, no GitHub-hosted or local Actions compute, no physical or external test.** This documentary study does not authorize disabling interlocks, opening a laser beam path, aligning optics, or human-exposed operation. Need exact machine/product variant and qualified laser safety review before any physical validation. Cross-machine transfer: independent energy/hazard removal, restoration after service, and optical containment are distinct from normal LinuxCNC outputs. See learner module and no-energy review.

# 4400-DB-RESISTOR-01 — A stopped drive can still heat a braking resistor: chopper fault, independent thermal trip and exact-drive exceptions
**2026-10-29 | original manufacturer source audit | documentary research, NOT a selected-machine test or graduation**

## Engineering question and novelty
Can an ordinary LinuxCNC stop request, VFD fault, drive enable removal, or STO assertion be treated as proof that a dynamic braking resistor can no longer dissipate power? **No.** An ABB original application note identifies a shorted/conductive braking IGBT as a fault path in which the drive's ordinary stop/enable/STO does not open the resistor's electrical supply path. This is a **different proposition** from the already-covered STO-versus-DC-link isolation and common-bus regeneration questions: it concerns **thermal/fire containment of an electrically powered shunt**, even when commanded motion is stopped. A separate ABB ACS800 manual contains an **exact-frame/architecture exception** to a universal external contactor rule.

Predecessor comparison: [common DC bus/PM backfeed](4400_COMMON_DC_BUS_REGENERATION_REENERGIZATION_AND_ISOLATION_OEM_AUDIT_2026-10-24.md), [dark HMI/STO/DC link](4400_DC_LINK_02_STO_DARK_DISPLAY_VS_CAPACITOR_ISOLATION_AND_LOTO_OEM_AUDIT_2026-10-26.md), and [2570 drive-safety preparation](../safety-course/2570_DRIVES_STO_BRAKING_HAZARDOUS_MOTION_SOURCE_PREP_2026-09-23.md) already distinguish torque, standstill and isolation. They do **not** trace a shorted chopper -> continuous resistor heating -> thermal-switch -> supply interruption path or the ABB model exception. This is **new exact OEM failure mechanism and variant reconciliation**, not a new 3000 specialization or a safety rating.

2. **ABB ACS800-PC hardware manual, 3AUA0000010601 Rev C, effective 2009-10-16**, original manufacturer PDF: https://library.e.abb.com/public/32b419aa58d44cca9e83097c656f1bec/3AUA0000010601_ACS800-PC_MU.pdf . ## Failure-path decomposition
| Layer | What can be true | What it does **not** prove |
|---|---|---|
| Normal LinuxCNC/FPGA command | Spindle/axis stopped or disabled; operator HMI shows fault | Chopper power semiconductor is nonconductive; resistor cool; line supply interrupted |
| Drive control / thermal model | Software calculates duty/temperature, may trip the inverter | A **shorted chopper** has ceased conducting, or the model can open upstream line power |
| Resistor thermal switch | Temperature threshold contact changes state on an equipped, correctly installed model | Main power is removed unless the contact is wired to a suitable independent interruption path; contact itself is not a mains-power disconnect |
| External input contactor (where required by selected OEM design) | On qualified thermal trip, selected line supply can be interrupted | DC bus is instantly discharged, motor mechanically stopped, ram held, all other feeds isolated, or personnel can enter |
| Drive-specific internal power-flow interruption | A manufacturer may provide an alternative for an exact frame/chopper topology | That all VFDs or even all frames in a family provide equivalent fault containment |
| Lockable isolator + qualified electrical verification | Identified source physically isolated and exposed circuits verified under exact procedure | Gravity/hydraulic loads safely restrained or no other unverified stored energy |

**DOC-CONFIRMED** causal link in ABB 2011: brake IGBT shorts / chopper remains conductive -> resistor may keep receiving energy after ordinary drive stop -> resistor thermal switch opens -> contactor coil loses power -> main contactor opens. The ABB example includes a momentary start and auxiliary seal-in contact; this is an **illustrative manufacturer circuit**, not a drop-in design or personnel-safety-certified function. A trip should require deliberate recovery and verification; a mere thermal switch reset is not authorization to restart machinery (**INFERENCE** risk-control recommendation).

**DOC-CONFIRMED exact exception:** ABB ACS800 R7/R8 with **internal** chopper and properly selected resistor has a documented input-bridge shutdown path, unlike an **external** chopper for which that manual says contactor required. The broad 2011 ABB application note and the exact 2009 manual are **scope-dependent**, not silently interchangeable. For any candidate drive, read the **exact current hardware and firmware manual**, model, frame, resistor, chopper option and protection configuration; if evidence conflicts, hold the design decision until qualified reconciliation. Do not universally mandate or omit a contactor.

**INFERENCE** common-cause/maintenance paths: an incorrectly selected resistor, failed fan, blocked airflow, bypassed thermal contact, incorrect thermal-model parameters, missing upstream interruption, welded contactor or auto-reset can defeat intended containment. These are failure hypotheses to evaluate, not observed OpenPressBrake faults. Distinguish overtemperature **fault containment** from braking **performance**: opening supply may remove deceleration capability and **does not** safely hold a gravity-loaded axis.

## Practical architecture and acceptance review (design, not a test script)
- **Identity first:** exact drive/chopper model and frame, resistor part number, duty, mounting, thermal-contact presence/rating, manual edition and approved protective topology. A Schneider accessory without a built-in thermal contact must not be marked as “thermal protected” merely because other part numbers are.
- **Fault tree:** start with shorted chopper and resistor overtemperature. Identify the actual physical interruption path for each failure, including any selected OEM internal input-bridge protection. Trace thermal contact to coil/control power and line contacts, not merely to an HMI alarm or LinuxCNC input. Confirm fault/restart state with a qualified reviewer; do not invent contact ratings, PL/SIL or resistor trip temperature for a different device.
- **Fire/exposure boundary:** noncombustible clearances, airflow and guarding against hot surface contact follow the exact OEM installation manual; a braking resistor may remain hot after the drive stops. Do not locate a hot shunt where removed guards, cable bundles or dust deposits predictably defeat the protection.
- **Motion boundary:** independent safety system owns any personnel stop/rearm function; mechanical/hydraulic final elements must separately stop/hold gravity-loaded axes. A thermal contactor trip is not necessarily a safe-stop function. Avoid a “fix” that cuts power mid-deceleration without evaluating resulting motion.
- **Service boundary:** source interruption is not electrical isolation. Follow exact lockable isolation, residual DC-link energy, backfeed and qualified absence-of-voltage verification; do not perform energized shorted-chopper or thermal-trip fault injection as a learner lab.
- **Human factors:** label the resistor and hot zone; make an accessible thermal-trip diagnostic and a clear qualified repair/reset path; prefer guards and mounting that can be serviced/reinstalled without awkward bypasses. A “nuisance trip” is a reason to inspect duty/cooling/selection, **not** to bridge the thermal switch.

## Transfer to machines
Mills/lathes: high-inertia spindle deceleration and hot braking shunts; routers: frequent deceleration and dust/fire exposure; automated cells/robots: common bus and multiple axes; press brakes: only **if** a relevant drive/chopper is installed, with independent gravity hold still required; plasma tables: axis VFD/servo and dust/fume enclosure differences. These are **candidate hazards**, not assertions of installed components or identical risk.

# 4400-FSOE-01 — Safety over EtherCAT black-channel transport versus independent safety authority
**2026-10-29 | NON-GRADUATED safety/4000 architecture | original ETG, Beckhoff, Pilz documentation | no selected-machine evidence**

## Decision question and novelty
**Can a LinuxCNC/EtherCAT system carry safety messages without making the LinuxCNC application, FPGA, ordinary EtherCAT master or ordinary I/O a safety controller?** In principle yes, using a properly selected and validated *separate* Safety over EtherCAT (FSoE) safety application and endpoints. Merely transmitting EtherCAT process data, having a green network link, or decoding an ordinary HAL bit is **not** a safety function.

A full safety-index/research predecessor scan showed generic safety-fieldbus coverage but **no FSoE, PROFIsafe, ETG or TwinSAFE-specific implementation/diagnostic module**. This is new 4000 source depth rather than repeating generic E-stop/guard lessons or designing the OpenPressBrake normal controller board.

## Original documentary evidence
1. **DOC-CONFIRMED — EtherCAT Technology Group (ETG)** [FSoE technology description](https://www.ethercat.org/safety/) and [ETG FAQ §6](https://www.ethercat.org/en/faq.html): safety containers ride over a standard **black channel**. The ordinary communications interface need not be redundant or safety-related; ETG says an EtherCAT main-device vendor can route FSoE subdevice-to-subdevice traffic without implementing a safety function in the ordinary main device. FSoE device implementation, protocol conformance and applicable product/safety lifecycle requirements remain separate. ETG describes FSoE as suitable *up to* SIL 3, not as an automatic machine-level SIL/PL claim.
2. **DOC-CONFIRMED — ETG** [original Safety over EtherCAT introduction](https://www.ethercat.org/safetyflyer/): the monitored safety connection uses frame checksum, connection ID and watchdog checks. These detect communication faults within the FSoE safety protocol; they do not verify a hydraulic valve's physical position, gravity-load holding, guard geometry or actual stop distance.
3. **DOC-CONFIRMED — Beckhoff** [TwinCAT Safety PLC original manual](https://download.beckhoff.com/download/document/automation/twinsafe/TcSafetyPLC_en.pdf), **version 1.2.0 dated 2017-06-29**, printed pp. 46–47 (PDF pages 45–46): distinct FSoE states **Reset → Session → Connection → Parameter → Data**; diagnostics explicitly distinguish **invalid connection ID**, **invalid CRC**, **watchdog expired**, **invalid FSoE address**, **failsafe value active** and other states. The manual separately defines controller **Stop, Run, Safe, Error, Reset** states. These are *protocol/controller diagnostic states*, not proof of the physical machine's stop or rearm behavior.
4. **DOC-CONFIRMED — Beckhoff** [EL6910 TwinSAFE Logic product](https://www.beckhoff.com/en-en/products/automation/twinsafe/twinsafe-hardware/el6910.html) identifies a **dedicated safety logic terminal** with FSoE connections, project-dependent cycle time, configurable watchdog and certified component-level parameters. The exact product's safety project has logic/parameter/mapping/info checksums. A changed mapping can affect safety semantics even when the ordinary EtherCAT network stays online. **Do not copy the published product PL e/SIL 3 to a different device or an unvalidated machine.**
5. **DOC-CONFIRMED — Pilz** [PNOZ m EF EtherCAT FSoE, product 772123](https://www.pilz.com/en-INT/eshop/product/772123) is explicitly an FSoE fieldbus module, while [Pilz communication-module application description](https://www.pilz.com/en-US/products/small-controllers/configurable-control-systems/pnozmulti-2-communication-modules) explains use with a PNOZ m B1 safety base for FSoE roles. The fieldbus module is not itself proof that any specific Pilz base, safety configuration, output or press-brake valve is installed. Its product page also distinguishes IP20 housing/terminals from IP54 mounting environment; no installed-enclosure evidence exists.

**Provenance limit:** ETG FAQ search text and the manufacturer product pages were read on 2026-10-29; the ETG FAQ direct page was intermittently inaccessible through the web viewer, so rely only on the explicitly returned ETG FAQ text, not uninspected implementation details. ETG membership-only conformance specifications were **not read**; do not fabricate frame layout, diagnostic coverage, watchdog settings, SIL calculation or protocol test results. Beckhoff PDF was read as published text; no byte hash was archived. No actual LinuxCNC/EtherCAT runtime or physical system was inspected.

## Four independent engineering layers
| Layer | Responsibility | Wrong shortcut |
|---|---|---|
| Normal control | LinuxCNC planner, ordinary HAL/FPGA, EtherCAT process data, operator HMI, non-safety diagnostics | “EtherCAT link healthy means guards and E-stop are safe.” |
| Transport / black channel | Ordinary EtherCAT master, wiring, couplers and packet forwarding of FSoE containers | “The transport is safety-certified, so LinuxCNC/FPGA can decide personnel safety.” |
| Safety-related endpoints/application | Selected certified safety logic and safe I/O/drive endpoints; correct FSoE connection identity, parameterization, watchdog, fault handling and deliberate reset | “An FSoE packet or product SIL 3 badge proves the complete safety function.” |
| Physical safe state | Validated contactors/drive STO, hydraulic blocking/venting and independent ram hold, guards, mechanical restraint, energy isolation | “Safe output FALSE means Y1/Y2 has physically stopped and is held.” |

**Boundary invariant:** normal LinuxCNC/FPGA may forward data and show diagnostics, but personnel-safety decisions and safe-output authority belong to an appropriately selected and validated independent safety application and its final elements. If ordinary network traffic stalls or the normal controller crashes, the safety function must meet its **selected SRS** fault response without depending on ordinary software to recognize or recover it. Do not assume a particular stop category, watchdog value, PL/PFH or machine performance without selected design and qualified proof.

## Failure-path discrimination (paper only)
| Fault/transition | What the original sources establish | Remaining machine-level question |
|---|---|---|
| Normal EtherCAT master crashes, stops forwarding or cable breaks | ETG black channel separates ordinary transport from FSoE endpoint safety; Beckhoff documents watchdog-expired diagnostic | Actual selected safety endpoint reaction, bounded response and physical final-element stop/hold **UNKNOWN** |
| Safety device replaced with wrong address/connection identity | Beckhoff lists invalid address/connection ID and parameter states | Actual mapping/version lock, change-control, no unsafe bypass or silent restart **UNKNOWN** |
| Safety frame corrupted/replayed/delayed | ETG describes CRC, connection ID and watchdog checks; Beckhoff lists invalid CRC/watchdog diagnostics | Complete fault model, acceptance times, diagnostic coverage, actual application reaction **UNKNOWN** |
| “Safe” FSoE status received but a hydraulic valve sticks | Protocol checks cannot observe uninstrumented physical mechanics | Independent Y1/Y2 stop/hold feedback, hydraulic energy paths, secondary restraint **UNKNOWN** |
| Power restored after communication fault | Beckhoff states Reset/Session/Connection/Parameter/Data protocol states, not automatic motion authorization | Selected independent fresh-start, guard occupancy and physical state validation **UNKNOWN** |
| Ordinary LinuxCNC changes software/config | Black channel means normal transport need not own safety application | Confirm safety project/connection mappings remain unchanged and review any dependent change; **UNKNOWN** |

## Practical architecture and human factors
For an R&D machine, prefer **off-the-shelf qualified safety logic and safe I/O** if networked safety is genuinely needed. Keep the normal controller's data-plane transport simple and its safety visibility **read-only**. Avoid writing a homebrew FSoE safety endpoint into the ordinary FPGA because the cable is already present; ETG explicitly treats device implementation/conformance as a safety lifecycle obligation. A short hardwired safety circuit may be easier to inspect, repair and maintain for a small machine; networked safety is justified by actual distributed requirements, not novelty.

Make four statuses explicit at the operator station: **NETWORK CONNECTED**, **SAFETY CONNECTION VALID**, **SAFETY FUNCTION PERMITTED**, **PHYSICAL STOP/HOLD VERIFIED**. A green link is not a green ram. Provide a non-defeatable, understandable recovery path with independent reset and deliberate fresh start; don't encourage operators to jumper safe inputs to avoid nuisance communication faults. Keep guards easy to reinstall and service. On a machine without qualified physical stop/hold and guarding, **no personnel-exposed hazardous operation**.

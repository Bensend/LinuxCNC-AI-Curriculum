# 4400 PWR-UPS-01 — Buffered 24 V is not a safe-state witness
**2026-10-28 | original manufacturer documentation audit | NON-GRADUATED | NO MACHINE TEST**

## Novelty and predecessor reconciliation
This is a **power-domain/UPS source-depth addendum**, not another generic E-stop, reset or multi-feed module. Existing [RST-01](../safety-course/4400_RST_01_POWER_RETURN_RESET_EDM_AND_FRESH_START_MODULE_2026-10-24.md) covers reset, EDM and fresh cycle start; [PANEL-01](../safety-course/4400_PANEL_01_MULTIFEED_SCCR_ARC_FLASH_ISOLATION_AND_RAM_HOLD_MODULE_2026-10-25.md) covers multi-feed lockout; the [PointMax output audit](4400_ROCKWELL_POINTMAX_SAFETY_OUTPUT_SA_BACKFEED_DETECTED_NOT_SAFE_OEM_AUDIT_2026-10-25.md) covers an energized actuator despite a detected output fault. The incremental question is: **which 24 V domains remain powered, disappear or return independently when an IPC UPS, safety logic supply, safety-output supply and main machine power do not share a single power-loss history?** Existing safety-source notes did not map this exact mixed buffered/unbuffered domain sequence.

## Original manufacturer evidence and scope
1. **DOC-CONFIRMED, Pilz PNOZ m B0, original Operating Manual 1002660-EN-13, pp. 11, 26–27, 44.** The OEM requires a supply capable of **20 ms secondary buffering** for its specified transient-power-failure immunity; requires safety system and input circuits to use one SELV/PELV supply; separately identifies A1/A2 system supply and 24 V/0 V semiconductor-output supply. The latter **must be present even when semiconductor outputs are not used**. Its example permits common or separate system/output feeds; when separately fed they are galvanically isolated. The technical table provides *different* supply-current and fuse conditions for the system and output domains. These are exact PNOZ m B0 requirements, not universal wiring rules. [Original manual](https://www.pilz.com/download/open/PNOZ_m_B0_Operating_Manual_1002660-EN-13.pdf), especially printed pp. 11, 26–27, 44.
2. **DOC-CONFIRMED, Phoenix Contact TRIO-UPS-2G/1AC/24DC/5, order 2907160, official product technical data.** This is an actual 24 V / 5 A buffered industrial supply with **battery-mode output range 18–30 V DC** and a distinct Battery Mode signal; the product explicitly supports controlled industrial-PC shutdown and cold startup without the input grid. This proves that **a 24 V control rail can outlive an upstream AC failure**. It does *not* establish which OpenPressBrake rail, if any, is UPS-fed or whether a selected safety controller tolerates the entire battery range. [Manufacturer data](https://www.phoenixcontact.com/en-us/products/uninterruptible-power-supply-trio-ups-2g1ac24dc5-2907160).
3. **DOC-CONFIRMED, Rockwell Compact 5000 I/O and Specialty Modules Specifications, 5069-TD001P-EN-P (March 2026), pp. 2 and 42.** MOD (module), SA (sensor/actuator), and LA (local actuator) are separate power functions, and field-potential distribution establishes SA domains. For the exact 5069-OBV8S/OBV8SK sourcing application, Rockwell warns that an external SA+ to output short can be **detected with the output commanded OFF while the actuator stays energized**; power-source cable segregation and qualified actuator monitoring matter. The cross-short itself is already covered by the predecessor audit; here it illustrates why software status and actual load voltage must be recorded separately. [Original technical data](https://literature.rockwellautomation.com/idc/groups/literature/documents/td/5069-td001_-en-p.pdf).
4. **SOURCE-CONFIRMED curriculum boundary:** [RST-01](../safety-course/4400_RST_01_POWER_RETURN_RESET_EDM_AND_FRESH_START_MODULE_2026-10-24.md) already establishes that LinuxCNC Task/HALUI reset and machine ON are ordinary control, not independent safety authority. No selected OpenPressBrake power schematic or UPS inventory was obtained.

## Fault-path analysis — conditional engineering inferences, not observed machine behavior
| Power-domain state or service event | Plausible failure / misleading observation | Discriminator before accepting safety |
|---|---|---|
| Mains/pump power fails; UPS-backed HMI or control 24 V remains | UI still responds; operator mistakes “control alive” for “actuator powered” or “ram safely held” | Separate supply map and actual hydraulic/mechanical gravity-hold witness; no inferred ram state |
| Safety-logic 24 V remains but semiconductor-output feed drops | Program/diagnostic state may persist while output drive cannot operate as before | Exact selected controller's two-domain power requirements, diagnostic reaction and final-element physical state |
| Output feed remains but safety-logic supply drops | Stored external power or wiring fault may continue to energize loads | Prove actual output/coil/valve state and independent energy removal; do not infer from controller being OFF |
| UPS changes from mains to battery or exhausts charge | Different voltage/time history and reboot order across IPC, safety and actuator domains | Exact installed UPS voltage range, connected loads, battery condition and selected device supply tolerances |
| Mains returns while HMI retains old pedal/start request | Normal motion demand may survive even if a safety device has rebooted or rearmed | Independent fresh-start/area-clear/reset ownership and qualified stop/hold proof, not HMI green |
| Maintenance disconnects the main AC isolator but UPS/battery remains live | Hidden control or actuator-related 24 V is not necessarily isolated | Label all sources; lockable isolation of every relevant source, verified absence of energy and stored-energy controls |
| A service technician substitutes a supply or moves one 24 V fuse | Segmentation, protective separation, voltage tolerance and fault detection may change | Exact OEM wiring, fuse/current, SELV/PELV and qualified change revalidation |
| A single failed 0 V return or cross-feed changes a module's electrical reference | “OFF” command may not equal a deenergized physical final element | Qualified circuit design and independent load-side measurement; do not generalize Rockwell model behavior |

**INFERENCE:** These are credible *conditional* topologies, not claims that any listed combination exists in OpenPressBrake or that a particular UPS causes unexpected motion. No universal safe response can be assigned without as-built power distribution, hydraulic truth and physical validation. **UNKNOWN:** actual UPS, all 24 V branches, safety logic/actuator supplies, pump/valve wiring, Pilz/AKAS program, stop and gravity hold.

## Practical architecture and human factors
Keep ordinary IPC/telemetry backup separate from the personnel-safety decision and final physical energy path. A UPS can be useful to preserve diagnostics and orderly computer shutdown, but **UPS RUN, PLC RUN, DC OK and safety-output OFF are four different propositions**. A buffered controller must not become the sole source of personnel-safety authority merely because it stays online. The chosen independent safety system must handle the real power-sequence envelope, detect relevant faults, inhibit unintended restart, and command qualified final elements; the physical machine must stop and remain safe under gravity and residual pressure. Do not blindly force all power OFF if loss of energy itself can release a suspended load.

Prefer a laminated cabinet power-domain map showing each AC/DC source, UPS/battery, fuse, isolation point, controller supply, safety output supply, sensor/actuator feed and independent physical holding path. Label hidden backup supplies at the main isolator and access doors. Make proper isolation and revalidation easier than jumper wires or “reset until green.” Preserve owner-facing [Stage A A–G](../safety-course/4400_OPENPRESSBRAKE_G0_EVIDENCE_REQUEST_ONE_PAGE_2026-10-25.md) without adding a new form; qualified reviewers can request this power-domain detail conditionally under existing electrical/hydraulic packets.

## Same-session quantitative compatibility cross-check — *not* a selected-machine rating
The Pilz B0 original technical table (printed p.44) gives a **24 V nominal, −20%/+25%** operating supply tolerance for the system and SC output supply: **19.2–30.0 V** by arithmetic. The Phoenix TRIO-UPS-2G/1AC/24DC/5 (2907160) published **battery-mode output range is 18–30 V**. **INFERENCE from two DOC-CONFIRMED ranges:** a hypothetical direct B0 feed from that exact UPS has **no guaranteed full-envelope voltage compatibility** at its lowest published battery voltage: 18 V is **1.2 V below** the B0's 19.2 V lower tolerance. This does *not* assert that an installed unit ever reaches 18 V, that a PNOZ actually faults, or that either product is installed on OpenPressBrake. A selected design could have a regulator, an approved threshold/stop before undervoltage, different battery equipment, or another qualified solution. The question is a design-specific supply envelope, not a reason to manipulate a live safety supply.

A second conditional timing discriminator: Pilz requires the AC source used for its system to provide **20 ms secondary buffering** against specified transient failures; Phoenix lists **switch-over <75 ms** for this exact UPS. These values describe different interfaces and are **not automatically comparable as a measured output interruption**. A manufacturer-specific UPS transfer specification alone cannot prove the selected safety controller's input remains inside its tolerance during every event. Obtain exact circuit/waveform and compatible PSU/UPS specifications and let a qualified reviewer establish whether the chosen system meets the OEM conditions. No bench test is justified without a selected topology and a concrete unresolved acceptance question.

# 4400 HYD-FIT-01 — Hand-tight is not pressure-safe
**2026-10-28; one-page maintenance review, NON-GRADUATED.** [Original DGUV/IFA source audit](../research/4400_HYD_FIT_01_DGUV_FBHM025_METRIC_INCH_THREAD_FALSE_FIT_PRESSURE_EJECTION_SOURCE_AUDIT_2026-10-28.md) and [selected-machine intake](4400_SELECTED_MACHINE_PHYSICAL_WITNESS_INTAKE_AND_RELEASE_GATE_2026-10-23.md).

**Dangerous real-world false fit:** DGUV FB HM-025 (09/2017) documents an inch-family **G 1/2** stud that can screw into a metric **M22×1.5** port without obviously stripping either thread. That does **not** make the joint pressure-capable. It can eject suddenly with flying metal and hydraulic fluid. The cited pair is a **warning example**, never an adapter recommendation. Original PDF pp.1–2 visually checked.

**No shortcuts:** A smooth thread start, correct-looking hex, dry exterior, low static gauge reading, or a brief pump-on test cannot prove the correct thread, seal and retention. Do **not** pressure-test an unidentified joint while anyone is exposed. Treat maintenance as hazardous-energy work: isolate every relevant pressure zone, control reaccumulation and physically support gravity loads.

**Make the right choice easiest:** approved labeled fittings and matching port map; segregated stock for metric/inch families; simple service chart showing both port and stud standards and seal; manufacturer-approved markings; ready-to-use correct replacement kit. If the correct part is hard to get, repair the spares process rather than using a near-fit. Use qualified identification/inspection and controlled installation instructions.

**Before release:** document exact port/stud standard and SKU, selected pressure/temperature/flow envelope, replacement CHG, residual energy, and independent safe stop/hold/guard/restart where applicable. This is **not** an executable pressure-test procedure. Actual OpenPressBrake port families and machine physical safety **UNKNOWN**; **G0/G1 HOLD**.

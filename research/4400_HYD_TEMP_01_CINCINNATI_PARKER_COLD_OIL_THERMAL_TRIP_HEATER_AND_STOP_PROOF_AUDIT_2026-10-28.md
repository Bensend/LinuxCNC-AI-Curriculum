# 4400-HYD-TEMP-01 — Hydraulic temperature envelope, thermal shutdown and independent heater energy
**2026-10-28 | Original manufacturer source-depth | NON-GRADUATED | DOCUMENTARY ONLY**

## Bounded safety question and predecessor comparison
Can a cold-start permit, a green oil-temperature display, or an automatic high-temperature **main-drive shutdown** be treated as proof that a gravity-loaded press-brake ram will stop, remain held, and be safe for access? **No.** The narrow increment is an original press-brake OEM's distinct *startup / continuous-running* fluid-viscosity envelopes, its *alarm versus main-drive shutdown* behavior, and an *optional heater operable independently of the motor drive*, coupled with a different valve manufacturer's explicit warning that leakage and response time change at temperature extremes. No installed OpenPressBrake temperature function is asserted.

This is **not** a rediscovery of cold viscosity: [FILT-01](4400_PARKER_HYDAC_FILTER_BYPASS_COLD_START_INDICATOR_RESET_AND_VALVE_CONTAMINATION_OEM_AUDIT_2026-10-25.md) already covers cold-oil filter differential-pressure/bypass/indicator lockout; [HYD-AIR-01](4400_HYD_AIR_01_BOSCH_PARKER_CAVITATION_AERATION_AND_ERRATIC_ACTUATOR_OEM_AUDIT_2026-10-26.md) already covers cold inlet restriction, cavitation and erratic response. [FLUID-FIRE-01](4400_FLUID_FIRE_01_HSE_MISTS2_FIRE_RESISTANT_HYDRAULIC_FLUID_AND_SEAL_COMPATIBILITY_AUDIT_2026-10-26.md) covers fluid substitution and fire/seal compatibility; [HYD-SURGE-01](4400_HYD_SURGE_01_PARKER_GS4_PEAK_PRESSURE_GAUGE_BLIND_SPOT_AND_TEST_RIG_SAFETY_SOURCE_AUDIT_2026-10-28.md) covers fast pressure peaks. **Incremental physical witness:** *operating fluid temperature/viscosity and valve response/leakage condition at the safety-function proof point*, distinct from a filter alarm, a steady gauge or ordinary temperature trip. **Incremental energy boundary:** heater operating independently of pump/motor commands, without asserting that it survives the actual main disconnect.

## Distinguish five physical and authority propositions
| Proposition | OEM fact or credible failure path | Wrong inference | Qualified evidence needed |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Cold pump startup** | Cincinnati oil viscosity may permit startup at a temperature below the continuous-running range; heater recommended in cold ambient | Pump starts -> fluid in suitable *running* envelope | Exact installed fluid grade and viscosity curve, oil temperature, pump/valve OEM running limits, qualified startup sequence |
| **Hot oil warning** | Cincinnati warning precedes its main-drive trip | Alarm cleared -> valves/guard/ram safe | Selected sensor location/accuracy, alarm path, actual response and any permitted operation/restart |
| **Main-drive shutdown** | Cincinnati low level / high temp can command main-drive shutdown | Motor OFF -> hydraulic pressure exhausted or gravity ram held | Independent physical stop, load restraint, trapped pressure and accessible hazard proof |
| **Valve leakage / response** | Parker VA130 OEM says temperature extremes affect leakage and response time | Room-temperature stop-time result covers every operating condition | Actual valve/coil/fluid identification, manufacturer limits, SRS-relevant operating envelope and qualified physical stop/hold validation at justified boundary conditions |
| **Auxiliary heater energy** | Cincinnati optional heater operable independently of motor drive | Motor STOP / HMI OFF / pump fault -> heater electrically isolated | Exact heater presence, feeder/disconnect, stored heat, lockable isolation, verification at work point; no guessed circuit |

**Physical consequence reasoning (INFERENCE):** hydraulic temperature can be a relevant *validation condition* for a safety function whose physical reaction depends on valves/flow/leakage. It is not proof that a selected press-brake stopping distance necessarily increases with heat or cold, nor proof that temperature is a safety-rated sensor. A particular machine may use an independently validated valve arrangement with different temperature sensitivity; only its exact design and tests decide.

## No-energy design and verification sequence
- **Document before testing:** identify the exact machine, fluid grade and recent fluid substitutions; exact pump, safety valves, pilot/holding elements, heater, level/temperature sensor and their supply/isolation paths; OEM limits and actual duty/ambient envelope. Treat absent hardware as N/A only with dated as-built evidence.
- **Allocate authority:** ordinary LinuxCNC/FPGA may show oil temperature, refuse normal motion, and explain a thermal alarm. An independent safety controller/final elements must retain personnel-safety authority; a temperature display, pump interlock, normal valve command or FPGA watchdog is not load-holding proof.
- **Design a qualified proof plan:** SRS-specific conditions include cold startup, stabilized operation, relevant high-temperature boundary, power loss, low fluid level, heater left enabled while motor stopped, reset/restart and service. Physical proof must use appropriate safe exclusion/restraint and qualified instrumentation; **no unsupervised energized press test or deliberate overheat**.
- **Human factors:** make correct fluid identification, warm-up status, temperature display and safe heater isolation easy to access. A nuisance cold alarm that operators defeat is a design defect; use appropriate warm-up/maintenance engineering, never tape switches or enlarge limits to keep running.
- **After changes:** oil type, cooler, heater, temperature switch, valve, hydraulic plumbing or safety program may invalidate the relevant stop/hold/thermal evidence. Record revision and revalidate affected propositions rather than merely clearing an HMI warning.

## Cross-machine transfer and release
A hydraulic press brake has a gravity-loaded Y1/Y2 ram and optical guard/muting context; a lift table, robot gripper, hydraulic fixture or machine-tool hydraulic chuck has **different** retention and exposure requirements. The generic question transfers, but Cincinnati's table and Parker's VA130 ratings do not. The operator must never use a temperature alarm or drive shutdown as permission to reach into an unproved gravity-load zone.

## Same-session original Chromalox source-depth — low reservoir level can create a *second* hazard if an immersion heater remains powered
**Chromalox**, [*TM-Series Industrial Flanged Immersion Heaters — Installation, Operation and Maintenance*, PD457-2, August 2010](https://assets.omega.com/manuals/M5622.pdf), original 10-page manufacturer manual hosted by Omega. PDF pp. 1–2 (cover and installation) and printed p. 7 (PDF p. 6) directly inspected. **DOC-CONFIRMED for this exact heater family**, not evidence of a Cincinnati or OpenPressBrake installed Chromalox unit:
- The **integral thermostat, if supplied, is for ordinary temperature regulation and is not fail-safe**; the manufacturer explicitly calls for suitable backup controls where failure can injure people or damage property (cover, PDF p. 0).
- The effective heated portion must remain submerged; the manufacturer warns of **fire** if the heater becomes exposed at low liquid level (PDF p. 1). The manual's specific minimum immersion depth in its operating section is **product-specific**, not a generic press-brake heater design number.
- Electrical servicing requires disconnection of all heater power; heater-sheath compatibility, watt density, sediment, moisture/ground faults and temperature-control arrangements are installation-dependent (PDF pp. 1–2, 6).

**New cross-source failure hypothesis (INFERENCE; NOT an observed machine fault):** Cincinnati's *low reservoir level -> main-drive shutdown* and *heater operable independently of motor drive* could coexist with an installation where the heater is not cut off by the low-level function. In that conditional architecture, a dry or partly uncovered heater can overheat despite the motor having stopped. **Neither OEM source proves that this unsafe arrangement exists on any Cincinnati machine**: the actual heater feed, low-level interlock, separate high-limit, installation geometry and wiring are UNKNOWN. Conversely, a normal thermostat alone is not an adequate substitute for whatever backup controls the exact heater application requires.

| Conditional state | Separate physical proposition | Wrong shortcut |
|---|---|---|
| Low fluid level / motor stopped | Is any heater still powered? Does its heated portion remain submerged? Is there independent low-level/high-limit protection appropriate to exact unit? | "Level fault stopped pump, so all thermal energy is safe." |
| Thermostat contacts stuck/failed | Does backup overtemperature/low-level control remove heater power before a hazardous condition? | "Thermostat shows OFF, therefore heater cannot overheat." |
| Heater power isolated | Are heater terminals verified de-energized and hot elements/fluids safe for the intended task? | "HMI off / motor off means electrical and thermal zero energy." |
| Fluid/heater replacement | Are wetted materials, watt density, minimum immersion, overtemp and level control requalified? | "Any oil heater with the right voltage is equivalent." |

**Practical design principle:** if an immersion heater is actually installed, make an approved level-protective heater cutoff, suitable independent high-limit where required, labeled isolation and safe service access easier than defeating alarms. These controls must be allocated to the *heater fire/electrical/thermal hazard*, not silently relabeled as the Pilz hydraulic ram-holding function. This extends the prior [FLUID-FIRE-01](4400_FLUID_FIRE_01_HSE_MISTS2_FIRE_RESISTANT_HYDRAULIC_FLUID_AND_SEAL_COMPATIBILITY_AUDIT_2026-10-26.md) oil-aerosol/compatibility branch with a distinct **immersion-heater dry-fire** path. No new numerical trip point, test method, energized dry-fire test, selected-machine fact or release.

# 4400 VAL-STD-01 — ISO 13849 validation edition, draft transition and calculation/physical-proof boundary
**2026-10-28; documentary source audit, NON-GRADUATED.** UTC start captured 2026-10-28T07:38:47.433Z. No machine-specific safety validation, simulation, PL calculation or independent competency test.

## Question and predecessor reconciliation
Can a published ISO 13849 edition, a SISTEMA Performance Level report or a safety-PLC application-software worksheet establish that a press brake's hazardous Y1/Y2 motion stops and its gravity-loaded ram stays held? **No.** These are different evidence propositions. This audit is a narrow *edition and validation-tool scope correction*, not a discovery that physical proof is necessary: the [selected-machine intake](../safety-course/4400_SELECTED_MACHINE_PHYSICAL_WITNESS_INTAKE_AND_RELEASE_GATE_2026-10-23.md), [cumulative package](../safety-course/SAFETY_DESIGN_PACKAGE_TRACEABILITY_TEMPLATE.md), 2520–25F0 and RST-01 already teach that separation. The canonical safety index included ISO 6909 and ISO 13855 editions, but lacked a direct ISO 13849-2 edition/draft and SISTEMA/SOFTEMA revision-to-proof crosswalk.

## Original official publisher and institutional sources (directly inspected 2026-10-28)
1. **ISO 13849-1:2023** edition 4, published **2023-04-26**: methodology for design/integration of safety-related control parts (SRP/CS), including software; covers electrical, hydraulic, pneumatic and mechanical technologies for high-demand/continuous use. Its public abstract does **not** choose a particular machine's safety functions or required PLr. [ISO publisher](https://www.iso.org/standard/73481.html). **DOC-CONFIRMED publisher abstract/metadata only.**
2. **ISO 13849-2:2012** edition 2, published **2012-10-05**: ISO explicitly identifies it as the **current published Part 2**. Its abstract addresses validation by analysis and testing of specified safety functions, category and achieved PL of SRP/CS designed under Part 1. [ISO publisher](https://www.iso.org/standard/53640.html). **DOC-CONFIRMED abstract/metadata only.**
3. **ISO/DIS 13849-2** is a **draft**, stage **40.99 on 2026-10-20** (DIS approved for registration as FDIS), **not** a published replacement. Its draft abstract addresses basic/well-tried safety principles, conditions for possible fault exclusions and design/validation guidance for mechanical, pneumatic, hydraulic and electrical SRP/CS under Part 1:2023. [ISO draft publisher](https://www.iso.org/standard/87709.html). **DOC-CONFIRMED draft status/public abstract; not an adopted normative prescription.**
4. **IFA/DGUV SISTEMA**, tool page updated **2025-10-31**, lists **3.0.4 build 5**. Its explicit edition map: SISTEMA **1.x → ISO 13849-1:2008**, **2.x → 2015**, **3.x → 2023**. It models declared SRP/CS architecture and calculates attained PL from entered category, CCF, MTTFd and DCavg. [Official IFA SISTEMA](https://www.dguv.de/ifa/praxishilfen/practical-solutions-machine-safety/software-sistema/index.jsp). **DOC-CONFIRMED tool purpose/version mapping.**
5. **IFA/DGUV SOFTEMA**, tool page updated **November 2025**, lists **1.4.4**. Its matrix method assists specification, validation, testing and modification documentation of **safety-related application software**. [Official IFA SOFTEMA](https://www.dguv.de/ifa/praxishilfen/practical-solutions-machine-safety/software-softema/index.jsp). **DOC-CONFIRMED tool scope.**
6. **IFA/DGUV 13849 practical aids**, describing **IFA Report 1/2025**, explicitly states that the **2023 Part 1 revision incorporated validation requirements from Part 2** and expanded lifecycle/SRS/software/subsystem guidance. [IFA institutional source](https://www.dguv.de/ifa%3B/praxishilfen/praxishilfen-maschinenschutz/sicherheit-von-maschinensteuerungen/index.jsp). **DOC-CONFIRMED institutional interpretation, not controlled normative text.** This does not withdraw the still-current published 2012 Part 2.

**Access boundary:** Full paid ISO normative clauses and full draft text were not inspected. No clause-by-clause compliance, specific fault-exclusion allowance, diagnostic coverage, category, PL, PLr or stopping distance is claimed. Publisher abstracts and IFA summaries are not substitutes for controlled copies in a qualified assessment.

## Five independent propositions and witnesses
| Proposition | Evidence required | What it does not prove |
|---|---|---|
| **BND/STD** applicable standard edition | Machine identity, build/retrofit history, jurisdiction, Type-C scope and qualified edition selection | Any achieved safety function |
| **SRS/ARC** intended safety function and architecture | As-built SRS, installed safety-program identity, exact Pilz/AKAS I/O, physical final-element wiring, dependencies/CCF | Actual physical stop/hold |
| **VAL-PL** achieved category/PL | Controlled model/report with matching SISTEMA edition or qualified alternative, selected component data, exclusions, CCF and independent review | Truth of uninspected wiring, machine guarding or ram stopping |
| **VAL-SW** application program | Versioned program, traceable SRS, independent logic/fault/transition review; SOFTEMA optional aid | Hydraulic holding or residual energy |
| **PHY** installed machine safe state | Independent physical Y1/Y2 stop/hold, guard/access, energy isolation and fresh restart under justified conditions | Automatic award of PL or certification |

**INFERENCE — adversarial examples, not observed faults:** A model assumes a qualified mirror NC while an unapproved generic NC was substituted; two nominal hydraulic channels share a pilot/pressure dependency; a correct program signature coexists with altered as-built wiring. Each can invalidate a physical-safety conclusion without changing a printed PL result. An ordinary LinuxCNC/FPGA signal or HMI indicator is never independent personnel-safety authority.

## Practical maintenance/change-control design (INFERENCE)
Bind each evidence record to exact machine/SRS, controller and program signature, safety input/output SKU, valve and hydraulic revision, guard, calculation tool and Part 1 edition, published Part 2 edition, witness/date. Keep calculation, application-software and physical records separate. On replacement, wiring/program/firmware/guard change, or changed fault-exclusion premise, create **CHG**, mark affected VAL/PHY **STALE**, and require scoped requalification. Make approved replacement kits, labels and a short change map easy to use; an onerous workflow that encourages bypass is a design defect.

**No hazardous experiments** are proposed: do not weld a contactor, defeat guards, deliberately drop a ram or inject faults with people exposed. Stage A prerequisites, independent reviewer, secondary restraint and remote/isolated safe test plan precede any qualified physical validation.

## Same-session original IFA tool migration and misleading-green addendum (DOC-CONFIRMED)
The original **IFA/DGUV “SISTEMA 3.0 — The main changes at a glance”** PDF (15-page web PDF, printed pages 1–12; direct text inspected at printed pp.1–2, 7–10 and 12) gives a sharper, practical failure path than the tool home page alone. [Original manufacturer/institutional PDF](https://www.dguv.de/medien/ifa/en/pra/softwa/sistema/innovations_sistema_3-0.pdf):
- **Conversion is one-way:** SISTEMA 2.x projects can be opened in 3.x but the converted format is not backwards compatible. Original 2.x project files are preserved; library conversion uses a separate tool, and protected libraries cannot be converted from the protected form. **1.x** requires an intermediate **2.x** conversion. IFA specifically warns that 2.x projects may produce *new messages* after opening in 3.x because DC/CCF descriptions and checks changed; review those messages rather than copying a prior “pass”.
- **Safety-model input changes:** the 2023 edition introduces/reworks qualitative software fields, EMI route/documentation, category checks, alternative PL/PFH method and fault-exclusion treatment. IFA's 3.0 document says a 2.x subsystem using the former simplified method can be marked **red** after migration. Do not assume a changed color means a physical hardware change; it can be a revised assessment criterion requiring qualified review.
- **Green is not an independent oracle:** IFA explicitly allows a *yellow* note to be documented and then **downgraded to green**. It does **not** allow a red message to be downgraded this way. The report still includes the documented messages; a green dashboard alone is therefore not evidence that the underlying fault-exclusion/maintenance assumption has been resolved. The IFA document states that the tool assists, rather than replaces, assessment under the standard.
- **Fault-exclusion high-PL trap:** IFA's explanatory text for 13849-1:2023 says **PL e shall not be based solely on fault exclusion**. SISTEMA may reduce a subsystem with an FE to PL d and issue yellow messages in specific manual-override/FE cases. This is an **institutional explanation**, not a claim that any particular OpenPressBrake architecture has PL d/e or qualifies for a fault exclusion. A qualified designer must examine the controlled normative edition and actual failure modes.
- **Exact 2025 tool defect corrections:** IFA's [02-Nov-2025 release notice](https://dguv.de/ifa/;/praxishilfen/practical-solutions-machine-safety/software-sistema/newsletter/2025/quartal_4/index.jsp) says **3.0.4 build 5** fixed earlier v3 faults including a documentation-field edit sometimes **not being saved**, documentation crashes, inaccurate version query, and summary loops. A historical green report or unsaved comment from an affected older build is not a durable validation record without verifying the saved project, exported report and exact tool build. **DOC-CONFIRMED notice, INFERENCE change-control implication; no observed user project.**

**Proposed reviewer gate (INFERENCE):** preserve original 2.x project and library SHA/backup; record old and new tool builds and normative editions; import a **copy**; review every new/red/yellow message and all fault exclusions, DC/CCF, EMI, software and alternative-method assumptions; verify all comments survive save/reopen; keep both report revisions and a signed change disposition; separately validate selected safety hardware and physical stop/hold. Never treat a manually greened note as a substitute for fault-exclusion rationale or an independent physical witness. No local tool run was justified without an actual selected project; **TEST-CONFIRMED none**.

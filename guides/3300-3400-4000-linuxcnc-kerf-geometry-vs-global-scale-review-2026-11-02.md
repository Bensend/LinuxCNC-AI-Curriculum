# 3300/3400/4000 — Kerf compensation: geometric-offset versus global-scale review card

**2026-11-02 | SOURCE/DOC-CONFIRMED conditional; NOT RUN.** See [CAM-KERF-170](../research/4400_LINUXCNC_CAM_KERF_170_GLOBAL_SCALE_VS_QTPLASMAC_CUTTER_COMP_SOURCE_AUDIT_2026-11-02.md). This is a **disconnected code-review exercise**, not production NC or machine commissioning.

A CAD/CAM “kerf” option is not automatically a contour offset. Inspect whether it translates every edge by a perpendicular distance, accounts for concave corners and holes, or merely scales coordinates about the origin. Pinned PathForge uses the last approach. For a translated 10×10 square at X/Y=100..110 and `kerf=1`, its current implementation predicts X/Y=120..132; a symmetric 1-unit geometric offset would yield 99..111. A single CIRCLE has zero horizontal endpoint span and bypasses the current kerf operation. Repeated conversion of the same in-memory DXF compounds transformations. Freeze original CAD entity handles, units, center, radius, contours, part placement, output and warning list before any transformation.

The [review-only pytest](../examples/4000_cam_kerf_170/test_pathforge_kerf_geometry_review_only.py) supplies three independent falsifiable checks: translated centroid invariant, circle radius response and original document immutability. It uses real `ezdxf` in-memory entities and the pinned `GcodeBuilder` API. It is **NOT EXECUTED**. Do not use an interpreter's acceptance of G-code as evidence that a part's contour was offset correctly.

LinuxCNC's [G41.1/G42.1 interpreter path](https://github.com/LinuxCNC/linuxcnc/blob/86cdca76fa2a36274c432caa21952b23c267989a/src/emc/rs274ngc/interp_convert.cc) uses the D word as diameter and D/2 as offset radius. QtPlasmaC documents `G41.1 D#<_hal[plasmac.kerf-width]>`, `G42.1` and `G40`, **but also prohibits G-code/velocity THC and overcut with compensation active**. Select a machine/process-specific approach and retain the independence of personnel-safety authority; do not insert cutter comp blindly into a plasma post. For offline compensation, validate contour winding, holes, nesting spacing, arc/line joins, kerf unit/width, lead-ins, and unchanged part placement before any simulation, let alone machine execution.

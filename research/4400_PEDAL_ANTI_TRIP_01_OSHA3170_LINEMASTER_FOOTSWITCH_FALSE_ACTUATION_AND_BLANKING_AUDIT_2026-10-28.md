# 4400-PEDAL-ANTI-TRIP-01 — foot-pedal false activation, blanked protection and mechanical anti-trip geometry
**2026-10-28 | source-depth / failure-path audit; NOT a selected-machine validation or a new basic accidental-cycling discovery.**

## Precise engineering question
A foot pedal has a visible metal cover and its input is deasserted at idle. Can that alone establish that a press-brake operator cannot accidentally initiate a hazardous stroke while adjusting a small part? **No.** A mechanical cover reduces some unintended inputs; a deliberate anti-trip release can reduce others; neither establishes independent point-of-operation protection, current mute/blanking configuration, safe stop/hold or correct fresh rearm.

## Failure-path analysis (conditional, not a physical test)
| Threat/event | Mechanical/ordinary-control path | Independent protective proposition still needed |
|---|---|---|
| Falling offcut, slipping boot, helper steps on treadle | Exposed pedal can become an unintended **cycle request**. A well-fitted shield and anti-trip latch may reduce this initiating event; a loose/movable guard or badly sized opening may not. | Guarding/protective sensing appropriate to all exposed people and access routes; independent safety stop/final elements and physical holding. |
| Operator deliberately holds or rides pedal while reaching into die | Even a correctly operated anti-trip latch is already released; **pedal guard is not a hand-location safeguard**. | Appropriate machine-mode protection, access/occupancy and point-of-operation safety function; no person-exposed live reach test. |
| Light curtain remains blanked from prior workpiece | Pedal false-actuation mitigation does not restore missing protective coverage. An HMI 'guard enabled' lamp does not prove blanking geometry or effective protection. | Current recipe/tool/mute/blanking state, actual sensing field, qualified reach/stop-time and alternative safeguards; stale-proof review after part change. |
| Foot pedal sticks ON, spring/latch fails, cable short, input stale, pedal remains depressed across reset | Ordinary LinuxCNC may see a persistent command; anti-repeat/edge-based logic may be useful **normal-control risk reduction**, but software-only suppression cannot be credited as independent personnel protection. | Safety-related restart prevention and independent final-element withdrawal/physical witness appropriate to actual design. |
| Guard/cover makes cycling awkward, boot cannot fit, pedal slides across floor | Operator may remove cover, relocate pedal into a reach hazard, defeat latch or leave pedal under stock. | Adjustable secured position, adequate boot clearance, easy cleaning, deliberate ergonomics review and post-maintenance requalification. |

**Authority boundary:** Pedal/latch/ordinary HAL input = operator-command interface and mechanical false-trigger mitigation unless a selected, validated safety architecture proves more. Independent safety controller and protective devices own safety-related stop/inhibit; hydraulic/mechanical final elements own physical motion/energy behavior. LinuxCNC/FPGA status or CRC cannot substitute for any of those physical witnesses.

## Minimal practical architecture and acceptance evidence
1. Prefer a **securely mounted, readily usable shielded/anti-trip foot control** matched to actual footwear, debris, sheet-support position and floor conditions. Confirm selected part number, contact/return/latch variants and actual installation. A shield alone is not an interlock or safety-rated input.
2. Retain the existing independent front/side/rear protective design; document the **current** blanking/muting configuration for each material/tool/job and revalidate after change. Never permit production based solely on a historical light-curtain pass.
3. Treat stuck/persistent pedal input, power restoration, mode change and safety reset as **separate** restart threats. Specify release-before-fresh-start semantics for the appropriate controller/safety function without guessing the installed logic.
4. In qualified **de-energized, isolated inspection**, review pedal cover/latch condition, mounting/cord, debris paths, access from each side, boot fit and guard reinstall friction. Any powered/physical safety validation needs a separately approved plan with people outside the hazard zone; no simulated or live hazardous test is authorized by this audit.
5. Stage A existing **B/E/F/G** intake is sufficient: request installed pedal identity/photographs and selected job/mute state only if relevant; do not lengthen the short A–G first-contact owner packet.

# 4400-ACC-NORM-01 — Ten public standards-scope and shutdown-isolation cases

**Status:** PAPER / UNSCORED; no legal or safety certification. [Standards-scope audit](../research/4400_ISO4413_2010_ACCUMULATOR_VENT_OR_ISOLATE_EXCEPTION_PED_SCOPE_AUDIT_2026-10-25.md). ISO 4413 §5.4.3.2 was reviewed via public **preview**, not the complete controlled normative document. Each answer must distinguish exact OEM instructions, standard-preview text, national applicability, physical safe-state and maintenance service.

1. **“All machines must dump on E-stop.”** A builder cites a Parker BA installation manual. Is it a universal rule for a different accumulator and every protective-stop phase?
2. **“Always vent at shutdown.”** A system requires retained pressure to complete a protective function. How does the ISO 4413:2010 preview's special-case language change the *question* without automatically authorizing retention?
3. **“Isolation is enough.”** A positive isolator retains pressure after shutdown. What personnel servicing and independent load-holding evidence is still needed?
4. **“PED mandates this one valve.”** A supplier attributes automatic discharge to Directive 2014/68/EU. What exact legal/normative source verification is missing?
5. **“Safety-rated component means machine safe.”** A catalog SAF block is selected but no physical pressure-zone or gravity-load witness exists. What remains unproved?
6. **“Clamping exception.”** A lathe chuck retains hydraulic clamp pressure. Why does a low-risk example not excuse loss of clamp force or an unsafe release during access?
7. **“Shutdown equals LOTO.”** Ordinary LinuxCNC OFF leaves gas precharge and a trapped actuator volume. What separate physical and personal controls govern servicing?
8. **“Fire changes the assumption.”** A thermal fuse releases gas while a proposed stop depends on stored energy. Which SRS and physical witnesses become stale?
9. **“Edition mismatch.”** An old brochure and newer preview differ. How should the reviewer preserve source edition, device variant and unresolved conflict rather than silently select a convenient answer?
10. **“Easier to bypass.”** A technician must remove a fixed guard to reach a bleed. What lower-friction redesign could preserve both safe access and positive energy control?

**Response contract:** identify SOURCE-CONFIRMED, DOC-CONFIRMED, STANDARDS-PREVIEW, INFERENCE and UNKNOWN correctly; specify machine-specific evidence still required. No fabricated hydraulic truth table, stopping time, pressure or PL/SIL. No scenarios executed, no independent score and **G0/G1 HOLD**.

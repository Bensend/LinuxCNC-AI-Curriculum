## Foundation sequence

### 4000-F1 — hardware block contract and taxonomy

Create a canonical block specification/template before selecting detailed circuits. It must capture:

- LinuxCNC/HAL/HostMot2-facing function;
- commanded physical authority;
- electrical interface and voltage/current/rate envelope;
- independent feedback/witnesses;
- validity/freshness/watchdog semantics;
- reset, brownout, FPGA/MCU reset and communication-loss behavior;
- local hardware enable/inhibit and fault latching;
- ownership/rearm/recovery semantics;
- realtime timing/bandwidth;
- isolation, grounding, EMC/ESD/transient boundaries;
- diagnostic observability;
- parameterization envelope;
- safety boundary and explicit non-safety claims;
- known-good reference topology/source where one exists;
- simulation/verification needed versus standard-engineering acceptance;
- AI/EDA handoff requirements.

### 4000-F2 — baseline reusable board architecture

Map the first-pass controller into blocks. Current expected families:

- FPGA/HostMot2 core;
- Ethernet communications integrated into core;
- USB-C/service/programming integrated into core where justified;
- power-entry, regulation, sequencing and supervision;
- digital inputs;
- protected digital outputs;
- encoder/feedback inputs;
- step/dir outputs;
- analog/PWM/PDM command outputs;
- proportional-solenoid/current driver;
- drive enable/fault interfaces;
- watchdog/failsafe hardware;
- expansion/service/diagnostic interfaces.

Do not assume this list is final; derive additions/removals from LinuxCNC interfaces and 3000 machine contracts.

### 4000-F3 — proven-topology baseline survey

For each applicable block, first identify and inspect proven open-source circuits, prioritizing Colorlight-family LinuxCNC/FPGA boards and other LinuxCNC-community hardware with available schematics. Preserve known-good topology before parameterizing or simplifying it. Press-brake-specific circuits without a direct proven equivalent, especially proportional-valve/current drivers, remain independently engineered from appropriate reference designs and standard engineering principles.

### 4000-F4 — AI skill/source enrichment

Before freezing each block, inspect applicable open-source electronics/PCB/FPGA/KiCad design guidance and AI-readable engineering skills already identified by the project. Use them to review the block and improve the design contract. Do not treat generic AI skill prose as evidence over datasheets, schematics, source, or engineering calculations.

## Simulation policy

Use standard engineering and proven reference topology by default. Simulate only where it materially reduces uncertainty: unstable analog/control loops, switching/current-control behavior, transient protection margins, timing-sensitive interfaces, fault transitions, or a disputed/novel behavior. Do not run repetitive simulations for ordinary pullups, buffers, dividers, connector mapping or already-proven copied topology merely to create activity.

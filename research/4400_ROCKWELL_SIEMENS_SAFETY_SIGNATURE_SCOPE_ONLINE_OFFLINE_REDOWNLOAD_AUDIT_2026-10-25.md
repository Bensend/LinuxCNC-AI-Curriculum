# 4400-CONFIG-01 — A matching safety-program signature is not a machine-safety proof
**2026-10-25 | Original manufacturer documentation audit | NO selected-machine or runtime proof | NON-GRADUATED**

## Question and prediction (declared before evaluating applicability)
Can an unchanged safety-program signature coexist with a change to ordinary control behavior, or with a wrong physical machine, and what would the signature prove? **Prediction:** it identifies a defined safety-program/configuration state, but cannot prove the standard program, external wiring, installed final elements, hydraulic Y1/Y2 stopping, guarding, or an authorized fresh restart. Check the exact vendor boundaries rather than asserting a universal hash property.

## Adversarial fault paths
1. **Ordinary application changes, safety signature unchanged:** LinuxCNC or other standard logic now requests motion sooner; a matching safety signature alone cannot approve changed hazard timing or reset/restart sequence. Require dependency/impact analysis and independent safety functions.
2. **Safety controller replacement:** accepted signature displayed, but target identity, module suffix, safety I/O configuration, actual wiring and final elements are unverified. HOLD.
3. **Signature deleted for service:** vendor says protection assumptions change; isolate hazardous energy and maintain physical exclusion, record authorized change, revalidate before release. A signature recreated by a technician is not independent validation.
4. **2012 legacy download:** a standard-only download can restore safety-tag initial values; assess potential state-machine rearm/first-cycle effects for that exact platform. No physical motion predicted without system design.
5. **Multiple F-CPUs:** matching offline data with a different network target is an identity failure; independently identify the actual F-CPU before accepting signatures.
6. **Safety logic unchanged but wiring moved:** no program signature can demonstrate a final element de-energizes or a suspended ram is restrained. Require selected physical proof.

## Safe architecture and human factors
Keep an accepted, revision-controlled safety baseline **separate** from LinuxCNC/HAL/FPGA normal control. Label the controller, module, firmware, signature and **as-built physical** revisions. Present a one-page compare sheet at every download/replacement. Make the verified recovery path faster than a shortcut: known-good backup, exact spare-part list, clear status-only diagnostics, no safety input jumpers, explicit independent reviewer. Never use an HMI green indicator as the physical stop/hold witness.

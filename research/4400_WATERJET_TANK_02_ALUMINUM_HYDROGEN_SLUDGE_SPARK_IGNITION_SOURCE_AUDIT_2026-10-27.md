# 4400-WATERJET-TANK-02 — Waterjet catcher-tank aluminum fines, hydrogen and later spark-producing cuts

**2026-10-27 | distinct 4400 cross-machine process/maintenance safety mechanism | NON-GRADUATED**

## Question, prediction and predecessor reconciliation
Does a CNC waterjet's cut completion, jet stop or pump isolation make its catcher tank chemically safe after repeated aluminum cutting, especially before switching to a spark-producing material? **Prediction (INFERENCE): no.** Reaction products and retained sludge may remain hazardous after motion/pressure stops; ventilation, material sequence, waste management and ignition controls have separate owners.

A complete canonical safety index, research plan and progress scan found no waterjet catcher-tank hydrogen module. Existing [DUST-05 predecessor correction](4400_DUST_05_PREDECESSOR_OVERLAP_RECONCILIATION_AND_SOURCE_DELTA_2026-10-27.md) and DUST-01..04 already cover combustible dust, collector fire, venting and material swap; [WELD-GAS-01](4400_WELD_GAS_01_SHIELDING_GAS_OXYGEN_DEFICIENCY_SCREEN_VENTILATION_AND_RESCUE_SOURCE_AUDIT_2026-10-27.md) covers shielding-gas oxygen displacement. **Incremental causal mechanism:** waterjet-generated aluminum fines retained **under water**, hydrogen generation and accumulation in catcher-tank/sludge pockets, with a later spark-producing cut or maintenance ignition source. It is **not** a new generic dust or ventilation lesson and does not imply all aluminum cutting creates a hazardous atmosphere.

## Failure-path composition
1. **Normal process control:** LinuxCNC/other CNC commands cutting and may record material/job identity. A completed job does not remove wet reactive metal fines.
2. **Physical reaction boundary:** retained aluminum fines + water may generate hydrogen; accumulation depends on material, sludge quantity, tank geometry, ventilation, dwell time and other unverified conditions. Do **not** calculate hydrogen concentration from generic machine dimensions.
3. **Ignition boundary:** a subsequent spark-producing material operation, electrical fault, static discharge or maintenance hot work can create ignition. The OMAX manufacturer explicitly calls out titanium as an example of spark-producing material.
4. **Protective and maintenance authority:** qualified material compatibility review, tank maintenance/cleaning, ventilation and ignition-source controls must remain independent of the CNC cycle-done signal. A pressure bleed or E-stop cannot prove a tank atmosphere safe.
5. **Rearm/changeover:** a new material/job recipe must not silently override a pending waste/atmosphere hazard. Qualified human acceptance is required where hazard analysis identifies it; do not assume a normal recipe bit is a safety function.

## Practical design and human factors
Make material tracking and sludge removal easy; provide a visible tank-cleaning interval and material-change warning; design safe accessible maintenance and effective ventilation/egress, avoiding a lid arrangement that traps gas or a cleaning method that creates new ignition. Use approved waste disposal and a competent site-specific fire/atmosphere plan. Operators should not have to defeat a cover to check sludge. Where hazards cannot be controlled or evaluated, **do not run with people exposed**; isolated remote experimentation does not excuse uncontrolled flammable atmosphere.

# 4400 HOSE-SHIELD-01 — Abrasion cover, pressure containment sleeve and hose-end whip restraint are not interchangeable

**2026-10-29 UTC | Exact manufacturer-source delta | NON-GRADUATED | No selected machine or physical test.**

## Question and predecessor/novelty reconciliation
A maintainer sees a covered hydraulic hose near an operator and says: “The sleeve protects us if it leaks.” **Which physical failure modes does that exact cover actually protect against, and where does the failed fluid go?**

This is **not a new discovery of hydraulic injection injury, hose routing or burst-flow arrest**. Those were already covered in [INJ-01 (2026-10-24)](4400_HYDRAULIC_PINHOLE_INJECTION_HOSE_FAILURE_ISOLATION_SOURCE_AUDIT_2026-10-24.md), [HOSE-ROUTE-01 (2026-10-26)](4400_HOSE_ROUTE_01_GATES_2025_HOSE_ASSEMBLY_ROUTING_CLAMP_TWIST_SERVICE_PROVENANCE_AUDIT_2026-10-26.md), [hose-burst velocity-fuse source](4400_HOSE_BURST_VELOCITY_FUSE_FLOW_WINDOW_AND_UNSAFE_TEST_TRANSFER_AUDIT_2026-10-24.md) and [HYD-SURGE-01 (2026-10-28)](4400_HYD_SURGE_01_PARKER_GS4_PEAK_PRESSURE_GAUGE_BLIND_SPOT_AND_TEST_RIG_SAFETY_SOURCE_AUDIT_2026-10-28.md). The **incremental exact-source distinction** is: (1) ordinary abrasion protection does not imply *pressure* containment; (2) a qualified sleeve has **specified failure envelope, attachment and discharge path**; (3) a hose-end containment grip addresses *whip on detachment*, not jet containment; (4) a sleeve can obscure damage and complicate inspection. This is source-depth of existing hose safety, not a newly scored competency.

## Failure-path distinctions and required physical witness
| Protection claim | Physical failure addressed | What it cannot prove | Qualified evidence required |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Abrasion guard / spiral wrap / wear jacket** | External rubbing, cuts, some surface wear | Pinhole spray containment, hose-end whip, pressure isolation, gravity hold | Exact cover product/installation and accessible wear inspection; no assumed jet rating |
| **Pressure containment sleeve** | Redirection/containment of specified hose-body leak/burst *within product conditions* | Indefinite protection, hose integrity, absence of spray at sleeve ends, hydraulic safe state | Exact matched hose/sleeve/clamp assembly; pressure/temperature/size/medium, rated envelope, drain/exit location, condition and inspection method |
| **Hose-end containment grip** | Whip following fitting separation, when correctly sized/anchored | Pinhole injection, hydraulic pressure zero, ram load holding | Exact grip/anchor/assembly rating and fitting separation path; no transfer of UHP article numbers |
| **Support grip / bend restrictor** | Weight support or excessive local bending | Jet containment or detachment restraint without separate qualified evidence | Correct mechanical load/bend geometry and accessible inspection |
| **Burst-flow/velocity fuse** | Some line-break flow events if selected/validated | Slow pinhole, other trapped zones, independent mechanical holding, arbitrary flow conditions | Exact hydraulic topology and device curve, proof of response for selected failure envelope |
| **Independent stop/hold/isolation** | Selected machine protective response and gravity-load/stored-energy containment | Direct hose spray protection unless separately guarded | Stage A C/D/F/G installed safety/final-element and qualified physical witness |

### Adversarial cases (INFERENCE, not an observed incident)
- A technician installs a cheap spiral abrasion wrap on an operator-facing hose and labels it “burst protection.” The cover might delay abrasion while providing **no documented spray containment**. Correct label, product selection, routing or physical barrier before crediting it.
- A correctly rated sleeve redirects a pinhole jet **toward its end clamp**, where a worker routinely stands or reaches. The sleeve's product pressure rating is not a site-specific **discharge-zone** risk assessment.
- The sleeve obscures a kink or worn reinforcement; maintenance leaves it in place because removal is awkward. Rework inspection access, fasteners, replacement path and schedule rather than encouraging blind touch checks.
- A hose pulls off a fitting. A sleeve might redirect fluid but does not establish that a hose-end grip will restrain the moving assembly, nor that the ram will stay up.
- An operator interprets Gates' **five-minute** claim as permission to remain nearby for five minutes after a leak. That is **not** what the product page establishes; a leak demands withdrawal, independent safe stop/hold, isolation, qualified pressure verification and replacement under approved procedure.

## Practical architecture and human factors
**Normal control:** LinuxCNC/FPGA may display leak/pressure alarms and log cycles, not authorize exposed work. **Monitoring:** a visible spill, pressure alarm or maintenance flag may reveal failure; none verifies every trapped volume. **Safety-related control:** qualified independent safety system owns protective motion inhibition/stop as designed and validated. **Physical:** protect line of sight by safer routing/rigid barriers or a correctly specified, maintained containment sleeve; independently restrain a gravity-loaded ram and isolate/verify each hydraulic energy zone. Design drains away from people, accessible shield condition checks, easy replacement with correct clamps and no temptation to remove guards. A sleeve is not a substitute for a fail-safe machine architecture.

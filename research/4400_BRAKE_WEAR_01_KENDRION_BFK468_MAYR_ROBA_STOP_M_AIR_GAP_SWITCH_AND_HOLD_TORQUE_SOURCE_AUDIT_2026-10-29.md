## Primary original manufacturer sources (exact product/edition)
1. **Kendrion INTORQ BFK468**, original operating instructions BA 14.0190, material 33002357, document history **v4.0 01/2024** (the PDF also carries retained 11/2013 page-footers). [Original PDF hosted by Lenze](https://www.lenze.com/fileadmin/lenze-selection/documents/en/Operating_Mounting_Instructions/operating-instructions-spring-applied-brake-BFK468_EN.pdf). Inspected PDF text: §3.1.6 printed p.14; §5.2.1 pp.31–33; §7.1–7.2 pp.39–40. **DOC-CONFIRMED:** the optional microswitch can be configured for *release* or *wear/air-gap* checking; the wear-check arrangement in Fig.17 is to be evaluated when the brake is **de-energized**. During release, asymmetric armature attraction can temporarily simulate the maximum gap and actuate the switch, with potential cycling if interpreted during the transient. The manufacturer says friction energy, initial braking speed, cycle count, spring fatigue and mechanical play affect wear; inspection includes rotor thickness and air gap. The manual specifically requires that the drive be disconnected from the load before the cited air-gap inspection. **Important limitation:** a microswitch is not a torque transducer; the manufacturer's broad claim about faults covered by its release interlock is not a selected machine safety-performance guarantee.
2. **Mayr ROBA-stop-M Type 891.06_.1, sizes 60/100**, Mayr instruction E070 06 005 000 4 EN dated **2016-06-14**, embedded as Appendix 10 (printed Mayr p.14/17, host PDF p.123) in **SEW-EURODRIVE 31558941/EN, 11/2025**. [Original SEW-hosted PDF](https://download.sew-eurodrive.com/download/pdf/31558941.pdf). **DOC-CONFIRMED:** maintenance evaluates air gap, rotor thickness, hub/rotor toothing, friction surface and brake torque; OEM recommends intervals for this exact product, explicitly says actual intervals depend on duty. The manual states that holding applications need friction-pair conditioning and subsequent torque verification; if conditioning cannot be performed, its dimensioning requires an added allowance. **Do not turn this into an instruction to dynamically brake an exposed gravity-loaded machine**: qualified OEM/plant-specific validation and physical exclusion are prerequisites. No Mayr numerical limits or intervals are transferred to other brake models.
3. **Mayr manufacturer product explanation** for ROBA brake-checker [2019 original article](https://www.mayr.com/en/news/press-releases/intelligent-safety-for-small-brakes~1885): sensorless monitoring infers brake states from coil current/voltage and other signals. **DOC-CONFIRMED** product capability; **INFERENCE** such diagnostics may improve condition awareness. Neither coil-signature analysis nor a switch proves selected-axis holding force without the required safety-function design and physical witness.

## Physical chain and failure-path distinctions
| Question | Observable, but insufficient | Required independent discriminator |
|---|---|---|
| Was electrical release requested? | LinuxCNC/FPGA brake command, relay output, coil voltage | Actual selected brake wiring and release mechanism, separate from personnel-safety authority |
| Did the armature move? | Optional release microswitch, coil-signature inference | Exact product, switch purpose, timing/edge interpretation, installed circuit, false transient behavior |
| Is wear within the manufacturer's envelope? | Healthy release bit or low cycle count | Exact model/revision and *de-energized, load-secured* air-gap/rotor/friction/hub inspection to OEM method |
| Can it physically hold a vertical load? | Spring-applied label, closed switch, static motor position | Correct rated application, duty/thermal/wear history, independently qualified holding/stop witness and secondary restraint |
| Can an operator safely maintain it? | E-stop or drive STO asserted | Independent mechanical support, all-energy isolation and verified no residual load/torque; follow exact OEM service procedure |
| Does a monitor alarm require stop or service? | Warning lamp, diagnostic status or HMI “BRAKE OK” | Safety-function allocation and fault reaction in the selected SRS; fail-safe restart and repair acceptance |

**INFERENCE failure chain (not observed):** repeated E-stops or holding cycles consume friction work; air gap and rotor wear change; a release-switch circuit continues to report plausible transitions; a controller reports brake healthy; actual torque capacity is unknown. Another conditional chain is *transient switch signal during armature release* misinterpreted as persistent wear fault, encouraging technicians to bypass it. These are separate failure propositions and neither proves a physical incident.

## Practical architecture and human factors
Make serviceable inspection points, accessible original-OEM gauges, visible model/variant tags, retained inspection history and **a convenient load-secured maintenance position** part of the machine design. Make replacing an out-of-limit brake easier than jumpering a troublesome switch. Display **COMMAND / BRAKE-SENSOR OBSERVED / INDEPENDENT SAFETY PERMISSION / PHYSICAL HOLD VERIFIED** as separate claims, with UNKNOWN rather than green when the last witness is missing. An HMI wear trend is useful for maintenance, but it is not personnel-safety authority. Ordinary LinuxCNC/FPGA must not be promoted into the safety chain.

For press brakes, this is a *transfer analogy*, not evidence that Y1/Y2 use electromechanical motor brakes. Their hydraulic valves, gravity-holding method, load paths, guard and independent safety configuration remain UNKNOWN. For mills, robots, lifts and automated cells, determine actual motor/brake/axis coupling and hazard before applying any of the above.

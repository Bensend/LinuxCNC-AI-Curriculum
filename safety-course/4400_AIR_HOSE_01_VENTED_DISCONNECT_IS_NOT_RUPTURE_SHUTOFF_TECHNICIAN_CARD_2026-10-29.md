# 4400 AIR-HOSE-01 — A safe quick disconnect is not a hose-burst shutoff
**2026-10-29 | short technician field card | no pressurized disconnection experiment**

A vent-before-disconnect coupling can release trapped hose pressure **when deliberately uncoupled**. It does not automatically guarantee that supply air stops if the hose tears **somewhere else**. A hose-rupture-responsive source device addresses a different failure, and positive coupling retention addresses accidental separation.

**Check three points:** the tool/hose connection is positively retained; the selected coupling vents before intentional disconnection; and a credible hose rupture has an appropriate source-side flow/pressure containment strategy. Neither receiver relief nor a machine safe-exhaust valve substitutes automatically for these. Air loss can also drop a pneumatic clamp, so mechanical load holding remains a separate hazard.

**Legal scope matters:** OSHA 1926.302(b)(7) requires a source/branch protective device for construction **pneumatic power-tool** hoses exceeding 1/2-inch inside diameter; OSHA's 1983 interpretation explicitly limits that subsection to pneumatic power tools. Do **not** apply the numeric trigger to every CNC pneumatic hose as if it were universal. Follow the actual workplace standard and OEM hose/coupling specifications.

**Make the safer setup convenient:** use rated fittings and easy one-hand vented couplings where suitable, proper routing/strain relief, reachable isolation and guards that reinstall without disturbing pressurized hoses. Do not repair a damaged hose with improvised clamps or disconnect it under pressure as a demonstration.

**General-industry distinction:** OSHA 1910.243(b)(2) requires pneumatic-tool hoses/connections suited to actual pressure and service. OSHA 1910.242(b)'s less-than-30-psi rule is for **compressed-air cleaning** with chip guarding/PPE, not every CNC air cylinder. Neither creates a universal >1/2-inch hose rupture-device trigger outside the construction pneumatic-power-tool scope.

# 4400-FIRE-SUP-01 — CNC automatic fire suppression: pneumatic detection, optional machine shutdown, bypass after discharge, and safe re-entry

**2026-10-27 | Original 4400 safety source audit | NON-GRADUATED / DOCUMENTARY ONLY.** Applies conditionally to enclosed mills, Swiss lathes, grinders, EDM and automated cells with combustible coolant or other fire exposure. No installed OpenPressBrake fire-suppression system is asserted. No physical test, blind score, fire test, simulation or machine release.

## Bounded engineering question and pre-source prediction

A self-contained CNC enclosure fire system detects a fire and releases agent even when CNC power is lost. Can the LinuxCNC/HAL machine-stop bit, an optional cylinder pressure switch, the extinguished flame, or a recharged cylinder independently authorize restarting the spindle or opening the enclosure? **Prediction (INFERENCE): no.** Distinguish independent detection/agent discharge, ordinary machine/process shutdown requests, agent inventory and impairment, the actual fire state, airborne agent/decomposition products, safe access, and qualified restart. A fire-system accessory contact is not a certified machine personnel-safety final element unless the selected assembly and function are qualified.

**Falsifier sought:** a documented OEM safety-rated fire-shutdown/restart architecture with selected physical machine validation could change the authority allocation for that *specific* chain. No such machine-specific proof was found. A pressure switch triggering an ordinary E-stop request does not establish a safety-rated electrical path or a safe enclosure atmosphere.

Do not count generic "stop != safe" as newly learned.

## Safety function and physical authority decomposition

| Layer / proposition | Legitimate evidence | False shortcut |
|---|---|---|
| **Detect** — fire/heat reaches selected tubing or sensor | Selected detector routing, coverage, condition, manufacturer qualification | "Machine power off" or one tube means every ignition zone is protected. |
| **Discharge** — agent delivered into the intended hazard | Selected agent, cylinder mass/pressure, valve/tube/nozzle integrity, enclosure leakage, service record | Pneumatic trigger or discharge switch alone proves flame extinguished. |
| **Process stop** — avoid continued fuel/ignition feed | Qualified shutdown design for spindle, coolant/oil pump, mist collector and relevant process; actual machine state | Optional pressure switch wired into ordinary LinuxCNC E-stop/HAL is a safety-rated final element. |
| **Independent machine protection** — no unexpected hazardous motion | Retained independent machine safety circuit and physical final elements | Fire relay replaces E-stop, guard or motion safety chain. |
| **Atmosphere/access** — people can safely approach/open/enter | Agent-specific qualified ventilation, atmospheric assessment where indicated, fire-service/entry control, physical isolation | Flame out, green HMI or elapsed arbitrary minutes means air safe. |
| **Rearm** — suppressor and machine independently restored | Correct refill/replace/inspect, root cause corrected, verified unbypassed interfaces, approved fresh reset/start | Shorting pressure switch, toggling CNC reset or replacing only a cylinder closes the incident. |

**Conceptual sequence, not installed logic:** `ARMED -> FIRE_DETECTED -> AGENT_RELEASED -> MACHINE_STOP_REQUESTED -> DISCHARGED/IMPAIRED + ACCESS_RESTRICTED -> QUALIFIED_FIRE/ATMOSPHERE/MECHANICAL/PROCESS_REVIEW -> SYSTEM_RESTORED -> DELIBERATE_FRESH_START`. The fire and motion state machines may advance asynchronously; a completed machine stop cannot prove the fire safe, and successful fire suppression cannot prove mechanical safe-state. A qualified design may intentionally use different sequences, but must preserve these independent propositions.

## Adversarial failure-path review

1. **Loss of CNC power:** independent pneumatic discharge may remain possible (F1), but an *electrical* machine shutdown interface, annunciator, fan, valve or telemetry may lose power. Verify fail state and power domain for each. Do not assume all interfaces survive.
2. **Detection tube cut, disconnected or depressurized during service:** can discharge or impair depending on the exact system; a cylinder gauge alone cannot prove complete detector/nozzle coverage. Preserve the selected manufacturer's service isolation/rearm process.
3. **Pressure switch opens after discharge:** CNC sees a stop request, but neither switch contacts nor HAL prove agent delivered, fire out or the air safe. A wire short, stuck contact or disabled interlock could permit restart without a charged system.
4. **Mist-collector shutdown tradeoff:** stopping extraction may help agent retention or reduce fire propagation, but could also change smoke/agent movement. The correct fan/damper strategy requires selected fire-system design; never infer that all fans should always run or always stop.
5. **Fire reignition:** coolant pump, oil mist, hot tooling, tool-placement defect, chip accumulation and incomplete process stop can reignite even after visible flame disappears. Treat root-cause repair as separate from recharge.
6. **Operator bypass after an expensive discharge:** F3 and F4 show opposite reported shop decisions. The bypass incentive is not hypothetical; neither report establishes how the switches were wired. Design accessible spare agent cartridges/cylinders, clear IMPAIRED state, local service availability and a planned safe production alternative rather than relying on warnings.
7. **Agent substitution:** CO2, HFC-227ea, FK-5-1-12 and Class D powder have different approved applications, atmospheric and residue effects. A drop-in replacement without hazard/approval and human-exposure review invalidates prior proof.
8. **Door opened immediately after discharge:** enclosure breach may reintroduce oxygen or expose workers to smoke, CO2 or decomposition products. Do not instruct operators to open the door to inspect or hand-fight an active fire; emergency response and re-entry must follow the actual hazard/agent plan.
9. **False assurance from a single pressure indicator:** cylinder pressure may indicate inventory under limited conditions, not tube routing, nozzle obstruction, actual agent concentration, fire extinguishment or safe personnel access.
10. **Common-cause power/logic:** one PLC/HAL signal that both claims "suppression healthy" and commands a normal reset can hide stale or bypassed state. Fire-system impairment should remain visible and be independently reviewed before restart.

## Practical design and human factors

A useful retrofit pattern is a self-contained, appropriately approved fire detection/discharge system with **separate** fire-discharge/impairment indication and a selected machine shutdown interface, while the independent machine safety chain remains intact. The interface should be accessible for qualified verification, labeled, protected from accidental defeat, and make a discharged/unavailable suppressor conspicuous. **Do not** silently grant personnel-safety integrity to an unqualified accessory contact.

Make recharging easier than bypassing: stock the correct approved replacement, arrange local qualified service, give maintenance a single-page impairment and restart workflow, and offer an authorized alternative machine/process during downtime. Prefer a physical keyed service procedure and change record over a hidden "temporarily ignore pressure switch" software setting. Design maintenance access to avoid leaning into a smoke/agent-filled enclosure. If protection is required for the risk reduction case, operating after impairment without a qualified alternative is not acceptable; if minimum attended-operation protection is absent, no personnel-exposed operation.
